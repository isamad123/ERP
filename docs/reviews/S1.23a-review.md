**Summary for the product owner.** The core of this part is sound. A policy is created as a draft, checked against its type's list of
parameters, and put into force from a date; the version before it ends the day before, and a version that was in force can never be
edited, by the service or directly in the database. Assignments of one type in one company are put into force together as a set from a
date, with clear checks (no two rules at the same rank, every unit, grade, branch and location exists in that company, a retired custom
field is refused). "Which policy applies?" gives the same answer every time, explains each rule that matched or did not, and never
falls back to a silent default. Probation now comes from the probation policy. Activations wait for a second person when the new
setting is on, and the person who asked cannot approve. No statutory value is anywhere in the code or the manifest. All tests pass,
including the tenant-isolation suite.

One high point must be fixed before the merge:
- **"Which policy applies?" shows custom-field values that the person's field rules hide.** I reproduced it: a field hidden from a role
  is hidden on the employee's record but shown in full on the resolution screen. Custom fields of an employment can hold personal
  data (the slice's own example is religion), so this breaks the field-level security rule of CLAUDE.md.

Two medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- the activation check lists the names and employee numbers of up to 50 employees to anyone who may manage policies, even when that
  person may not see those employees at all (reproduced);
- the rules read the employee's **current** employee type and custom values for every date, so "which policy applied on 1 March"
  can change when someone edits a custom field today. The slice asks for attributes "valid on a date"; this limit is not recorded.

The nine low points can wait or be ruled on.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: the resolution view bypasses the field rules of custom fields (FR-HRM-POL-03, FR-KRN-MET-02; CLAUDE.md "field-level security")
- `PolicyService.ExplainAsync` (`PolicyService.cs:542-593`) checks `hrm.policies.read` and the employment's data scope, then returns
  `attributes = { …, who.Custom, who.GradeDefaults }` (`:590`): the employment's whole custom-field object as stored, read by
  `PolicyAttributeReader.Of` (`PolicyResolver.cs:168-170`). Each evaluation also carries `conditions[].actual`, the employee's value of
  the custom field the rule names (`PolicyRules.cs:215`). Neither goes through `FieldShaping` / `FieldSecurity.ShapeCustom`, which the
  employment reads use (custom fields are ruled per key as `custom.<key>`, `FieldSecurity.cs:21, 71, 243`).
- Failure scenario (R1): a customer adds the custom field "Religion" to employments (the slice's own example of a pack field
  requirement) and hides it from line managers' and HR assistants' roles. A person in such a role with `hrm.policies.read` opens
  "Which policy applies?" for any employee in their scope and reads the religion of each, although the employee's record hides it.
  Masked fields are shown unmasked the same way.
- Suggested fix: shape the answer with the caller's field access for `hrm.employments` (`IFieldAccess.PlanAsync(EmploymentAccess.Fields,
  EmploymentAccess.Scope, "read", …)` and `FieldSecurity.ShapeCustom` on `attributes.custom`), and replace `actual` of a custom
  condition the caller may not read by a "hidden" marker (keep `met`, so the explanation still says whether the rule matched). The same
  for the assignment row's fields if a later slice classes them (grade, unit). Add a test with a hidden and a masked custom field.

### 2. MEDIUM: the activation check names employees the caller may not see (data scope, CLAUDE.md "data scopes"; Build item 5)
- `PolicyWriter.UnmatchedAsync` (`PolicyWriter.cs:214-231`) reads every current employment of the company
  (`PolicyAttributeReader.CompanyAsync`) and returns the first 50 that no rule covers with employment id, employee number and the
  person's first and last name (`:229-230`). It is called by the check (`PolicyService.cs:457`) and the activation (`:537`, returned to
  the maker in both the applied and the pending answer). The caller was checked for `hrm.policies.manage` and the company's scope on
  `hrm.policies` only; neither `hrm.employments.read` nor the employment data scope is applied to the list.
- Failure scenario (R2): a customer gives a "policy administrator" role `hrm.policies.read` and `hrm.policies.manage` only, or HR
  administrators of one unit a unit-scoped employment read. Pressing "Check" on a set of a required type (`notice_period`) that covers
  nobody lists 50 names and employee numbers of the whole company, including employees outside the caller's scope.
- Suggested fix: keep the count over the whole company (it is the warning), but list only the employments the caller may read
  (`IDataScopeFilter.ApplyAsync` with `EmploymentAccess.Scope`, "read"), and list none without `hrm.employments.read`; say in the answer
  how many are not shown. Add the case to the permission test.

### 3. MEDIUM: rules read the current employee type and custom values for every date (Build items 3 and 4: "attributes valid on a date"; not in CR-079)
- `PolicyAttributeReader.OneAsync` takes the assignment row valid on the date (unit, branch, location, grade: dated correctly), but
  `employee_type` and `custom` come from `hrm.employments` as they are now (`PolicyResolver.cs:168-170`). The employee type changes
  through a correction event (`EmploymentService.Changes.cs:441-449`, applied in `EmploymentApplier.cs:177-178`); custom values change by
  a plain edit of the employment, which keeps no history and raises no `hrm.employee_changed`.
- Failure scenario: leave policy "Shift workers" is assigned by the custom field "Shift worker = yes". An employee is a shift worker
  until 31 March and HR sets the field to "no" on 1 April. Asked in April "which policy applied on 15 March" (an appeal, an audit, or the
  leave and pay engines recalculating March after a backdated change, S1.32 and S1.34), the resolver now answers the general policy.
  Same with a contract worker converted to permanent by a correction: every earlier date resolves as "permanent". The resolution view
  shows the date, so it looks like a dated answer.
- Suggested fix: ruling needed. Either (a) record in CR-079 that employee type and custom fields are read as they are today, show that
  in the resolution view ("current value"), and add a risk for the engines (results keep the version they used; recalculation of a past
  period may pick another policy); or (b) take the employee type from the events valid on the date and keep custom values of the
  employment dated (a data-design change, D-39). I recommend (a) now and (b) with S1.21 or the first engine that recalculates.

### 4. LOW: a lost update answers 500 instead of 409 on every policy write
- `PolicyService.SaveAsync` (`PolicyService.cs:747-763`) catches `DbUpdateException` only when its inner exception is a
  `PostgresException`. `row_version` is a concurrency token of all three tables, so a stale save throws `DbUpdateConcurrencyException`
  (no inner exception) and escapes. The row version is compared before the lock is taken (`DraftAsync`, `ManagedPolicyAsync`), so the
  window is real for activation, rename, retirement and draft edits (R3).
- Failure scenario: two HR administrators work on the same draft; one saves parameters while the other activates. The second sees an
  error page instead of "changed meanwhile, reload". Data stays correct (the update is refused).
- Suggested fix: catch `DbUpdateConcurrencyException` in `SaveAsync` and answer `PersonService.Concurrency` (409), as
  `EmploymentService.cs:607` and `StatutoryRegistrationService.cs:404` do; better, re-read the draft and compare its row version after
  `select … for update`.

### 5. LOW: the approver sees ids and raw JSON, and not the coverage warning
- `PolicyAssignmentChangeHandler.DescribeAsync` (`PolicyChangeHandlers.cs:216-236`) lists each assignment as
  `precedence: CODE {"conditions":[{"attribute":"grade","operator":"in","values":["0193…"]}]}`; the version activation shows the raw
  parameter JSON (`:175`). The maker saw rules in sentences and the "N employees would have no policy" warning; the approver sees GUIDs
  and nothing about uncovered employees (the same pattern as finding 1 of the S1.20d review).
- Failure scenario: a checker approves a set that leaves 300 employees without a notice-period policy, without being told.
- Suggested fix: describe conditions with the names (`PolicyWriter.NamesAsync`) and add the unmatched count from a dry run
  (`SetCheckAsync` already computes it) as a detail line.

### 6. LOW: no test of the re-checks at approval or of the approver's own rights
- `PolicyTests.FR_XM_32_…` (`PolicyTests.cs:406-435`) covers holding, the requester refused and a second HR administrator approving.
  Not covered: a draft edited after the request is refused at approval (`PolicyChangeHandlers.cs:140-143`); a second pending set with
  the same `basedOn` is refused once the first is approved; an approver without `hrm.policies.approve`, or with it only for another
  company, can neither see nor approve the request (`VisibleAsync`, `:41-54`). These are the "held changes re-checked on approval"
  rules of CR-079 (k).
- Suggested fix: add these three cases to the FR-XM-32 test.

### 7. LOW: the precedence trigger is only a backstop while every writer takes the set lock
- `hrm.tg_policy_assignments_checks` (`V202611150350__hrm__functions_s1_23a.sql:34-61`) looks for an overlapping row with the same
  precedence in other transactions' committed data only. Two transactions inserting clashing rows at the same time both pass (read
  committed). The service is safe because `PolicyWriter.LockSetAsync` serializes activations per company and type, but CR-079 (c) says
  "the database refuses two overlapping assignments … with the same precedence", which holds only under that lock.
- Failure scenario: the S1.23b pack loader installs policy assignments without `LockSetAsync` while HR activates a set for the same
  type and company; both commit and two assignments share a precedence, so "first match" depends on row ids.
- Suggested fix: take the same advisory lock inside the trigger (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('hrm.policy_assignments:' ||
  company_id || ':' || kind, 0))` before the check), or state in CR-079 and the S1.23b brief that every writer must call
  `LockSetAsync`.

### 8. LOW: the coverage warning counts employees who join after the date
- `UnmatchedAsync` includes `pre_joining` employments (`PolicyWriter.cs:56, 224`). One whose first job row starts after the set's date
  has no assignment row on that date and is listed with reason `no_assignment_row`, although every rule may cover them from their
  joining date.
- Failure scenario: HR activates the notice-period set from 1 December; ten new joiners starting in January are reported as "matching
  no rule", so HR learns to ignore the warning.
- Suggested fix: evaluate a pre-joining employee on their joining date (or leave out employments whose first row starts after the
  date and say so).

### 9. LOW: `notice_period` differs from ruling Q3 without a note in CR-079
- Ruling Q3 describes `notice_period` as "days by employee type and length of service band". The manifest (`policy-types.json`) has
  service bands only; employee types are chosen by assignment rules (the type's description says so). This is a reasonable reading,
  but CR-079 detail (j) records only the probation part of Q3.
- Suggested fix: add one sentence to CR-079 S1.23a detail (j) (or a new letter): "notice periods vary by employee type through
  assignment rules, not inside the parameters".

### 10. LOW: the schema checker accepts null for optional parameters and does not check keyword values at load
- `PolicySchema.Walk` skips a declared property whose value is `null` (`PolicySchema.cs:124`), so `{"months": 3, "reviewer": null}` is
  stored for `probation`. `EnsureSupported` checks keyword names but not their values: a later manifest with `"minimum": "3"` or a
  non-array `enum` loads and then throws at the first check (`:138, 173, 99`), a 500.
- Suggested fix: refuse `null` unless the property's schema allows it (or drop such keys before saving), and check in
  `EnsureSupported` that `minimum`/`maximum` are numbers, `min*`/`max*` are non-negative integers and `enum`/`required` are arrays.

### 11. LOW: raw font weight in the policy screens (CLAUDE.md "Design tokens only")

- Suggested fix: `font-weight: var(--font-weight-strong)`.

## Checked and found in order
- Resolution order: assignments of the type and company valid on the date, precedence ascending (ties impossible), first match for
  `one`, every match for `many`; a matching policy without a version in force answers `no_version_in_force` and stops (ruling Q5,
  CR-079 f); a stored rule that no longer parses matches nobody. 1,000 identical answers are tested.
- Versions: one draft per policy (unique index), activation only after the latest start, the previous version ended at the new start,
  a version that was in force never edited or deleted (trigger tested directly), retirement refused while assigned on or after the
  date. Assignment sets: `basedOn` refuses a set activated meanwhile; a future set can be replaced only on its own start date.
- Permissions and scopes: every endpoint checks `hrm.policies.read` or `hrm.policies.manage` and the company scope; the resolution
  view also needs the employment's read scope (the scope engine denies without a grant of `hrm.employments.read`); approvals need
  `hrm.policies.approve` and the company in the approver's `approve` scope; the requester is refused by the kernel (FR-XM-32, tested).
  Changing `hrm.policies.requires_approval` is itself held.
- D-10: HR reads no kernel table; units, branches, locations, companies and custom-field definitions come through the new kernel
  contracts `IOrgStructure` and `ICustomFieldCatalog`. No foreign key or join across schemas.
- AS-4: no rate, slab or threshold; `probation` and `notice_period` carry no default values (only ranges of what may be entered).
- Probation: opened at hire, rehire and transfer from the `probation` policy on the joining date; the extension limit is counted from
  the original last day; reviewer `hr` leaves the manager out of the reminder (tested).
- Front end: design-system components, logical properties, texts in `policies.{en,ur}.json`, routes lazy-loaded, no browser storage.
