**Summary for the product owner.** The foundations of the leave engine are sound. Leave types are checked both in the program and in the
database (code, counting and unit are frozen once used; a type is retired, never deleted). The ledger is truly append-only: the database
refuses any change or deletion by any login, and a correction is accepted only as a new entry that exactly cancels one earlier entry. The
"no negative balance unless the policy allows it" check runs under a per-employee-and-type lock, so two people cannot both spend the same
days. The accrual job posts each employee, leave type and period only once, however often it runs or restarts. An employee sees only their
own balances, chosen from their sign-in, never from what the request says. No leave number is shipped anywhere (only test data), there is no
floating-point arithmetic, and every date comes from the injected clock. CI is green and every test passes here too.

One high point should be fixed before the merge:
- **The balance check ("recompute") can never be cleared.** When it finds a difference and HR posts the correcting adjustment, as the job
  description tells them to, the same difference is reported again every month. Reversing the entry does not clear it either. I reproduced
  both. Because the job log keeps only 500 lines, already-fixed differences soon push out new ones, so the monthly check that R-148 relies on
  for late changes stops being useful.

Three medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- retiring a leave type makes the check report every past automatic entry of that type as "unexpected" (reproduced);
- changing the leave-year start month (a company setting anyone with settings access may change at any time) silently changes past balances,

- a policy version that moves a leave type from a yearly grant to monthly accrual in the middle of a year gives the employee both, and the
  check calls that correct (reproduced: 18 days instead of 12 for the year).

The nine low points can wait or be ruled on.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: a difference the recompute found is never cleared by HR's correction (Build item 6, FR-HRM-LVE-14, R-148)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveEntitlements.cs` lines 18 to 31 (`PostedEntries.ReadAsync` reads only entries with source type
`hrm.leave_accrual`) and 106 to 149 (`LeaveRecompute.RunAsync` compares the plan with those entries only); `LeaveEntitlements.cs` lines 59 to 72
and the comment "A grant HR reversed is left to HR" (the job never posts a reversed period again, because its key is still in the ledger).

What goes wrong: Build item 6 says "nothing is changed automatically (HR posts an adjustment)", and the job's own description says the same.
But HR's adjustment is a `hrm.leave_manual` entry, which the recompute never counts, so the difference stays. The only other tool HR has, the
reversal, makes it worse (`posted` drops to 0 and the job will not post the period again). There is therefore no way to bring a corrected
employee back to "no difference". Every backdated policy change (R-148's main case) leaves its differences in every later run for the two-year
horizon; the job writes only the first 500 lines (`LeaveJobs.cs` line 157), in employment-id order, so after one backdated change affecting a few
hundred people the log is full of settled differences and a new one is not shown. The recompute is the only control R-148 names for late
changes, and balances feed encashment and unpaid-leave deductions (class B).

How checked: reproduced (R1 above).

Suggested fix (one of):
- let an adjustment name the period it settles (for example an optional `settles: {leaveTypeId, period}` on `POST …/leave-ledger`, stored with
  source type `hrm.leave_recompute` and the period's key, so the reason and creator stay the record), and count such entries in the group's net
  in `LeaveRecompute`; or
- compare per leave year (automatic plus adjustments against the plan's total for the year) instead of per period, listing the periods only as
  the explanation.
Either way, add a test that a settled difference disappears, and have the job log the newest or largest differences first, or all of them in a
structured result, rather than the first 500 by employment id.

### 2. MEDIUM: retiring a leave type turns its past automatic entries into "unexpected" differences (Build items 1 and 6)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/EntitlementPlanner.cs` line 121 (`types.Where(t => t.IsActive && !t.IsDeleted)`: a retired type has no plan at
all, not even for past dates) with `LeaveJobs.cs` lines 70 and 152 and `LeaveRecomputeService.cs` (the recompute is given retired types too).

What goes wrong: "a retired leave type gets nothing new" is applied to the whole horizon, so the plan of a retired type is empty and every
automatic entry it already has (up to 24 monthly accruals and two grants per employee) is reported as `unexpected` each month. With finding 1's
log limit, retiring one type for a large company fills the log.

How checked: reproduced (R2 above).

Suggested fix: plan a retired type up to the day it was retired (the change log or `updated_at` of the retirement gives the date), or exclude
retired types from the comparison while still showing their running balance under `years`.

### 3. MEDIUM: changing the leave-year start month rewrites past balances and the recompute cannot see it (ruling Q3; not in CR-086)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveReaders.cs` lines 64 to 83 (`LeaveYearSetting` reads today's value of `hrm.leave.year_start_month` for every
date), `LeaveLedgerService.cs` lines 311 to 336 (`ViewAsync` picks entries by the stored `leave_year` label and the span worked out with today's
setting), `EntitlementRules.cs` line 154 (`YearKey` is `Y2026`, without the start month) and `LeaveLedgerService.cs` line 202 (the reversal's
span when the labels no longer match).

What goes wrong: `leave_year` is stored on each entry with the start month in force when it was posted, but every reader works out spans with the
setting's (or the policy's) value of today. After a change from January to July, entries dated January to June of this year keep the label of
this year while the new rule puts them in last year's leave year, so a balance "as of" a past date changes, the negative-balance check
(`LeaveLedgerStore.LowestFromAsync`, by label) mixes two years, and S1.32b's year-end will carry forward or lapse the wrong entries. Because the
yearly key holds only the year number, the plan and the ledger still match label by label, so the recompute reports nothing. The same happens
when a policy version gives a type a different `yearStartMonth`. The setting has no guard and its help text does not warn.

How checked: reproduced for the company setting (R3 above); the policy case read.

Suggested fix: refuse a change of the setting (and of a type's `yearStartMonth` between policy versions) once a company has leave entries, unless
it takes effect from the next leave-year boundary with a defined short transition year; or store the span (start date) on each entry and key
yearly grants by the year's start date (`Y2026-07`). Record the ruling in CR-086.

### 4. MEDIUM: a mid-year change from a yearly grant to monthly accrual grants both (Build item 3, CR-086 h, R-148)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/EntitlementPlanner.cs` lines 132 to 145 (the yearly grant is the whole year's quantity under the policy in force on
the year's first day) and 147 to 175 (monthly accruals under the policy in force at each month end).

What goes wrong: when a policy version moves a type from `fixed_yearly` (or yearly tiers) to a monthly model from 1 July, the employee keeps the
full yearly grant and also receives the July to December accruals: 18 days for a 12-day year in the test. The opposite move (monthly to yearly
mid-year) gives nothing for the rest of that year, which R-148 mentions only as "a policy that starts in the middle of a leave year". Because
the plan itself contains both postings, the recompute calls the over-grant correct, so HR is never told.

How checked: reproduced (R4 above); the opposite direction read.

Suggested fix: pro-rate a yearly grant by the part of the leave year during which the yearly model is in force (look ahead to the version change
known today, as the leaver correction already does for the end of employment), or let the recompute flag a model change inside a leave year
for HR to settle; record the rule in CR-086 h and R-148.

### 5. LOW: the leaver correction is a pay input posted without the attendance lock and up to a month late (FR-XM-26, R-146)

`LeaveEntitlements.cs` lines 59 to 72: the `Y…/leaver` correction is negative, dated on the last day, and posted by the monthly job after that
day has passed. R-146 treats the job's postings as "entitlement, not attendance or pay input", but the leaver's balance on the last day is what
final settlement encashes or recovers. Suggested fix: name the leaver correction in R-146, and have final settlement (S1.4x) run
`hrm.leave_accrual` for the employment (the job already takes `employment_id`) before it reads the balance; S1.31 decides how the lock treats it.

### 6. LOW: what an opening balance covers on its own date is not stated (CR-086 i)

`EntitlementPlanner.cs` lines 179 to 186: postings due **before** the opening's date are dropped, postings due on that date are kept. An opening
dated 1 January plus a yearly grant due 1 January therefore gives both; an opening dated on a month end plus that month's accrual gives both.
The existing test (`…an_opening_balance_is_a_cut_in…`) only covers a monthly type with an opening on 1 January. Suggested fix: say in the contract
row and the screen text that an opening is the balance before that day's own entitlements, and add a yearly-type test.

### 7. LOW: go-live order: a job run before the openings posts two years that HR must reverse one by one (CR-086 i)

If a `leave` policy is activated from a date in the past (as the tests do) and the monthly job runs before HR loads the opening balances, the job
posts the previous and current year's grants and every month's accrual since the horizon; once the opening is posted those become
`unexpected` and HR must reverse each entry separately (up to 24 per employee and type). Suggested fix: a runbook line (load openings before the
first run, or activate the policy from the go-live date), or a bulk "reverse the unexpected automatic entries" action in S1.33.

### 8. LOW: `isPaid` and `isUnpaidDeduction` may change after a type has entries (Build item 1, CR-086 e)

### 9. LOW: the "fixed once used" rule can be missed by a concurrent posting (Build item 1)

### 10. LOW: the attendance-lock check of a manual entry runs outside its transaction (FR-XM-26, ruling Q8)

### 11. LOW: a reversal may be dated after the employee's last day or on a cancelled employment

## Checked and found in order

- **Append-only ledger and reversal.** Update, delete and truncate are refused by the first migration's triggers for every login (test
  `…no_login_can_update_or_delete…`). `tg_leave_ledger_reversal` refuses a zero entry, a `usage_reversal` without a target, a reversal of a reversal,
  and anything but the exact negative of one entry of the same employment, type, leave year and kind (operator precedence of the kind rule is
  correct); the unique index on `reverses_entry_id` stops a second reversal, and `WriteAsync` re-checks under the lock and maps the unique
  violation to `already_reversed`. The composite foreign key keeps a reversal in the same company.
- **Negative balance under the lock (ruling Q8).** `WriteAsync` takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on employment and type, runs the caller's check,
  then `LowestFromAsync` (lowest running balance from the entry's date to the end of the leave year, future-dated entries included) before
  inserting in the same transaction. Reversals of positive entries go through the same check. The job takes the same lock.
- **Run-once and resumability.** Automatic postings carry a source id derived (SHA-256) from employment, type and key; the insert uses
  `on conflict … do nothing` on the unique index; the leaver correction has its own key. The walk is in employment-id order with the checkpoint
  saved in the same transaction as each chunk, so a resumed run continues after the last finished employment and posts nothing twice.
- **Arithmetic.** Decimal only; pro-rata by days, months started or completed; rounding to a step (up, down, nearest away from zero) then four
  places; service months with month-end clamping; tiers pick the highest band not above the service, grades by code case-insensitively; worked
  time only for months whose first and last days are locked. Due dates: yearly on the leave-year start or joining, monthly on the month end or the
  last day; nothing for pre-joining or cancelled employments; leave years keep per-type start months apart.
- **Permissions and scopes.** Every new endpoint checks its permission first and the data scope on the employment or entry (`post`, `read`,
  `recompute`, `create`, `update`); out-of-scope records answer 404. `/api/hrm/my/leave-balances` takes the employments whose `user_ref` is the
  token's user and lets the request only choose among them by company (test covers a foreign company and an unlinked user). The baseline
  `employee` role holds `hrm.leave_ledger.read` with scope `own`; the post permission is sensitive and only `hr_admin` holds it.
- **Tenant isolation.** `HrmProbe` targets all seven new routes (another customer's ids answer 404, and the probe fails on any route it cannot
  target); the suite passed locally (881 of 881) and in the CI job `tenant-isolation` at `cc76796`.
- **AS-4, R-002, R-145.** `policy-types.json` has minimums and maximums but no default quantity, cap, limit or month; `LeavePolicyCheck` refuses
  a missing value a model needs. No pack or seed adds a leave type or number. The only defaults are modes (`sandwich` off, `rounding` none).
- **Clock, floating point, texts.** No `DateTime.Now`/`UtcNow`, `double` or `float` in the new code; ids use the clock's time. Errors and
  explanations are keys with English and Urdu texts (Urdu to be reviewed, R-062).
- **Deviations in CR-086 S1.32a detail.** a (one policy with rules per type), c (closed writes; requests and year-end left open for S1.32b),
  d (no product source), f (`numeric(19,6)` kept, four places enforced; no kind "reversal"), g, k, l, m, n, o are sound and consistent with the
  code. h and i are sound as written but miss the cases in findings 3, 4, 6 and 7.
- **Migration.** One new file `V202611150359__hrm__functions_s1_32a.sql`, generated from `rules.yaml` (no drift); it only creates functions and
  triggers on tables that exist since `V202611150100`, so either apply order works. No merged migration is changed.
