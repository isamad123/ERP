**Summary for the product owner.** Most of this part is solid. The account number, the IBAN and the registration number are stored
encrypted. Lists always show them masked (`****` and the last four), even to HR administrators. The full number appears only through a
separate "reveal" action, which needs its own permission and is written to the access log first. The IBAN check (country, length,
check digits) works. "One remainder account and at most 100 percent" is checked on every date, both by the service and by the
database. A new or changed account waits for a second person, and the person who asked cannot approve it. Nor can the employee whose
account it is. The old generated write endpoints are closed. No contribution rate, ceiling or other statutory rule is stored or coded.
The tenant-isolation suite passes.

One high point must be fixed before the merge:
- **the "ghost employee" check can be passed without anyone seeing it.** This check warns when one bank account is given to two
  different people. I reproduced two ways round it. (1) Two account requests waiting at the same time, each giving the same account to
  a different employee, raise no warning at all. A second person approves both. (2) When the warning does appear, the person entering
  the account can answer it themselves ("different people"). The approver is then shown nothing about the warning. This is the fraud
  pattern the check exists for: an HR clerk paying a fake employee into an account they control.

Three medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- **the branch fails its own tests**, so CI will be red: one generator test still expects the old number of open rules, and the
  generated data dictionary was not rebuilt;
- a request can name a bank that does not exist. It is accepted for approval, the approvals inbox says "can approve", and approving it
  then fails. The change request says the inbox runs a full trial approval; the code does not;
- the tests miss parts of the acceptance list: no data-scope test other than "company", and no test that an approver outside the
  employee's records can neither see nor approve a request.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: the ghost-employee check can be passed by two waiting requests, and the approver never sees its warning (FR-HRM-EMP-06, FR-HRM-EMP-18, FR-XM-32)
- `DuplicatesAsync` (`EmployeeBankAccountService.cs:689`) compares a new number or IBAN only with rows already stored in
  `hrm.employee_bank_accounts`. When the change is held (`:320-330`), the account is not stored until approval. The approval
  (`BankAccountChangeHandler.RunAsync`, `:59-108`) checks the employment, the row versions and the split, but never checks duplicates
  again. So two requests waiting at the same time never see each other.
- When the warning does appear, the maker answers it alone. `duplicates: "distinct"` needs only `hrm.person_duplicate_cases.update`,
  which the baseline HR Admin role holds beside the account create and update permissions. The case is then recorded as decided by the
  maker (`:773`). `duplicates: "review"` lets the change go on with the case left open. In both cases the held payload (`BankPlan`)
  carries no trace of the warning. `DescribeAsync` (`BankAccountChangeHandler.cs:110-164`) shows the approver the title, the last four
  characters and the dates, and nothing about the other person.
- Failure scenario (reproduced, R1 and R1b): an HR officer creates a fictitious employee and adds, as its salary account, an account
  they control, which is also on file for a real employee (or which they add to a second fictitious employee in a parallel request). In
  the first case they answer the warning "distinct" themselves. In the second, no warning is raised. The second HR administrator sees
  an ordinary new account ending in four digits and approves. Payroll later pays two salaries into one account. Maker-checker was meant
  to be the control over exactly this step, but the checker decides without the one fact that matters.
- **Fix:** (a) run the duplicate search again in `RunAsync` on the approval's transaction. It must also look at other *pending*
  bank-account requests: store the blind indexes in the payload (they already are) and compare them with the payloads of pending
  requests of the same kind, or take a transaction advisory lock on each blind index at submit and approval. (b) Record the warning in
  the payload (the matched persons as ids, whether each is in the approver's scope, the maker's answer and the case ids). Show it in
  `DescribeAsync` as a detail line, for example "Same account as another person: review case open", with the person named only when the
  approver may read them. (c) While approval is on, do not let the maker's "distinct" decide the case. Store it as `open`, and decide it
  `distinct` only when the approver approves, or require the case to be decided by someone other than the maker before the approval
  can pass. (d) Add tests for both reproduced paths, citing FR-HRM-EMP-18 and FR-XM-32.

### 3. MEDIUM: a held request is not tried against the database rules, but the inbox says it can be approved (CR-076 S1.20d detail d)
- Detail d says: "the inbox's dry run is the full apply on the rolled-back transaction". In the code, `CheckAsync` calls
  `RunAsync(apply: false)` (`BankAccountChangeHandler.cs:51`), which returns before `BankAccountPlanner.ApplyAsync` (`:100-103`). So
  the foreign key to `ref.banks`, the bank-fields trigger and the priority exclusion are never tried until the real approval. At
  submission (`EmployeeBankAccountService.cs:320-330`), with approval on, `ApplyAsync` is not tried either, and the service has no way
  to check a bank id (R-232: no reference-data contract).
- Failure scenario (reproduced, R3): HR submits an account with a mistyped or deleted bank id. The request is accepted (202) and shows
  in the inbox as approvable. The approver approves and gets "unknown bank". The request stays pending until someone rejects it, and
  the employee has no new account. Also, the approved change-request text describes a check that does not exist.
- **Fix:** make the dry run what detail d says. In `CheckAsync`, run `ApplyAsync` (the kernel already rolls the listing back to a
  savepoint, `AccessAdministrationService.Held.cs:103-121`). At submission, while approval is on, run the same apply inside a savepoint
  of the submit transaction and roll it back before `SubmitAsync`, so a bad bank, a priority clash or a bank-fields error is refused
  at once. Add a test.

### 4. MEDIUM: the access tests miss data scopes other than "company" and the approver's scope (Acceptance: "every endpoint … data-scope tests (own, team, subtree, company)")
- `PayDetailsTests.cs` tests a role limited to another company (`:406-440`, `:491-528`). Nothing tests own, team or subtree scope on the
  eight new endpoints. Nothing tests `BankAccountChangeHandler.VisibleAsync`, which is the only thing stopping an approver outside
  the employee's records from seeing and approving a bank change. The held-change listing, approve and reject all depend on it. There
  is also no test of `remove` without `hrm.employee_bank_accounts.delete`, or of a registration update or delete with the row out of
  scope for update while readable.
- Failure scenario: a later edit drops the `update` half of `VisibleAsync`, or probes with the wrong company. An HR officer limited to
  one branch can then approve bank changes for the whole company, and every test still passes.
- **Fix:** add, citing FR-HRM-EMP-06 and FR-KRN-IAM-12: an approver holding `hrm.employee_bank_accounts.approve` but scoped to another
  company or branch does not see the request in `/api/iam/maker-checker-requests/pending`, and approve and reject answer 404; an approver
  with read scope but not update scope gets the same answer. Add one team or subtree (manager) scope case for the list, the change and
  the reveal, and a `remove` without the delete permission.

### 5. LOW: two accounts cannot swap priorities in one request
- `BankAccountPlanner.ApplyAsync` saves all changed rows in one `SaveChanges` (`:208-213`). The exclusion constraint
  `ex_employee_bank_accounts_employment_id_priority` (`V202611150100__hrm__hrm.sql:704`) is not deferrable, so the first update clashes
  with the other row's old priority. The service's own simulation (`PaySplit.Check`) accepts the result; only the database refuses it.
- Failure scenario (reproduced, R2): HR wants the salary account paid before the savings account. The swap is refused with
  "priority taken". With approval on, it takes three approved requests (move one to a free priority, move the other, move the first
  back).
- **Fix:** a new migration that re-creates the exclusion constraint `deferrable initially immediate`, then `set constraints … deferred`
  around the writes in `ApplyAsync`, as already done for the split trigger. Or write changed rows whose priority changes in two steps
  inside the transaction (end-and-reinsert). Add the swap to the split test.

### 6. LOW: `validTo` and `validFrom` on a `change` are silently ignored
- The `change` branch (`EmployeeBankAccountService.cs:633-662`) builds the op without `ValidTo` and never looks at `input.ValidFrom`.
  The contracts register lists both fields for the change list. The registration service refuses `validFrom` on an update
  (`errors.hrm.registration.effective_from`); the account service accepts the request and drops the fields.
- Failure scenario (reproduced, R4): HR sends a change of the title with an end date 30 days ahead. It answers 200 (or 202, then
  approved), and the account stays open-ended. HR believes the account will stop being used and payroll keeps paying into it.
- **Fix:** refuse `validFrom` and `validTo` on `change` with `errors.hrm.bank_account.effective_from` (use `end` for an end date), or
  apply `validTo` to the row (or the new row) and check the dates. Add a test.

### 7. LOW: a rejected request leaves its duplicate case behind
- The cases of an answered warning are written before the request is submitted (`EmployeeBankAccountService.cs:320-326`). If the
  approver rejects the request, the account never exists, but an `open` (or `distinct`) case between the two persons remains in the
  review queue, keyed to a match that is not there.
- **Fix:** with approval on, create the cases in `BankAccountChangeHandler.ApplyAsync` (from the payload, see finding 1), not at
  submission.

### 8. LOW: the "other person" refusals are checked in code only and can race
- The registration duplicate (`StatutoryRegistrationService.cs:416-427`) and the bank-account warning lock only the employment being
  written (`:412`). Two requests for two different employees with the same scheme and number (or account) run under different locks.
  Both see no match and both commit. There is no database rule behind the registration refusal, which the service treats as a hard
  rule (409).
- **Fix:** take `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on a hash of the blind index (for registrations, scheme and blind index) before the search, in
  both services and in the approval. Record the residual risk if you prefer to accept it.

### 9. LOW: the approvals inbox shows codes, not words
- `DescribeAsync` builds the account line as text: `"{priority} · {paymentMode} · {kind} {value}"` (`BankAccountChangeHandler.cs:137-139`),
  so an Urdu approver reads `bank_transfer` and `percent 40`. The account number line shows the last four digits without the `****`
  that the IBAN line has (`:147`), so it reads like a full, very short number.
- **Fix:** send the payment mode and split kind as `key` details (existing option labels), the value as its own text detail, and
  prefix the account number with `****` as for the IBAN. CLAUDE.md: no hard-coded user-facing strings.

### 10. LOW: smaller robustness points
- `RevealAsync` (`EmployeeBankAccountService.cs:195-196`) does not catch `CryptographicException`, which the list handles
  (`Masked`). A row written under another key answers 500 instead of a refusal. The registration reveal is the same.
- The split trigger (`V202611153347__hrm__functions_s1_20d.sql`) checks only `new.employment_id` on an update. If a row's
  `employment_id` were ever changed, the old employment would not be checked again. No service path does this today. Checking both
  `old` and `new` on update costs one line.

### 11. LOW: the approver confirms an account from its last four characters only
- The approver of a held change cannot see the new number or IBAN in full. There is no reveal of a pending value. This is the same as
  the company bank accounts (S1.14a), so it is consistent, but a checker cannot compare the account against the employee's bank letter.
- **Fix (optional, needs your ruling):** a reveal of a pending request's values for holders of `.approve` and `.reveal`, access-logged
  like the account reveal. Otherwise record it as a known limit in R-233.

## What was checked and found sound
- **Maker-checker.** The kernel refuses the requester (`AccessAdministrationService.Held.cs:55`). It requires the handler's
  `ApprovePermission` (`hrm.employee_bank_accounts.approve`, sensitive) and asks `VisibleAsync` before approve and reject. The handler
  refuses the employee linked to the employment (`BankAccountChangeHandler.cs:83-86`, tested). A request is void when any account it
  touches changed (row versions; tested). The setting `hrm.bank_accounts.requires_approval` defaults on, and switching it off itself
  waits for a second person (tested in the fixture). The payload carries ciphertext and blind indexes only; the plain number is in
  neither the payload nor the inbox (tested).
- **Split rule.** `PaySplit.Check` and the deferred constraint trigger agree: one remainder and at most 100 percent on every date where
  any account is valid. Dates are taken at every `valid_from` and `valid_to`, which covers every interval. `set constraints … immediate`
  then `deferred` turns a commit failure into an answer. The service locks the employment row first, so its own writes are serialized.
  Amounts are `numeric(19,4)` with at most four decimals checked.
- **History.** A change from a later day ends the row and opens a new one; from the first day it corrects the row. The exclusion
  constraints of both tables hold. A start before the joining date is refused. Removal is refused for an account a payment line uses
  (the foreign key; R-230 covers backdated changes once payments exist).
- **Masking, reveal, field security.** Lists mask the three numbers for every role, and hide or mask other fields by field rules. The
  reveal needs the reveal and read permissions, the row in read scope and the field at least read-only. It writes the access log before
  the answer (all tested). The generated read endpoints never return the encrypted columns. The generated writes are closed (tested).
- **Tenant isolation.** The eight new routes are in `HrmProbe`; the suite passes. The tenant is resolved by the pipeline only.
- **Statutory content (AS-4).** No rate, ceiling, threshold or contribution rule is stored or coded (tested on the table's columns).
  Scheme codes are typed (R-234). The IBAN lengths are the ISO 13616 technical format, not statutory content; R-232 moves the full
  registry to the pack.
- **Migrations.** Only new, generator-written files, ordered after the last merged HR migration; frozen files untouched.
- **Tests and FR ids.** Every new test names its FR ids (FR-HRM-EMP-06, -18, FR-HRM-PAY-24, FR-HRM-CPL-02, GHR-02, FR-XM-32,
  FR-KRN-IAM-12, -13). The acceptance bullet is covered: a new account waits for a second person; an invalid IBAN is refused; the same
  account number on two employees warns (approval off only, see finding 1); numbers are masked for roles without field access, and full
  views are in the access log.
- **Scope discipline.** Nothing beyond Build items 14 and 15. Screens are left to S1.21, contributions to S1.37.
