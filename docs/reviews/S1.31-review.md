**Summary for the product owner.** The lock itself is solid. When payroll calculates (or HR asks after the cut-off date and someone else approves), the
period's attendance days, counted leave days, payable overtime, lateness penalties, approved regularizations and leave usage freeze together, and the
database refuses changes to them even if a program tried. Monthly accruals and other entitlement postings still go in (R-146). A lock always records when,
how, who and (for payroll) which run, and can never be edited. A period reopens only when the run that locked it is cancelled before that run is
final; an HR lock never reopens. Punches that arrive after the lock are kept and listed. Nobody approves their own lock, reopen or adjustment request,
and the employee never approves an adjustment about themselves. The nine new endpoints check permissions and company scope and are in the
tenant-isolation suite; every new text exists in English and Urdu, and every new test names its FR id.

One high point should be fixed before the merge:
- **The same locked day can be corrected twice, and payroll would pay (or recover) it twice.** Each adjustment compares against the day as it was
  locked, not against an earlier approved correction, and the "already waiting" check only looks at waiting requests of the same kind. I reproduced it:
  a late day corrected to present, approved, then the same correction asked and approved again; payroll's reader returns both for the next period,
  each "late to present". An attendance correction and an overtime correction of the same day are also both accepted at once, and both carry the
  day's overtime minutes.

One medium point needs a fix or your ruling:
- **Adjustments of a payroll-locked period survive its reopening.** If payroll's run is cancelled and the period reopens, adjustments already approved
  still wait to be paid in the next period, and one still waiting can be approved afterwards, although the days are open again and will be corrected
  and paid directly in the recalculated run (reproduced).

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: one locked day can be adjusted more than once, and each adjustment is paid against the original locked result (Build item 5, FR-XM-26, FR-XM-06)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/Locking/AttendanceAdjustments.cs` lines 246 to 252 (the "already waiting" check) and lines 97 to 119
(`AdjustmentPlanner.PlanAsync`, the old summary). The only guard against a second adjustment of the same dates is "a *submitted* adjustment of the *same kind*
overlaps". An approved (or applied) adjustment of the date does not count, and neither does a waiting one of another kind. The old summary of every
adjustment is read from the locked records (the current day result, the counted leave days, the payable overtime), which never change after the lock, so
a second adjustment shows the same "old" as the first, and `IAttendanceAdjustments` (lines 594 to 612) hands payroll both, each with its own old and new.

Failure scenario (reproduced, RA): Monday was locked as `late` with 30 late minutes. HR asks for "late to present, 0 late minutes"; the HR administrator
approves. A week later another HR user (or the same one, not knowing it was done) asks for the same correction; nothing refuses it, the preview shows
"late to present" again, and it is approved. S1.35/S1.39 read two adjustments for the next period, each worth the difference between `late` and `present`,
and pay back the lateness deduction twice. The same happens with an absent day corrected to present (paid twice), with unpaid leave days (given back twice
in pay), and across kinds: kind `attendance` also carries `overtimeMinutes` (`AttendanceLockRules.Corrected`), so an attendance correction and an overtime
correction of the same day both change its overtime and are both accepted, even while the first still waits.

Suggested fix: treat the latest approved or applied adjustment of a date as the day's current locked result. Either (a) refuse an adjustment (and the
impact's `canSubmit`) when any adjustment of any kind that is `submitted`, `approved` or `applied` covers one of its dates (simplest; a further change then
waits until the first is decided and starts from it), or (b) take the old summary of a date from the newest approved or applied adjustment covering it
(its new summary), so the second adjustment carries only the further difference; check again at approval in `AttendanceAdjustmentHandler` under the
employment's advisory lock (`hrm.attendance_request:`), since two requests can be decided in either order. Separate attendance and overtime corrections
cleanly (an `attendance` correction should not restate `overtimeMinutes`, or the two kinds must not overlap). Test it with the RA scenario.

### 2. MEDIUM: adjustments of a payroll-locked period outlive the period's reopening (Build items 4 and 5, CR-095, FR-XM-26)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/Locking/AttendancePeriodLocks.cs` lines 191 to 261 (`ReopenOnAsync`) and `ReopenRefusalAsync`, and
`AttendanceAdjustments.cs` lines 507 to 516 (`AttendanceAdjustmentHandler` approval). An adjustment exists because its days are locked; the database checks
that only when it is inserted (`hrm.tg_attendance_adjustments_rules`). Reopening neither refuses while adjustments of the period are submitted or approved
nor ends them, and the approval handler does not check that the adjustment's period is still locked.

Failure scenario (reproduced, RB): payroll calculates on the 26th and locks the period; HR finds Monday's missing punches and, as it must, asks for an
adjustment, which is approved into the next period; a second one for Tuesday waits. Payroll then decides to cancel its run and recalculate (the reason
reopens exist), the reopen is approved and the period opens. HR now corrects Monday and Tuesday directly (regularization, re-processing), the new run pays
the corrected days, and the next period also pays both adjustments: twice again. The waiting Tuesday adjustment can even be approved after the reopen.
A `leave_cancellation` adjustment is worse after a reopen: its request is cancelled and its usage reversed in the next period, while its days in the
reopened period still show the old approved status (R-166), so the recalculated run and the adjustment disagree about the same days.

Suggested fix: on an approved reopen, in the same transaction, end the period's adjustments that are not applied: withdraw the waiting ones (the
workflow instance withdrawn, status `withdrawn` with a history entry "period reopened") and either refuse the reopen while approved, not applied
adjustments exist (`errors.hrm.attendance.lock.adjustments_open`, shown on the console) or mark them withdrawn too (the database rule would then need
`approved` to `withdrawn` for this case, an expand-only change of the unmerged S1.31 function). For a `leave_cancellation` already approved, refusing the
reopen is the safe choice. In `AttendanceAdjustmentHandler`, refuse approval when `row.PeriodId` is no longer locked (`errors.hrm.attendance.adjustment.not_locked`).
Test it with the RB scenario.

### 3. LOW: a reopen re-works only the people with late punches; days skipped while locked stay unprocessed for everyone else (Build item 4, ruling Q5)

`AttendancePeriodLocks.cs` lines 250 to 254. On reopening, only employments that have punches received after the lock are re-queued. The engine skips
every locked date (`AttendanceDayProcessor` line 110) and the nightly run covers only the last two days, so when payroll locks before the period ends
(the lock accepts the remaining days as not processed, CR-091 detail d, R-165) the days between the lock and the end are never processed for people who
did not punch: absent people, people on leave, rest days.

Failure scenario: a monthly period is locked by payroll's calculation on the 26th, accepting the 26th to the 31st as not processed. The run is cancelled
on the 3rd and the period reopens. Employees who punched after the 26th are re-queued and worked out; an employee absent from the 27th to the 31st has no
punch, so no re-queue, and those five days stay without a result. The next lock shows them as "not processed" again; if it is accepted again (as before),
payroll never sees the absence. HR can process them by hand (`/attendance-days/process`), but nothing tells them which people need it.

Suggested fix: on reopen re-queue every employment of the period (`AttendancePeriodQueries.EmploymentsAsync`, already computed for the event) for the
period's dates up to the company's today, not only those with late punches.

### 4. LOW: a pay period without an attendance period row is not protected against a write racing the lock (Build item 2, CR-091 detail b)

`tools/Erp.CodeGen/rules.yaml` lines 487 to 504 (`hrm.locked_attendance_period`, used by the leave-day, overtime, regularization and leave-ledger guards)
and `AttendancePeriodQueries.TakeAsync`. The guards serialise with a lock by reading the attendance period row `for share`. When no row exists yet (no day
of the period was processed: every period of a company that does not use the attendance engine, R-168), the guard takes no lock at all, while the lock
creates the row under an advisory lock the writers never take.

Failure scenario: in a leave-only company, a manager approves an unpaid leave request for the 20th at the moment payroll's calculation locks the period.
The approval's transaction updates the leave days (guard: no period row, no lock, not locked) and has not committed; the lock creates the period row,
locks it and commits; payroll reads the leave in its own transaction and does not see the uncommitted approval; the approval then commits. The 20th is
now approved unpaid leave inside a locked period that payroll did not deduct, and no adjustment exists for it.

Suggested fix: let the guard fall back to the pay period row (`hrm.pay_periods` of the employment's pay group covering the date, `for share`), and let
`TakeAsync` take `for update` on the pay period row before it creates or locks the attendance period, so the two always meet on a row that exists.

## Checked and found right

- Database rules (rules.yaml S1.31, migrations `V202611151229`, `V202611151328`, `V202611151358`): a period's company, pay group, pay period and dates never
  change; a lock needs its time, kind, who and (exactly for payroll) its run; a locked row changes only in the reopen request fields; it opens only with a
  cancelled locking run, time and reason, never for an HR lock; a locked period is never deleted. Day results and penalties of a locked period are never
  inserted, changed or deleted; counted leave days (approved or waiting for cancellation) never change or become counted, while a waiting request may still
  end and a rejected cancellation may return to approved; overtime of a locked date keeps its minutes, approval and rate and only moves approved to paid or a
  waiting claim to rejected or back; the comp-off entry may still be recorded; no regularization becomes approved for a locked date; in the leave ledger only
  usage, usage reversal and encashment are refused in a locked period, so accruals and the leaver's correction still post (R-146; tested by the accrual job).
  Punches are never refused. Every guard reads the period `for share` and the lock takes it `for update` before counting, so a lock waits for writers
  already inside and later writers see it (finding 4 is the exception). An adjustment keeps its facts, is never deleted, starts `submitted` with one history
  entry for dates of one locked period, targets another unlocked pay period, and moves only forward with exactly one history entry per move.
- Frozen migrations: no merged migration file changed; the generator's `PlacedAsPart` change only places S1.31's new files and keeps the pending wording of
  the placeholders in the design file that sorts before them (`GeneratorTests` updated). Files that sort before already-applied ones are applied by
  `RecordedSchemaMigrator` (recorded by name, not by highest version), so existing customers get them.
- Lock (`AttendancePeriodLocks`): refused for an unknown pay period or a run of another period or cancelled; days not processed (every employment of the
  pay group between joining and leaving) and open exceptions refuse the lock unless accepted, and the accepted counts are recorded; a second lock answers
  AlreadyLocked; `hrm.attendance.locked` carries kind, run, who, accepted counts and the employments; without a caller transaction only a lock or reopen commits.
- HR lock (ruling Q1): permission `hrm.attendance_periods.lock`, company scope with action `lock`, only after the cut-off date in the company's time, a reason;
  the approval locks as the requester's HR lock with the request's acceptance (refused at decision if new open items appear that were not accepted); a payroll
  lock meanwhile ends it quietly; rejection or withdrawal clears the request; a returned one is sent again.
- Reopen (CR-095): refused for an HR lock (`use_adjustment`), when the run or any run of the period is final (`run_final`), while the locking run is still open
  (`run_open`, the contract) and for one's own approval (FR-XM-32, at the contract and in the workflow); the approved request cancels the run (stage log entry)
  and reopens in one transaction, publishing `hrm.attendance.reopened` with run, reason, requester, approver and employments; the decided requests are cleared
  so a later lock or reopen is a new request. The database's "never cancelled once locked" for `hrm.pay_runs` is the placeholder `pay_runs_lock` of S1.38;
  until then only the application refuses it (R-165).
- Adjustments: impact before submit (GHR-04: each day locked and corrected, leave given back with balance before and after and its date, overtime, penalties,
  locked and target pay periods, modules); the web form enables "send" only after a preview and clears the preview whenever an input changes. Target = the
  next pay period of the group that is not closed and not locked, set again at approval; leave cancellation reverses the usage once, dated on the target's
  first day, same leave year, refused for a closed year; the locked days are untouched (tested by comparing every day row before and after). The workflow
  excludes the requester and lists the employee as a subject. R-166 (readers of a locked period by request status) is recorded and owned by S1.35.
- Late punches (ruling Q5): appended, never worked into a locked day, listed with the total (at most 200) and only to callers who may read punches, in scope.
- Readiness `attendance_locked`: passed once locked; failed with the group's employees (in the caller's scope) before.
- Endpoints: all nine carry a permission and `HrmEndpointMetadata`, answer 404 for another company's period or employment and empty lists for its periods, are
  in `HrmProbe`; the generated writes of both tables are closed (tested). Baseline roles: HR administrators ask, payroll managers decide reopens (with their own
  workflow tasks only).
- Web (`/time/periods`, `/time/adjustments`): design-system components and tokens only, logical properties, texts in lazy catalogues (`periods.{en,ur}.json`,
  menu texts in the core files), dates and numbers through `@erp/i18n`, loading, empty, error and denied states, no request buttons for readers or in read-only
  mode. `5e378e8` makes the S1.30b tables' scroll regions keyboard-reachable with a visible focus; `a5ba143` reads the delivery summary of the test's own day.

  refusals by constraint name. Scope: only the slice's items and registers; no ledger table, no Finance call, no new package.
