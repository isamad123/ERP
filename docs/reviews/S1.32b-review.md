**Summary for the product owner.** Most of part b is careful work. The employee's own employment always comes from the sign-in; HR applying for
someone is recorded as the one who applied and is held to the data scope; the kernel keeps the requester and the employee off their own approval;
every write of one employee's leave of one type runs under a lock and the approval checks the balance again under it; the database refuses a
second whole day of leave on the same date; ledger entries are only added and cancellations are reversals; numbers come from the numbering
service and are never reused; there is no statutory leave value anywhere, every date comes from the injected clock and all SQL is parameterized.
The eleven new endpoints are in the tenant-isolation probe, and every new test cites its FR id. Files on a leave request (CR-114) are open only to
the employee, the person who applied, readers of leave requests within their scope and the people with an open approval task.

Two high points should be fixed before the merge:
- **Cancelled leave can come back as leave that never expires.** If an employee books leave that is paid from expiring leave (carried-forward
  leave, compensatory off, a grant with an end date) and then cancels or changes it, the days return as ordinary leave with no end date. I
  reproduced it: of two employees with the same 5 expiring days, the one who booked and cancelled 3 days still had 3 days after the expiry date
  and could book them; the other had 0. Booking and cancelling can be repeated, and what is kept is later carried forward or paid out.
- **When HR applies for an employee who has no sign-in, the "line manager" step goes to HR's own manager**, not the employee's. I reproduced it.
  In factories many workers have no sign-in and HR enters their leave, so this would route most such requests to the wrong person (who also sees
  any attached medical certificate). The same pattern exists in the attendance requests (S1.30a) and employment changes (S1.20), so the fix is
  best made once for all three.

Five medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- a request or a cancellation the approver sends back ("return") can never be withdrawn, and a returned cancellation blocks cancelling that leave
  for good (both reproduced);
- the year-end confirmation re-checks only the lines of its preview: a balance added after the preview (or an employee with none at preview time)
  is closed into the year without carry-forward or lapse, and leave still waiting for approval in that year can no longer be approved (reproduced);
- a transfer between companies in January moves only the new year's balance; last year's carry-forward then lands on the employment that has
  already ended and never reaches the new company (reproduced);
- compensatory off earned late in the year lapses at the year-end even though it should still be usable for its expiry days, and comp-off whose
  work date is in an already-closed year is posted into that closed year;
- when one leave type has policies with different leave-year start months, the year-end checks only the first employee's year, so it can close
  other employees' year before it has ended.

The eight low points can wait or be ruled on.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: a reversed usage gives its days back as leave that never expires (Build items 9, 11, 12; FR-HRM-LVE-03, LVE-06, LVE-08)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Application/Leave/LeaveLots.cs` lines 74 to 100: a positive line becomes a lot only when it has `ExpiresOn` and no
`ReversesEntryId`; every other positive line goes to the non-expiring pool. A `usage_reversal` (cancellation and change, `LeaveRequestStore.cs`
lines 47 to 61) and the S1.32a reversal of a negative `adjustment` are positive lines with `ReversesEntryId` and no expiry, so they land in the
pool. The usage they reverse is still in the list and still takes from the lot expiring first (lines 102 to 127). Result: the lot is consumed
by leave that was never taken, and the same quantity sits in the pool without expiry. The same projection feeds the request check
(`LeaveRequestPlanner.BalanceAsync`), the expiry job (`LeaveExpiryJob`), the year-end (`LeaveYearEndPlanner`, `LeaveYearEndPosting`) and the transfer.

What goes wrong: carried-forward leave expiring on 31 March, compensatory off expiring after 30 days, or a grant with an end date, is "laundered"
into permanent leave by booking it and cancelling (or changing) the booking before the expiry. The employee can do this alone (own scope has
`create` and `cancel`; a policy without `cancellationApproval` cancels at once). What is kept is then carried forward, or paid out at year-end
and on transfer (`ILeaveEncashments`), so it reaches payroll. The existing unit test
`FR_HRM_LVE_08_…_a_reversed_lot_does_not_expire` covers a reversed lot, not a reversed usage.

How checked: reproduced (RA).

Suggested fix: make a reversal undo what the reversed entry did. In `Project`, when a line has `ReversesEntryId` pointing to a negative entry,
give back to each lot what that entry took from it (record per negative entry the lots it used) instead of adding to the pool; or, simpler and
exact because a reversal always negates its entry and is dated on or before it for usage, drop both the entry and its reversal before the
simulation (keep them for the running balance only if a reversal can be dated later, as the S1.32a reversal can: then use the first option).
Add unit tests: lot 5 expiring, usage −3, its reversal +3 → 5 expires; and the same with the reversal dated after the usage.

### 2. HIGH: for an employee without a sign-in, the line-manager step goes to the manager of the person who applied (Build item 7, FR-HRM-LVE-05, GHR-09)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveRequestService.cs` line 508 (and 347 for cancellations): the subjects are `[e.UserRef]` only when
the employment has a user; `LeaveRequestStore.cs` lines 117 to 120 set the `employee` field the same way. The kernel resolves "manager" for
`ApproverResolution.AboutPerson(instance)` (`src/Platform/Erp.Kernel.Infrastructure/Workflow/ApproverResolution.cs` line 31): the first subject,
**else the requester**. With no subject, the requester is HR, so HR's own line manager is asked.

What goes wrong: GHR-09 exists because HR enters leave for employees, very often workers without a sign-in. Each such request goes to HR's
manager instead of the worker's (LVE-05 "route line manager"); the worker's manager never sees it; the wrong person can approve it and, through
`LeaveRequestAttachmentOwner` (an open task makes one a reader), read its medical certificate. The fallback to HR administrators only applies
when HR has no manager, which is the case in the tests (the harness's HR users have no employment), so the tests do not show it.
The same subject pattern is in `Attendance/AttendanceRequestService.cs` line 260 (S1.30a) and `Employments/EmploymentService.Moves.cs` line 334 and
`EmploymentService.Changes.cs` line 395 (S1.20), so those routes have the same defect today.

How checked: reproduced (RE).

Suggested fix (one change for all HR document types; a small kernel contract change, so its own change request): let a start request name the
employment it is about (for example `WorkflowStartRequest.SubjectEmploymentIds`, or the directory resolving managers from an employment id), and
make `AboutPerson` never fall back to the requester when the document names a subject employment. Until then, the leave service could pass the
employee's line manager (the user of the employment's `manager_employment_id`) as a `user` field and start the default route with that, falling
back to HR administrators. Add a test with an HR user who has a manager, applying for a worker without a user.

### 3. MEDIUM: a returned request or cancellation is a dead end (Build items 7 and 9, FR-HRM-LVE-05, LVE-06)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveRequestHandlers.cs` lines 50 to 53 (`hrm.leave_request` accepts an outcome only while the request
is `submitted`), lines 156 to 167 (`hrm.leave_cancellation` accepts one only while `cancel_requested`, and turns any non-approval, including
`Returned`, back into `approved`); `LeaveRequestService.cs` lines 306 to 309 (a draft with an instance must be withdrawn through the approvals) and
341 to 349 (a cancellation always starts a new instance; nothing resubmits a returned one).

What goes wrong: (a) after "return", the request is a draft whose instance is `returned`; the leave engine sends the employee to withdraw it,
and the withdrawal calls the handler with `Cancelled`, which throws `not_pending` because the request is a draft, so the withdrawal is rolled back.
The draft can only be resubmitted; it can never be dropped (an administrator's cancel of the instance fails the same way). (b) After a returned
cancellation the leave is `approved` again but the cancellation instance stays open (`returned`): a new cancel is refused by the kernel's one-open-
request rule (`errors.workflow.request_open`), resubmitting is impossible (no API), and withdrawing it throws `not_pending`. That leave can then
never be cancelled.

How checked: reproduced (RD).

Suggested fix: in `LeaveRequestHandler`, accept `Cancelled` also for a `draft` whose `workflow_instance_id` is the outcome's instance (set it
`cancelled`); in `LeaveCancellationHandler`, leave the request `cancel_requested` on `Returned` (the leave stays taken), accept `Cancelled` from
`cancel_requested` (back to `approved`), and let `CancelAsync` resubmit a returned cancellation instance (as `SendAsync` does with `ResubmitAsync`)
instead of starting a new one. Add the two RD scenarios as tests.

### 4. MEDIUM: the year-end confirmation does not see what changed outside its preview's lines (Build item 12, FR-HRM-LVE-11, R-300)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveYearEnd.cs` lines 217 to 230: the staleness check recomputes only the preview's `ok` lines.
The preview leaves out employments and types with no entry in the year (lines 55 to 63) and lines whose closing balance is 0 (lines 84 to 87).
After confirmation the year is closed for every type in the run (`LeaveYearEnds.ClosedAsync`, `LeaveRequestStore.cs` lines 84 to 92).

What goes wrong: a grant, adjustment, opening balance or approved request posted into the year between the preview and the confirmation, for
anyone not on an `ok` line (or turning a 0 line into a balance), is not stale: it is neither carried forward nor lapsed nor paid out, and nobody can
correct it in that year any more (HR's own post is refused `year_closed`). Separately, a request of that year still waiting for approval at
confirmation can no longer be approved (`LeaveRequestHandler` lines 97 to 101 refuse a closed year), so December leave approved in January
after the year-end can only be rejected and is never recorded. R-300 covers only a race with the confirmation, not these ordinary sequences.

How checked: reproduced for the added balance (RB); the waiting request from reading the handler.

Suggested fix: at confirmation, recompute the plan of the whole company and year (as the job does) and refuse as stale when it differs from the
preview in any line (added, removed or changed), and refuse (listing them) while requests of the year are `submitted` or `cancel_requested`. Add a
test for each.

### 5. MEDIUM: a transfer moves only the current leave year; last year's balance is stranded on the ended employment (Build item 14, FR-XM-30)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveTransfers.cs` lines 67 to 74: the balance moved is the one of the leave year of the last day only.
A year before it that is not yet confirmed keeps its balance on the old employment. The year-end then treats that employment as current on the
year's last day (`LeaveYearEnd.cs` line 70: it ended after that day) and carries forward into the new year **on the old employment**, dated before
its end, after the transfer already ran. Also, lines 121 and 122: the `transfer_in` has no expiry, so carried-forward leave that should expire in
March becomes leave without expiry in the new company.

What goes wrong: transfers often take effect in January, while last year's year-end is confirmed weeks later. The carried-forward days never
reach the new employment (and the transfer view shows nothing due); the employee loses them unless HR notices and posts by hand.

How checked: reproduced (RC).

Suggested fix: refuse to post a carry while an earlier leave year of the old employment with a balance is not confirmed (list it as
`waiting_year_end` and post when the year-end applies, or let the year-end's carry-in of a transferred employment go to the next employment with
the transfer's source); carry an expiring lot as its own `transfer_in` with its `expires_on` (one pair per lot) or record the ruling that expiry is
dropped on transfer.

### 6. MEDIUM: comp-off at the year boundary lapses early, or is posted into a closed year (Build items 11 and 12, FR-HRM-LVE-08)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Leave/LeaveFeeds.cs` lines 126 to 137: the entry's leave year is the work date's and its expiry is work
date + `compOffExpiryDays`, which may be after the year's end; nothing checks a closed year. `LeaveLots.cs` line 53 leaves a lot expiring after the
year's end to the year-end, and `YearEndRules.Split` (`LeaveLots.cs` lines 164 to 178) treats it like any balance: without carry-forward on the
comp-off type it lapses on 31 December, with carry-forward it gets the carry-forward's expiry (or none), not its own.

What goes wrong: overtime worked on 20 December earns comp-off usable for 30 days (LVE-08 "with expiry"), but it lapses 11 days later, or lives on
under a different expiry. Comp-off for work late in December announced after the year-end is confirmed (S1.29b processes days after the fact) is
posted into the closed year and is never carried or used.

How checked: by reading; the existing unit test `…a_lot_past_the_year_end_is_left_to_the_year_end…` shows the lot is left to the year-end split.

Suggested fix (needs a ruling): carry a lot whose expiry is after the year's end into the next year with its own expiry, outside the carry-forward
cap; and post comp-off for a closed year into the next open year (dated its first day, same expiry), or refuse and retry with an alert. Test both.

### 7. MEDIUM: the year-end checks "is the year over" with the first employee's year of each type (Build item 12, ruling Q3)

`LeaveYearEnd.cs` lines 65 to 67: a type's span in the run (`spans.TryAdd`) is the year of the first employee processed, by that employee's policy
(`YearStartMonth` is a per-type policy parameter). Each line keeps its own `YearEnd`, but the confirmation's "not over" test uses the types' spans
(`LeaveYearEndService.ConfirmAsync` lines 309 to 313, `LeaveYearEndPosting.ApplyAsync` lines 211 to 215), and closing is by company, type and
year number.

What goes wrong: if two policies of one company give the same type different start months (for example January for staff, July for workers), a
run for year N confirmed in January N+1 posts carry-forward, payout and lapse for the workers in the middle of their year N (which ends in June
N+1) and closes it, so their leave in January to June is refused `year_closed`.

How checked: by reading.

Suggested fix: test "not over" against every line's `YearEnd` (and the types' latest), or refuse a preview whose lines of one type have different
spans until each group can be run separately.

### 8. LOW: every reader of leave requests in scope reads the sensitive files (CR-114, FR-HRM-LVE-16)

`LeaveRequestHandlers.cs` lines 201 to 209: `CanReadSensitive` equals `read`, and readers include anyone holding `hrm.leave_requests.read` in scope;
the baseline `line_manager` role has it with `direct_team`, so a manager reads the medical certificates of every leave request of their team, at
any time, while `WorkflowTaskOwner` keeps a decision's evidence to the people in the request. Please rule whether line managers should read
medical certificates; if not, give sensitive access to the parties, HR (a scope wider than the team, or a separate sensitive permission) and the
open-task approvers only.

### 9. LOW: a required document can be removed or still unscanned after submission (FR-HRM-LVE-16, CR-114)

`LeaveRequestHandlers.cs` lines 203 and 209: the employee and the person who applied may add, replace and **delete** files while the request is
`submitted`; `RecordFiles.cs` line 18 counts files still `uploading` (not yet scanned) as current, and the approval never re-checks. The approver
can therefore see a request of a document-requiring type with no file, or with one the scan later quarantines. Suggested: parties write only while
`draft`; the approval handler checks again that a clean current file exists for a type requiring a document.

### 10. LOW: the day rows of a draft are deleted without an audit entry (CLAUDE.md "every change audited")

`LeaveRequestService.cs` lines 215 and 279 remove the day rows with `ExecuteDeleteAsync`, which bypasses `ChangeAuditInterceptor` (it reads the EF
change tracker). Suggested: load and `RemoveRange` the rows so the interceptor records them (or keep the rows and update them).

### 11. LOW: the unpaid-leave feed reads the type's current flags, not the day's copy (FR-HRM-LVE-07, FR-XM-26)

`LeaveFeeds.cs` line 35 filters on `t.IsPaid` and `t.IsUnpaidDeduction` of the leave type now; the days carry `is_paid` as it was. Changing a used
type's flags (HR, or a pack upgrade: `LeaveTypePackHandler` updates the flags) changes what payroll reads for past and locked periods. Suggested:
use `d.IsPaid` and refuse changes of `is_paid` / `is_unpaid_deduction` on a type with entries (as code, counting and unit are).

### 12. LOW: cancelling approved leave while its change is waiting leaves the change unapprovable

`LeaveRequestService.CancelAsync` (lines 311 to 354) does not look for an open change (`modifies_request_id`), and the "one change at a time" check of
`ChangeAsync` (line 133) runs outside the lock. A cancelled original makes the waiting change refused at approval (`NotApproved`); it can only be
rejected, and it holds its days until then. Suggested: refuse the cancel while a change is open (or withdraw the change in the same transaction),
and move the line-133 check inside the transaction after the lock.

### 13. LOW: two leave-type locks are taken in request order (ruling Q8)

`LeaveRequestService.cs` lines 430 to 434 and `LeaveRequestHandlers.cs` lines 73 to 77 lock the new type, then the old one. Two changes of one
employee in opposite directions (A to B and B to A) can deadlock; PostgreSQL ends one with a concurrency answer. Suggested: lock in a fixed order
(by type id).

### 14. LOW: the expiry job scans every fully used lot forever

`LeaveFeeds.cs` lines 167 and 168 select lots with no expiry entry; a lot used up before its expiry never gets one, so it is projected again every
day until its year is confirmed (and for ever in a company that never confirms). Suggested: limit to lots whose expiry is within the job's look-back
(for example the last 400 days) or record that a lot was checked.

### 15. LOW (for S1.43): a confirmed year-end also closes the year of employees who left before its end

`LeaveYearEnd.cs` lines 70 to 73 list such employments as `separated` and leave them to the final settlement, but `ClosedAsync` closes the year for
the type as a whole, so the final settlement (S1.43) will be refused `year_closed` for any leave entry of a leaver in that year. Record this for
S1.43 (exempt separated lines, or run the final settlement before the year-end).
