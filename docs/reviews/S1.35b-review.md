**Summary for the product owner.** Most of part b is sound. Every amount stays a decimal; the trace of each result lists the inputs, the versions used,
every step, the rounding and the engine version, and has its own fingerprint; the same snapshot gives exactly the same result and trace, also after a worker
is killed in the middle (the tests prove it byte for byte). The calculation now runs as a background job in "lanes"; each batch of employees is saved together
with its bookmark, the worker's hold on the job is checked inside the same database transaction, so a stopped or taken-over lane continues where it
stopped and no employee is calculated twice by one lane. Cancel stops at the next batch. Recalculation replaces a result as a new version of the same row
and the change log keeps the earlier amounts; a locked run is never recalculated (refused by the service and by the database). The trace is shown only
with the new trace permission, inside the data scope and with the salary field class; each view is written to the access log, and the trace is never in
the audit search. No statutory rate, slab or threshold is in the code. 10,000 employees took 39 seconds in the build machine (target 15 minutes).

Two high points should be fixed before the merge:
- **The job can be started from the job centre, which skips the attendance lock.** Anyone who holds the job-centre right to start jobs and the payroll
  right (a tenant or system administrator who is also a payroll manager) can start `hrm.payroll_calculate` directly with parameters of their choice. The
  job then calculates a run whose attendance was never locked (FR-XM-26), before the cut-off date, with any rounding mode, and makes it "calculated".
- **The new custom-divisor rule cuts pay by far more than the missing days when the period has fewer counted days than the divisor.** With the common
  "26 days" rule counted on working days, a whole month pays in full but a joiner who misses one working day loses several days' pay. I reproduced it:
  50,000 a month, five-day week, January 2027 (21 working days): the whole month pays 50,000, a joiner on Monday 4 January (one working day missed) is paid
  38,461.54, while one day of unpaid leave in the whole month costs only 1,923.08. The same happens with a six-day week in a month with Eid holidays.

Two medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- An employee who stops belonging to the run (moved to another pay group, or employment cancelled, after a first calculation) keeps their old result:
  the run is finished "calculated" with that stale result in its totals, and the employee's new pay group cannot calculate them.
- The 30-day rule as ruled pays 2,000 to someone on unpaid leave for the whole of February (0 paid days), and 3,000 for one worked day in February, but 0
  for one worked day after 30 unpaid days in January. The ruling should say what happens at these edges (and the payroll practitioner confirm it).

The seven low points can wait or be ruled on.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: `hrm.payroll_calculate` can be queued from the job centre with any parameters, skipping the lock, the cut-off, the one-calculation guard and the frozen rounding (Build item 9, FR-XM-26, FR-HRM-PAY-34)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Access/jobs.json` lines 168 to 184 (`"manual": true`, `"request_permission": "hrm.pay.run"`); the kernel requires a job
without a schedule to be manual (`JobManifest.cs` line 160), and the job centre's `POST /api/jobs/definitions/{id}/run` (`JobCentreService.RunNowAsync`,
line 156) and `POST /api/jobs/runs/{id}/rerun` (`RerunAsync`, line 171) queue it for anyone with `job.runs.create` and `hrm.pay.run` in the company they
name. `PayrollCalculateJob.RunAsync` (line 45) and `Parse` (line 204) accept any well-formed `pay_run_id`, `request_id`, `lane`, `lanes`, `fresh`,
`rounding_mode` and `currency_decimals`; `PayCalculator.ChunkAsync` (line 116) checks only that the run's status is `created` or `inputs_imported`. Nothing
checks that the request was made by `PayRunService.QueueAsync`: that it is the run's last request in the stage log, that this job run is one of its
`jobRunIds`, that its parameters are the ones recorded, that the period's attendance is locked by this run, or that the requester's data scope covers the
run's company (the job centre checks the company the caller names, not the run's). The job description ("started and cancelled from the payroll run, not
from here") is not enforced. The kernel baseline roles `tenant_admin` and `system_admin` hold `job.*`; in a small customer the same person is often also
`payroll_manager`.

Failure scenario: on 20 November a tenant administrator who is also payroll manager opens the job centre, chooses "Payroll calculation" and runs it with
the November run's id, a new request id, lane 0 of 1, `fresh` true, rounding `half_even` and 0 decimals. The cut-off (25 November) has not come and the
attendance is not locked; the job calculates every employee from the unlocked attendance, rounds to whole rupees half-to-even instead of the company's paisa
and half away from zero, makes the run `calculated` and publishes `hrm.pay_run.calculated`. `POST .../calculate` is now refused (not calculable), so the run
goes on to validation (S1.38) with attendance that can still change (FR-XM-26 broken), and the stage log has a "calculation_started" for a request id that no calculation request recorded.
A second variant: while a real two-lane calculation runs, a "rerun" of a failed lane of an older request with another lane count works the same employees
in parallel; the guard of `PayRunService.QueueAsync` (line 279) does not see it and `cancel-calculation` cannot stop it.

Suggested fix: make the job refuse to work for a request it was not queued for. In the start chunk (under the run row `for update`, as `StageOnceAsync`
already takes it): find the run's last `calculation_requested` or `recalculation_requested` entry; require its `request` to equal `request_id`, its
`jobRunIds` to contain `job.RunId`, and `lanes`, `fresh`, `roundingMode` and `currencyDecimals` to equal the parameters; require
`IAttendancePeriodLock.IsLockedAsync` for the run's period (locked by this run); otherwise fail with a key such as `errors.hrm.pay.job.not_requested`
(no retry). Better still, let a manifest say that a job is queued only by its module (for example `"queued_by": "module"`, allowed without a schedule and
refused by `RunNowAsync` and `RerunAsync`), which `IJobRequests` was added for; that is a kernel manifest change to record in the contracts register.
Add an integration test: a job-centre run-now and a rerun of `hrm.payroll_calculate` are refused (or fail at once) and write no result.

### 2. HIGH: a custom divisor larger than the period's counted days takes several days' pay for one missing day (CR-093 S1.35b detail a, FR-HRM-PAY-05)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Application/Payroll/PayEngine.cs` lines 561 to 578: for a fixed divisor the paid basis is the divisor when the whole period is
employed (line 567), otherwise `min(divisor, employed counted days)`. The coordinator's ruling settled the 30-day rule (`fixed_30`, calendar days: 28 to 31
counted days against 30); the build applied the same form to `custom` divisors ("same defect, same fix"). For a custom divisor counted on **working days**
the period's own count is often below the divisor: a five-day week has 20 to 23 working days, a six-day week 24 to 27, fewer in a month with public
holidays (the engine's non-working days include the holiday calendar). Then a whole period pays the full amount, but the first missing day costs
`(divisor − counted days employed) / divisor` of the salary: the days the period does not have are taken as well. The edge the ruling accepted for
February (2 days at most) becomes 3 to 6 days. Unpaid leave, in contrast, costs one day each (line 574), so the same missing day costs more for a joiner
than for a person on unpaid leave. S1.35a's formula deducted one divisor-day per missing day; part a's unit assertion for `custom` 26 over working days
was changed to the new form (`PayEngineUnitTests.cs` line 210), so the test now keeps the cut.

Failure scenario (reproduced, R1): a company with a five-day week pays a 26-day rule over working days. In January 2027 (21 working days) everybody employed
all month gets 50,000. A new hire starting on Monday 4 January (one working day, Friday 1 January, missed) gets 38,461.54: one day missed costs 11,538.46.
A colleague employed all month with one day of unpaid leave gets 48,076.92. Worked out (not run): with a six-day week and the 26-day rule, a 31-day month
with four Sundays and four days of Eid holidays has 23 working days; a joiner who misses one working day is paid 22/26 = 42,307.69 instead of about
48,000.

Suggested fix (needs your ruling; a payroll practitioner should confirm it, R-175): keep the ruled form for `fixed_30` and for a custom divisor counted on
calendar days, and for a custom divisor whose period has fewer counted days than the divisor use the period's own count, `amount × (employed counted days −
unpaid days) / counted days of the period` (a whole period stays exact, one missing day costs one of the period's days); or keep S1.35a's deduction form
for custom divisors. Either is a new engine version. Add unit tests: five-day week January with a joiner on the 4th, six-day week with holidays, and the
whole-month and unpaid-day cases, with the expected amounts written out.

### 3. MEDIUM: an employee who leaves the run's eligibility keeps their old result, and the run is finished with it (Build items 9 and 11, FR-HRM-PAY-34, -07, -33)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Payroll/Engine/PayCalculator.cs` lines 80 to 106 (`NextAsync` walks `EligibleAsync` only), 332 to 342
(`FinishAsync` counts pending and incomplete employees among the eligible only) and 355 to 362 (totals and `employee_count` over every result of the run);
`PayRunService.cs` line 309 (a recalculation marks every calculated row `included`) and line 316. `EligibleAsync` (`PaySnapshotBuilder.cs` line 42) is read
again at each chunk, so an employee whose assignment moved to another pay group (a backdated change), whose employment was cancelled or whose separation
was backdated before the period after their first calculation is simply no longer listed. Their `hrm.pay_run_employees` row (status `calculated`,
`included` or `error`, `run_status` not `cancelled`) and their result, lines, trace and consumed one-time inputs stay.

Failure scenario: run A (November, pay group A) is calculated; HR then records a transfer of employee E to pay group B from 1 November; payroll
recalculates run A for everybody. E is marked `included` but never calculated (not eligible); `FinishAsync` finds nothing pending, makes run A
`calculated` with E's old full-month result in the totals and the employee count, and E's row stays `included`. Run B then reaches E: the insert of E's
row hits the exclusion constraint `ex_pay_run_employees_employment_id_period` with run A's row, the lane fails with `errors.hrm.pay.run.employee_in_other_run`
and run B cannot finish; if run A is approved and locked first, E is paid November from the wrong pay group, and a bonus entered for E and consumed by
run A is never paid by run B (it stays `consumed` by run A).

Suggested fix: when a request is queued and again in `FinishAsync` (under the run row `for update`), find the run's rows whose employment is no longer
eligible; remove their results, lines and traces (the change log keeps them, as for an incomplete employee), give the consumed one-time inputs back
(`pending`, `consumed_by_run_id` null) and mark the row `excluded` with `run_status = 'cancelled'` (or a flag `not_eligible` the summary shows), so
totals, the count and the exclusion constraint follow the eligible employees only. Add an integration test: calculate, move one employee to another pay
group from the period start, recalculate, and check run A's count and totals and that run B calculates the employee.

### 4. MEDIUM: the 30-day rule pays days that are not paid at the edges of the month (CR-093 S1.35b detail a, R-175, FR-HRM-PAY-05)

`PayEngine.cs` lines 567 and 574: a whole period employed has the basis 30 whatever the month's length, and each unpaid day costs amount / 30.
- In February a person employed all month and on unpaid leave every day is paid 30,000 × (30 − 28) / 30 = 2,000 while the result's `PaidDays` is 0
  (reproduced, R2); with one worked day and 27 unpaid days, 3,000 (a joiner on 28 February with one day is paid 1,000).
- In a 31-day month a person with 30 days of unpaid leave and one worked day is paid nothing (30 − 30 = 0) while `PaidDays` is 1 (reproduced); with all
  31 days unpaid the part would be negative and is floored at 0 (line 599).
The ruling's examples do not cover these cases, so the amounts are the formula's, not a decision. The trace and the payslip will show a salary line that
does not match the paid days.

Failure scenario: an employee on long unpaid leave (for example extended sick leave without pay) through February 2027 is paid 2,000 of basic salary and
the percentage allowances and formulas based on it; in March, with the same leave all month, nothing; payroll cannot explain the February payment from the
paid days.

Suggested fix (needs your ruling with the payroll practitioner): at least, pay nothing when no employed counted day is paid (`employed − unpaid ≤ 0`), and
never more days than are paid when the period is shorter than 30 days and the person had unpaid leave; decide whether one worked day after 30 unpaid days in
a 31-day month pays one day. Record the edges in R-175 with the amounts, and add the cases to `FR_HRM_PAY_05_R_175_the_30_day_rule_…`.

### 5. LOW: the linear-growth check of the performance test is weak and wall-clock based (Build item 10, GHR-01)

`tests/Erp.IntegrationTests/PayRuns/PayRunPerformanceTests.cs`: the baseline is a recalculation of a tenth of the employees, which also deletes and rewrites
lines and traces and carries the fixed costs of a request (the lock call, queuing, the worker's first poll) over only 100 employees. On this machine it cost
19.00 ms per employee against 8.02 ms for the full run, so `perEmployee <= basePerEmployee * 2` passes even if the full run became about 4.7 times slower
per employee; the performance document's own numbers (3.9 against 14.2 ms) give the same slack. Both budgets are wall-clock thresholds, which CLAUDE.md's
"Reliable tests" asks to avoid where operations can be counted. The 15-minute projection itself is generous enough not to flake.
Suggested fix: compare like with like (two first calculations of different sizes, for example 200 and 1,000 employees in two pay groups), or count the
database commands per employee (an `DbCommandInterceptor` counter in the rig) and require that number to stay constant between the two sizes; keep the
15-minute projection as the stated budget.

### 6. LOW: quadratic work under the run lock at 50,000 employees (R-173, S1.52)

`PayRunService.cs` line 316 (`eligible.Count(id => rows.FirstOrDefault(...))`) and line 309 (`recalculate.Contains` on a list) run inside the transaction
that holds `hrm.pay_runs` `for update`; for a recalculation of 50,000 employees that is about 2.5 × 10⁹ comparisons in the request. Each chunk also reads
the whole eligibility list and every row status of the run (`PayCalculator.NextAsync`, lines 82 and 89) and `FinishAsync` reads them again: 250 chunks of
200 at 50,000 employees read 25 million rows. Not visible at 10,000 (39 s), but S1.52 measures 50,000.
Suggested fix: a dictionary of rows by employment and a hash set of the listed ids; in `NextAsync` select only the lane's next ids in SQL (eligible, after
the cursor, without a done row, `limit` the chunk size; the lane test `LaneOf` can be a SQL expression over the last four bytes, or the lane can be
stored on the row at queue time).

### 7. LOW: two lanes can sweep the same employees at once (Build item 9, R-324)

`PayrollCalculateJob.cs` lines 90 to 113 and `PayCalculator.FinishAsync` lines 334 to 339: the sweep is given to every lane whose finish sees all lanes done
and employees pending. A lane whose job run is retried after its finish chunk committed (worker crash before the run was marked succeeded) finds its
`lane_done` already recorded and also gets `Sweep`, while the last lane sweeps. `ChunkAsync` reads the employees' rows without a lock (line 128), so both
calculate the same newly eligible employee: one insert fails on the exclusion constraint and that lane ends failed for good with the misleading key
`errors.hrm.pay.run.employee_in_other_run` (`PayrollCalculateJob.cs` line 164), or both rewrite an `included` row. No result is written twice (the constraints hold), so
this is low.
Suggested fix: take the chunk's `hrm.pay_run_employees` rows `for update` (and skip ids locked by another lane, `skip locked`), or record a single sweep
owner (`sweep` entry with the job run id) under the run row lock in `FinishAsync`.

### 8. LOW: `IJobRequests` trusts the caller with the requester and skips the licence check (CR-093 S1.35b detail c)

## Checked and found right

- **Money.** Every amount and rate is `decimal`; the trace writes decimals from their invariant text (`PayTraceDocument.Dec`), never through floating point;
  internal rounding is four places half away from zero (`PayRounding.Internal`), and the trace says so. The ruled 30-day examples (2nd of a 31-day month
  30,000, 31st 1,000, leaver on the 10th 10,000, 15 February 14,000, whole February 30,000, two unpaid days 28,000) are unit-tested and correct; a whole
  period is exact also after a mid-month change of the amount (segments share the basis by employed days; `FR_HRM_PAY_05_R_175_the_calendar_and_…`), and
  the calendar and working-day rules keep their earlier results. The engine version is `S1.35b.1`, so `S1.35a.1` snapshots make an employee incomplete,
  never a silent change.
- **Determinism.** The trace and the result hash are built only from the snapshot and the calculation in canonical JSON (ordinal key order); the stored
  `jsonb` reorders keys, and the endpoint's `traceHash` is computed after putting it back in canonical form, so it is stable. The tests check byte-identical
  snapshot, result and trace hashes after a recalculation from stored snapshots and after a killed worker, and that one changed input changes only that
  employee; a recalculation that changes nothing writes no `update` entry in the change log.
- **Chunks, checkpoint and takeover.** Each chunk runs inside `CommitChunkAsync`, which takes the job run `for update`, checks the lease owner and the cancel
  flag, runs the work and saves the checkpoint in the same transaction; a lost lease or a cancel saves nothing. Lanes are a fixed function of the employment
  id, so two lanes of one request never share an employee; the chunk re-reads the employees' statuses, so an employee with a result is skipped after a
  restart. The order of `Guid.CompareTo` matches PostgreSQL's uuid order, so the cursor is consistent.
- **Locks on the run.** Chunks hold `hrm.pay_runs` `for share` and check the status; the finish, the stage entries, the queue and the cancel take it
  `for update`; the lock order (job run, then pay run) is the same everywhere, so I found no deadlock between lanes, the finish and the cancel. The finish
  makes the run calculated once (it re-checks the status under the row lock), writes the totals, the employees' copy of the status and the event once
  per request. Two requests at once are refused by the row-version check under the lock (`QueueAsync`).
- **HR context on the chunk connection.** `OnAsync` points the scope's `HrmDbContext` at the chunk's connection and transaction and clears the tracker
  before and after; `PayCalculator`, the snapshot builder and the input providers share that context, so the inputs, the results and the checkpoint commit
  together. The cancel is recorded on a context of its own after the chunk that saw it saved nothing.
- **One-time inputs.** `ConsumedAsync` takes the rows `for update` and rolls the chunk back if one was cancelled meanwhile (S1.35a finding 5 fixed); a
  recalculation re-reads inputs consumed by the same run.
- **Recalculation.** Only `created`, `inputs_imported` and `calculated` runs; a calculated run goes back to `created` with its totals cleared until every
  employee has a result again; the result row is kept (`row_version` + 1) and its lines and trace replaced; an employee who becomes incomplete loses the
  result (never a stale or zero one, with finding 3's exception); a locked run is refused by the service (409 `not_calculable`) and by the database
  (`ck_pay_run_locked`, tested). The lock-first and cut-off checks (S1.35a findings 2) apply to calculation and recalculation alike.
- **Permissions, scope, field classes, GHR-06.** `hrm.pay.trace.read` is new, sensitive, in `permissions.json` and in `PayRunPermissions.HandWritten`, given
  to `payroll_officer` and `payroll_manager`; the trace read applies the result's data scope (404 outside it) and refuses without the salary class (403
  `errors.hrm.pay.trace.salary_class`); it writes the access log before answering; `hrm.pay_traces` stays unaudited (CR-034) and the audit search shows no
  trace (tested). Part a's results read now writes the access log when it shows amounts. Job parameters, job logs, the stage log, the summary's progress and
  its `incomplete` problems hold ids, counts, keys and component codes, never an amount.
- **Kernel contract `IJobRequests`.** Queue and cancel run in the caller's PostgreSQL transaction (the run's stage entry and its job runs commit together),
  write the same log lines and events as the job centre, and are registered in both hosts; the contract is in the contracts register.
- **Tenant isolation.** The three new routes have probes in `HrmProbe` with real material in A (a queued calculation to cancel, a result with a trace) and
  positive controls; the suite passes (920 of 920).
- **Tests and FR ids.** Every new test names its FR or GHR id; part a's integration assertions are kept, now through the job (`CalculateAsync` waits for the
  lanes and checks no job run failed); the one changed unit assertion follows the ruling (finding 2 asks to revisit its `custom` line). No test was skipped
  or weakened otherwise.
- **No statutory value.** No rate, slab or threshold in the diff; the divisor 30 is the name of the `fixed_30` rule, a company convention (R-175).
- **Registers and documents.** CR-093 S1.35b detail a to f, R-173, R-175 (ruled), R-176, R-324 to R-326, the contracts register (API version 2, event,
  snapshot version, trace, job, `IJobRequests`), the handoff, the performance document, `CLAUDE.md` and the settings, jobs and permissions manifests agree
  with the code.
