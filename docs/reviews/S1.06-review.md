## Summary for the product owner

Most of the access-control work is solid:
- One engine decides for every endpoint.
- Roles assigned for company A grant nothing in company B.
- "Cannot grant what you do not hold" is checked for the requester and again for the approver.
- The Tenant Admin role keeps Manage Roles and Assign Roles.
- Every SQL value is a parameter, and no frozen migration was touched.

I found **three serious defects**. All three let people see salary or personal records they should not see:

1. **Every employee can read every other employee's tax declaration amounts.** Example: zakat, donations or pension-fund amounts. The table of declaration lines was given no data-scope key, so the engine treats it as "not scoped": the permission alone decides. The shipped Employee role holds that permission and may see salary-class amounts. I reproduced it: an employee opened a colleague's declaration line and got `declaredAmount: 777777`. The same employee was correctly refused the colleague's declaration header.
2. **A data scope can be widened with one ordinary API call, without the second person or any record.** Example: changing "Own records" to "All records". The generated endpoint `PUT /api/iam/data-scopes/{id}` is still open, and the scope rows are shared by every role that uses them. I reproduced it: the Tenant Admin's request through the proper service was refused (403, they do not hold the HR permission). The same person then changed the shared scope row directly: 200. The role's users saw every employee at once. No maker-checker request and no `iam.*` event was created. Done on the shipped "own" scope, this would show every payslip to every employee.
3. **Salary leaks when a person holds two roles on the same records** (needs your decision). The Employee role lets people see their *own* salary. That permission is not tied to "own" rows: it applies to every row the person can see through any of their roles. I reproduced it:
   - A manager holding the Employee role was also given a "team pay" role with salary hidden. They then saw a report's gross pay of 150,000.
   - A manager with the team role alone did not see it.

   The shipped roles do not combine like this today. But the first customer who lets managers or HR Admin see pay records "without amounts" will leak salaries. Ruling Q4 ("most open field access wins") allows this as written, so a refinement of Q4 is needed.

There are also **four medium issues**:
4. The "last tenant admin" rule counts a Tenant Admin assignment that only starts in the future. So the last real administrator can leave, and nobody is an administrator until that date (reproduced).
5. One administrator alone can switch maker-checker off, or switch off MFA for privileged users. These are ordinary settings, written through a generated endpoint that records nothing. So maker-checker does not stop a single rogue administrator. This **needs your decision**.
6. An approver is not checked against the company of the change. An approver may also approve a role being given to themselves.
7. A held permission change stores the whole permission list. If someone else changes the role while it waits, approving it silently undoes their change.

Low items and test gaps follow. I recommend fixing 1, 2 and 4 before any real customer data is loaded; each is small, and the fixes are listed below. Item 3 needs your ruling first.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: every employee reads every tax declaration line, amounts included

- **Where:**
  - `docs/data-design/hrm-pay.yaml:339` (`hrm.tax_declaration_lines`): `field_class` is set but there is no `scope:`. The table locates its owner only through `declaration_id`.
  - `AccessEngine.cs:97-100`: an unscoped target means "every row".
  - `src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Access/baseline-roles.json:43`: Employee holds `hrm.tax_declaration_lines.read`.
  - Same file, line 63: Employee has `class:salary` `read_only` on resource `*`.
- **Evidence:** reproduction 4.
  - `GET /api/hrm/tax-declarations/{colleague's}` → 404, which is correct.
  - `GET /api/hrm/tax-declaration-lines/{its line}` → `200 {"declarationId":…,"kind":"C101117","declaredAmount":777777.0000,…}`.
  - The list endpoint returns every line of every employee.
- **Scenario:** any employee lists all declaration lines. That gives each colleague's exemption and credit amounts (zakat, donations, pension fund), grouped by declaration.
- **Fix:**
  - Short term: remove `hrm.tax_declaration_lines.read` from the Employee baseline until the table can be scoped (`migrate` does not remove grants, so fix customers already provisioned in the same change).
  - Proper fix (both need a CR):
    - Let `scope:` locate a row through its parent, e.g. `scope: {via: declaration_id → hrm.tax_declarations}`.
    - Or add `employment_id` and `company_id` to the line table.
  - Also: the generator should refuse a role manifest that gives a narrow scope (`own`, `direct_team`, `subtree`) to a permission on an unscoped table. Make that a test (T1).
- **Also unscoped:** `hrm.pay_traces` (calculation inputs per payslip). Nobody but all-scope roles holds it today. It needs the same treatment before any narrower role gets it.

### 2. HIGH: generated writes to `iam.data_scopes` bypass maker-checker, "cannot grant" and the audit events

- **Where:**
  - `docs/data-design/kernel.yaml:167-174`: `iam.data_scopes` has no `api_actions`.
  - So `src/Platform/Erp.Kernel.Api/Generated/Iam/DataScopeEndpoints.g.cs:60-61` maps `POST` and `PUT` (kind, definition, is_active all writable).
  - Data-scope rows are shared: `AccessSetup` seeds `all`, `own`, `direct_team` and `subtree` once, and every baseline role points at them (`AccessSetup.cs`, `RolesAsync`). Tenant Admin holds `iam.*`, so it holds `iam.data_scopes.update`.
- **Evidence:** reproduction 1. A sensitive role was set up with `hrm.employments` → a test scope of kind `own`.
  - `PUT /api/iam/roles/{id}/data-scopes` as Tenant Admin → `403 errors.access.cannot_grant_unheld`, which is correct.
  - `PUT /api/iam/data-scopes/{scope}` `{kind: "all", …, rowVersion: 1}` as the same Tenant Admin → `200`. The role's user then read an employment outside their scope.
  - Maker-checker requests: unchanged. `iam.outbox_messages` for the scope: 0.
- **Scenario:** one Tenant Admin call changes the shipped `own` scope to `all`. Every Employee then sees every employee's records. The Employee salary rule (finding 3) makes that include every payslip amount. All of this happens with no second person, against ruling C ("scope changes on sensitive roles count as permission changes"), and with nothing for S1.07 to record.

  Setting `isActive: false` on `all` silently removes access from every role, the Tenant Admin role included: its `*` scope is `all`.
- **Fix:**
  - Close the writes with `api_actions: [read]` (an extension of CR-021).
  - Add a scope service in the access administration, with the scope designer of S1.13. A scope already used by roles should be immutable (create a new one and repoint roles). Otherwise treat an edit as a scope change of every role using it: grant check, maker-checker if any of them is sensitive, and an `iam.*` event.
  - The other generated writes still open on `iam.*` (`iam.delegations`, `iam.users`) should be reviewed with the same question. Delegations are not read by the engine yet, so this is not urgent.

### 3. HIGH (needs a product-owner decision): field access is merged across roles regardless of which role makes the row visible

- **Where:** `AccessEngine.cs:131-145` (`ForAsync`) and `:148` (`Merge`).
  - Field access is the most open level among every role that grants `resource.read` for the row's company.
  - It is not limited to the roles whose data scope covers that row.
  - `GeneratedEndpointSupport.ShapeAsync` shapes per company only.
- **Evidence:** reproduction 2.
  - The user holds Employee (`*` own, `class:salary` read-only) and a customer role "team pay" (`hrm.pay_results.read`, `direct_team`, no salary rule).
  - `GET /api/hrm/pay-results/{report's}` → `200 {…"gross":150000.0000,"deductions":…,"net":…}`.
  - Control: a manager holding only the team role gets the row without `gross`.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Nearly every user holds Employee. The rule "you may see salary" written for your own payslip then applies to every pay row you can see through any other role.
  - The shipped roles do not overlap on salary resources today: Line Manager and HR Admin hold none. So this is latent.
  - It triggers as soon as a customer follows ruling B ("HR Admin sees no salary until the customer opens it"): giving HR Admin pay records without amounts shows HR Admin every amount.
  - Ruling Q4 ("for a field the most open access among those roles wins") permits this as written, but it defeats GHR-06 and the spec 02 AC.
- **Suggested ruling (CR):** for each row, merge field access only over the roles that both grant the permission and whose scope covers that row.
- **Cost:** the engine already builds one predicate per grant. It can tag each row with the grants that matched, or evaluate the compiled predicates per row: cheap for a page of 50.
- **Alternative:** a simpler stop-gap is to make the Employee salary rule apply only to rows matched by its own scope. That still needs per-row evaluation.

### 4. MEDIUM: a future-dated Tenant Admin assignment counts as a tenant admin

- **Where:** `src/Platform/Erp.Kernel.Infrastructure/Access/TenantAdminGuard.cs:28,33`. Both branches test only `valid_to is null`, never `valid_from <= today`.
- **Evidence:** reproduction 3. A customer of its own, with maker-checker off as a micro customer may set it:
  - The only admin assigns Tenant Admin to another user from five years ahead → `201`.
  - The admin ends their own assignment → `200`.
  - Tenant Admin assignments valid today: 0. Both users get 403 on `/api/iam/permission-catalog`.
- **With maker-checker on:** the same happens with two admins approving each other's requests.
- **Impact:** recovery then needs the platform Super Admin (`tenant admin-grant`).
- **Fix:** add `ur.valid_from <= $today` and `gr.valid_from <= $today` (injected clock). The same check belongs in `PlatformAdminRecovery.RevokeAsync`'s "now has no tenant administrator" warning, which uses the same count.
- **Test to add:** future-dated assignment + end the last current one → `400 errors.tenant_admin.last_admin`.

### 5. MEDIUM (needs a product-owner decision): one administrator alone can switch off maker-checker and privileged MFA, and nothing is recorded

- **Where:**
  - `iam.maker_checker.sensitive_roles` and `iam.mfa.required_for_privileged` (`IdentitySettings.cs`) are ordinary `cfg.setting_values` rows.
  - Tenant Admin holds `cfg.*` (`src/Platform/Erp.Kernel.Infrastructure/Access/baseline-roles.json:46`).
  - The generated `POST/PUT /api/cfg/setting-values` are open (`SettingValueEndpoints.g.cs:60-61`). Generated writes raise no outbox event (same mechanism as finding 2, where 0 events were measured).
- **Scenario:** a rogue or compromised administrator switches maker-checker off, assigns themselves or a friend a sensitive role, and switches it back on. No second person is involved and the setting change leaves no trace. Q6 says "the customer can switch the setting off", but not that one person can do it unseen.
- **Suggested:**
  - When the customer has two or more active tenant admins, turning either setting off is itself a maker-checker request.
  - In every case, settings with keys `iam.*` change only through a service that raises an event.
  - Settings writes may be planned for S1.15. If so, record this as a risk until then.

### 6. MEDIUM: an approval is not checked against the change's company, and an approver may approve their own gain

- **Where:** `AccessAdministrationService.Changes.cs:26-80` (`ApproveAsync`).
  - It re-runs `ValidateAsync` and `CheckGrantsAsync`, but not the company coverage of `CheckAssignmentAsync` / `EndAnyAsync` (ruling A).
  - The only identity check is `request.RequestedBy == by` (`:41`).
- **Scenario:**
  - An approver whose roles cover only company A can approve an all-companies assignment, because `HoldsAsync` is a union over companies.
  - User B holds `iam.maker_checker.approve`. A requests "give B the Payroll Officer role for all companies", and B approves their own new access. B already held the role's permissions for company A only, so B gains company B. FR-XM-32 ("nobody approves their own") is meant to stop exactly this.
- **Fix:**
  - At approval, require `CoversCompanyAsync(ApproveChanges, change.CompanyId)` and `CoversCompanyAsync(AssignRoles, …)` for assignment changes.
  - Refuse when the approver is `change.UserId`, or a member of `change.GroupId` for group changes.

### 7. MEDIUM–LOW: a held permission change applies a stale full list

- **Where:**
  - `SetPermissionsAsync` stores the complete list (`AccessAdministrationService.cs:219`).
  - `ApplyPermissionsAsync` (`…Changes.cs`) computes added and removed against the role *at approval time*.
- **Scenario:** request 1 removes X (pending). Meanwhile request 2 adds Y and is approved. When request 1 is approved, it removes Y as well, and the approver sees only "remove X" in the request.
- **Fix:** store the delta (added/removed), or store the role's `row_version` in the request and refuse approval when the role has changed since.

### 8. LOW: copying a role copies its scopes and field rules unchecked

- **Where:** `AccessAdministrationService.cs:104-138`. Only the copied permissions are checked (`:105`). Data scopes (`:118`) and field rules are copied as they are. A copy of a sensitive role is not held for a second person.
- **Impact:** small, because the copy grants nothing until assigned, and assignment is checked and held. But a person who may not open salary on a role through `field-policies` can create one with salary open by copying Payroll Officer. That bypasses the field check in `CheckGrantsAsync`.
- **Fix:** run the field-rule check over the copied rules, and treat a copy of a sensitive role like a permission change.

### 9. LOW (question): masking money to its last four digits reveals most of a salary

- **Where:** `FieldSecurity.cs:149`. 85,000 becomes `****5000`, and 125,500 becomes `****5500`.
- **Question:** ruling Q5 said "last 4 characters", which suits account numbers and CNICs, not amounts. Should masked `money` fields show `****` only, or a band?

### 10. LOW (question): a Company Admin of one company administers every user record

- **Where:** `iam.users` has only a `user` locator (`kernel.yaml`), so by ruling A it is not company-limited.
- **Scenario:** a Company Admin assigned for company A only holds `iam.users.update`. They can therefore change the username or e-mail of any user, Tenant Admins included. The changed e-mail is where later reset links will go (S1.11).
- **Question:** should user administration be limited to users holding roles only in the admin's companies, and never touch Tenant Admins?

### 11. LOW: the Super Admin check trusts a self-declared e-mail

- **Where:** `PlatformAdminRecovery.cs` checks `actor.Role == platform_admin`. The actor comes from the environment variable `ERP_PLATFORM_ACTOR` (`OperatorCommands.cs:120-121`), an e-mail typed by whoever runs the migrator.
- **Impact:** anyone with migrator access and control-plane credentials can name any platform admin and run `tenant admin-grant`. This is the S1.03 operator model, and the handoff already proposes console MFA and a two-person rule for 1.1. Record it as a risk so the "only a Super Admin" wording in CR-024 is not read as an authenticated control.

### 12. LOW: smaller points

- **Data-scope preview** (`…Previews.cs:53`) needs only `iam.access.preview`.
  - It returns counts and ids of any resource, as any user, whatever the caller may read or which company they cover. Example: how many disciplinary or medical records a named person has.
  - Suggest also requiring `resource.read` and company coverage of the sample user.
- **Repair marks the role as untouched:** `tenant admin-role-repair` writes `updated_by = system user` (`PlatformAdminRecovery.cs:43`). A customer-edited Tenant Admin role then counts as "never edited", and the next `migrate` tops it up. This is probably acceptable; say so in the runbook. The `update … set is_active = true` there stamps no audit columns.
- **Privileged password length:** the 14-character minimum (Q10) applies only at the next password change. Existing privileged users keep shorter passwords until then. Decide whether a privileged assignment should set `must_change`.
- **Sign-in timing:** known users now run one more query (`IdentitySettings.cs:144`, role facts) that unknown users skip. This reopens a small part of S1.05 finding 9. The fix is to run the query with a dummy id on the unknown path.

## Checked and found correct

- **Tenancy:**
  - The engine and the workforce directory read only the host-bound customer's data source.
  - Business tables gained no `tenant_id`.
  - The new access endpoints are probed by the isolation suite (`AccessProbe`), which passed.
- **Module boundary (D-10):**
  - The kernel asks the HR module through `Erp.Kernel.Contracts.Access.IWorkforceDirectory`. Only ids cross.
  - The kernel never reads `hrm.*`. Subtree uses the kernel's own `org.org_unit_links`, cycle-safe through `UNION`.
  - Architecture tests passed.
- **Deny by default:**
  - No grant means a `false` predicate.
  - A role without a scope for a scoped resource adds no rows.
  - An unreadable scope definition becomes "matches nothing".
  - A condition on an attribute the table does not declare matches nothing.
- **Writes:**
  - The scope is checked on the row before and after the change.
  - A non-editable field that is sent changed gives 400 `errors.access.field_not_editable`. A missing value keeps the stored one.
  - Custom-field keys are merged key by key.
- **Company (ruling A):** checked on reads, on writes and when giving or ending assignments (`CoversCompanyAsync`). An all-companies assignment needs an all-companies giver.
- **Grant rules:**
  - Permissions are checked for the requester and again for the approver.
  - A field rule cannot open a field wider than the caller's own access. The default for a removed rule never opens salary.
  - A `*` scope needs every permission of the role.
- **Maker-checker:**
  - The request row is locked during a decision, and the requester cannot approve.
  - Rejection and expiry apply nothing, and expiry is recorded even when the decision is refused.
- **Last tenant admin:** counted before and after the change under an advisory lock, so two concurrent removals cannot both pass (apart from finding 4).
- **CR-024:** the protected role keeps its mandatory permissions, including at approval. It cannot be archived. The recovery commands require a reason and are recorded both in `cp.platform_audit` and as customer events.
- **Registry and baselines:**
  - Seeding is idempotent and runs under an advisory lock.
  - Retired codes are kept and grant nothing, and roles the customer edited are left alone (Q8).
  - Data scopes and roles are keyed by stable codes.
- **SQL:** every value is a parameter. Interpolated text is limited to constant table names and constant fragments.
- **Other rules:**
  - The injected clock is used everywhere, with no `DateTime.Now`/`UtcNow`.
  - All new error keys exist in English and Urdu.
  - No new package was added.
