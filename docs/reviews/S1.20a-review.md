**Summary for the product owner.** The person services are careful work. Every write checks the permission, checks the
data scope, and checks field security. Identifier numbers are encrypted, searched through a blind index and shown masked.
A full view needs a separate permission and is written to the access log. A merge waits for a second person, and the
person who asked cannot approve it. The approval runs in the kernel's own transaction, so a refused approval changes
nothing. The whole test suite passes at `43a26a1`. There is no high finding. Four medium points need fixing, or a ruling
from you, before the merge:
- the approvals inbox shows the names of persons the approver is not allowed to see;
- one administrator can switch off the merge approval and then merge alone;
- a merge can move another company's employment records without a check on that company;
- the required merge reason is thrown away when approval is off.

The seven low points can wait.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. RESOLVED (was HIGH at `3aee3d4`): older tests called the generated writes the PR had closed
- At `3aee3d4`, `tests/Erp.IntegrationTests/Numbering/NumberingApiTests.cs:43`/`:50` and `NumberingReviewFixTests.cs:131`/`:137`
  created employments through `POST /api/hrm/employments`, which that commit had removed, together with the
  `hrm.employments.create`/`.update` codes. The test
  `FR_KRN_NUM_03_FR_HRM_EMP_04_creating_an_employment_through_the_generated_api_...` failed: `AccessHarness.RoleAsync`
  expected 3 permissions and found 1 (filter-class `NumberingApiTests`: total 7, failed 1). `AuditTests.cs:388` posted
  `religion`, which the new service refuses.
- `43a26a1` reopens the employment tables' generated writes. They stay open until S1.20b to d, as before this slice; the
  bypass is recorded as R-113. The same commit adapts the audit test: the second personal value is now a relation's phone,
  and the test still asserts that the value is absent from the change log in plain text and that the row carries the subject
  person. The point of the test is kept, not weakened. The full suite above is green. Not counted in the totals below.

### 2. MEDIUM: the approvals inbox names held-merge persons outside the approver's data scope, and anyone with the permission can reject them
- `src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/People/PersonMerger.cs:230-245` (`DescribeAsync`) reads both persons without a scope
  check and returns person number and full name in English and Urdu. `AccessAdministrationService.Pending.cs:58-62` shows it
  to every holder of `hrm.persons.merge` (`AccessEndpoints.cs:120`, `HeldKindsAsync`), whatever their data scope.
  `AccessAdministrationService.Held.cs:37-72`: a reject checks only the permission. The handler, and so the data scope,
  is involved only on approve.
- Failure scenario: a customer gives an HR officer of company B a role with `hrm.persons.merge` limited to company B. An HR
  officer of company A asks to merge two company-A persons. Officer B's inbox lists the request with both persons' numbers
  and names. `CheckAsync` refuses the approval as "person not found", but the names have already been shown. Officer B
  can also reject the request, which ends company A's merge. This breaks the slice's rule that no name or number leaves the
  caller's data scope (CR-076 detail h, `PersonService.MatchAsync`) and the review brief's "information leaking outside scope".
- **Fix:** (a) in `DescribeAsync`, and before `CheckAsync`, check `scope.AllowsAsync(person, PersonAccess.Scope, "read")` for the
  viewer. Omit a held change the viewer cannot see from `toDecide`, or show it without names. (b) Add a handler hook for
  rejects (for example `CheckRejectAsync`, or call `CheckAsync` with a reject flag) so a reject needs the same data scope
  as an approve. (c) Add a test with a company-limited approver.

### 3. MEDIUM: one person can switch off merge approval and then merge alone (FR-XM-32 by-pass)
- `src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Settings/settings.yaml:4-11`: `hrm.person_merge.requires_approval` has no
  `requires_approval`, so the generated definition says `"requiresApproval": false` (`Access/settings.g.json`). A holder of
  `cfg.settings.update` sets it to `false` at once, through `SettingsAdministrationService.ChangeAsync`, with no second person.
- Failure scenario: a tenant administrator who also holds `hrm.persons.merge` switches the setting off, merges two persons at
  once (`PersonService.Duplicates.cs:154`), then switches it back on. No second person was involved at any step. This is the
  same by-pass the kernel closed for `iam.maker_checker.sensitive_roles` ("switching it off needs the approval of a second one").
- **Fix:** add `requires_approval: true` to the setting (the settings format supports it for non-`iam.*` keys), regenerate, and
  add a test that switching it off is held. Turning it on can stay immediate if the settings engine allows only
  the loosening direction to be held; otherwise hold both directions.

### 4. MEDIUM: a merge moves employments outside the caller's data scope
- `src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/People/PersonMerger.cs:69-76` checks the data scope of the two persons only.
  `:110-113` then moves every employment of the merged person, whatever its company. Data scopes find a person through any
  employment (R-111), so a role limited to company A can see a person who has an employment in company A, and through
  that person can move the person's company-B employment as well.
- Failure scenario: person P has an ended employment in company A and an active one in company B. An HR officer limited to
  company A, with merge rights, merges P into person Q (approved by a second company-A officer). The company-B employment,
  with its documents and later its pay and leave history, is moved to Q. Nobody with company-B rights saw or approved it.
  Company B's data scopes now find Q. The slice asks for the data scope to be checked "before and after every write".
- **Fix:** in `RunAsync`, check `scope.AllowsAsync(e, EmploymentAccess.Scope, "update")` for each employment to be moved (and
  again after setting `PersonId`), and refuse with a clear key when one is outside the scope. A simpler alternative, if
  the product owner prefers it, is to require a role whose merge scope covers all companies. Add a test with a
  company-limited merger.

### 5. MEDIUM: the required merge reason is discarded when approval is off
- `src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/People/PersonService.Duplicates.cs:125` requires `reason`. On the held path it is
  stored in `iam.maker_checker_requests`. On the direct path (`:154-175`) nothing keeps it: no event, no merge record, and the
  change log has no reason. `PersonMerger.RunAsync` takes no reason.
- Failure scenario: with approval off (ruling Q4 allows it), HR merges two persons and types "same CNIC, typo in name". Later
  an auditor asks why the records were combined. The change log shows rows changed by the user, but the reason is gone,
  although the API required it. FR-HRM-EMP-18 asks for a "merge audit", and Build item 4 says "records the merge (Q5),
  audited".
- **Fix:** record the merge with its reason on both paths. Either raise an outbox event `hrm.person_merged` {personId,
  intoPersonId, reason, requestId?} in the same transaction, or write the reason into the change-log rows of the merge
  if the audit interceptor offers a per-save reason. Assert the stored reason in the existing approval-off test.

### 6. LOW: the create answer gives the ids of possible duplicates outside the caller's scope
- `src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/People/PersonService.cs:204` returns `otherPersonId` for every case it opened. The
  refusal path (`MatchAsync`, `:424-427`) hides the id of a person the caller cannot read. In practice, creators today
  have all-company scope (R-111), so the exposure is small, but it goes against CR-076 detail h.
- **Fix:** return `otherPersonId` only when `MatchAsync` found it `InScope`, and `null` otherwise.

### 7. LOW: a creator can close a possible-duplicate case without the review permission
- `PersonService.cs:166-175`: `duplicates: "distinct"` on create stores the case as `distinct`, decided by the creator. It needs
  only `hrm.persons.create`. `MarkDistinctAsync` needs `hrm.person_duplicate_cases.update`.
- Failure scenario: a data-entry role without review rights skips the review queue for every warning.
- **Fix:** accept `"distinct"` only when the caller holds `PeoplePermissions.DuplicatesUpdate`. Otherwise refuse it, or store
  the case as `open`.

### 8. LOW: case decisions check the scope of one of the two persons only
- `PersonService.Duplicates.cs:80-89` (and the listing, `:36`) apply the scope of `person_id`. The case's
  `other_person_id` may be outside it. A reviewer can mark "distinct" a pair whose second person they cannot see. The list
  hides that person's name, which is correct.
- **Fix:** also require read scope on `other_person_id` to decide a case, or rule that one side is enough.

### 9. LOW: the identifier refusal tells any creator that a CNIC exists in the customer, and the probe is not logged
- `PersonService.cs:136-142` and `Sections.cs:194-205`: an exact match refuses with `errors.hrm.identifier.duplicate`, even
  when the owner is outside the caller's scope. The refusal is required (FR-HRM-EMP-05, -18), but it lets a creator check
  whether a given CNIC belongs to someone in the customer.
- **Fix:** write an access-log entry (`hrm.person_identifiers`, owner row, action `duplicate_check`) when the owner is outside
  the caller's scope, or record the risk in the register.

### 10. LOW: deleting an address leaves a gap in the history
- `Sections.cs:298` and `:704`: a delete removes the row, and the address before it keeps `valid_to` = the deleted row's
  start date. From that date onward the person has no address of that kind, and later as-of reads (GHR-03) return none.
- **Fix:** when the deleted row was the latest of its kind, reopen the previous row (`valid_to` = the deleted row's
  `valid_to`) in the same save. Otherwise refuse the delete of a middle row and ask for a correction instead.

### 11. LOW: one failing held-change check breaks the whole approvals inbox
- `AccessAdministrationService.Pending.cs:58-62` calls `DescribeAsync` and `CheckAsync` of the module handler for each row,
  inside the list's transaction, with no exception handling. An unexpected exception in one module's handler (bad
  payload shape, a database error that aborts the transaction) answers 500 for every pending request of every kind.
- **Fix:** catch exceptions per row, roll back to a savepoint, and show the row as "cannot be checked now".

### 12. LOW: the same national number entered as a CNIC on one person and as a NICOP on another is not caught
- `PeopleCatalogue.cs:34-41` and `PersonService.Sections.cs:194-200`: the blind-index match includes the kind, so a 13-digit
  number held as `cnic` on one person and as `nicop` on another is neither refused nor warned. A NICOP normally carries its
  holder's CNIC number. This is for the pack owner to confirm (R-112).
- **Fix:** add it to R-112 or R-114 for S1.23. A pack rule could name kinds that share a number space for duplicate
  detection.

## Checked and found sound
- FR-XM-32: `DecideHeldAsync` refuses `request.RequestedBy == by` for approve and reject, on the locked request row. The
  merge applies on the kernel's approval transaction (`PersonMergeHandler.OnAsync`, `UseTransactionAsync`). `RunAsync`
  commits only on `Done`, so a refused or failed apply rolls back everything, including partial EF updates.
- The acting user on the approval path: the handler is scoped, and its `HrmDbContext` uses the request scope's options with
  `UseErpAudit(sp)`, so the change log records the approver (GHR-09). The request row keeps the requester.
- Merge concurrency: both person rows are locked `for update` in id order, the checks run again after the lock, and every
  moved row is version-checked. The partial unique index `uq_employments_person_id` backs the "one active primary
  employment" rule.
- Identifiers: field encryption and blind index (`IFieldEncryptorProvider`) with their own purpose, last four for display, a full view only with
  `.read` + `.reveal`, the field-access level of `value_last4`, and the access log written before the answer. The unique
  constraint backs the pre-check, and its refusal is translated.
- Address effective dating: `valid_to` is exclusive (`daterange(..., '[)')` exclusion constraint), and `FitAddressAsync`
  closes the covering row at the new start, with the covering row's data scope checked.
- Tenant isolation: all 23 new routes carry `HrmEndpointMetadata`, and `HrmProbe` checks 404/no-leak/unchanged/positive
  control. Tenant comes from host and token only. No `tenant_id`, no `DateTime.Now`, injected clock throughout.
- Migration: expand only, new file, FK indexes present, the one cross-schema FK (`decided_by` to `iam.users`) follows the existing hrm pattern (three earlier ones in `V202611150100`).
- Rulings: Q1 (hand-written endpoints, generated reads kept), Q3 (catalogue as data, CNIC 13 digits and dash layout only),
  Q4 (at most one active primary employment, second person when on, retired person keeps its id), Q5 (`merged_into_person_id`)
  and Q13 (no erasure) are followed. The departures are recorded as CR-076 details a to m.
