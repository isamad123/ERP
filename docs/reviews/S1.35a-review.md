**Summary for the product owner.** The engine's foundations are sound. Every amount is a decimal, never a floating-point number; each step keeps four decimal
places and each payslip line is rounded to the currency's paisa by the company's rounding mode. A whole month always gives exactly the monthly amount. The
calculation is a pure function of a frozen snapshot: the snapshot is stored compressed with a fingerprint (SHA-256), and the engine calculates from the stored
form, so the same snapshot gives the same lines. The period's attendance and leave are locked before anything is calculated, and nothing is calculated when the
lock is refused. A statutory component (tax, contributions) is never worked out by this part: without its calculator the employee is "incomplete", gets no
result, and the run does not finish, so no one is silently paid with zero tax. Once a run is locked the database refuses to change or delete its results. Amounts
are hidden from anyone without the salary field class in the new endpoints, the generated lists and the change log. No statutory rate, slab or threshold is in
the code. Two runs of one calculation at once cannot write the same employee twice.

One high point should be fixed before the merge:
- **Day-rate and hour-rate pay is cut twice for joiners, leavers and unpaid leave.** A per-day component already pays "rate times paid days"; if it is also given
  a pro-rata rule (which the component screen allows), the engine cuts it again by the days employed. I reproduced it: a joiner on 16 January on 300 a day is
  paid 4,800 with the rule "none" but 2,477.42 with the rule "calendar days". With "none", a mid-month change of the day rate pays all days at the new rate.

Five medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- Payroll can lock attendance before the period's cut-off date (HR cannot), which freezes days not yet worked.
- An employee moved to another pay group in mid-month has the whole month's worked days, hours, overtime and lateness counted in both runs.
- Three small gaps in the database guard of locked runs (emptying a table with TRUNCATE, the employees' copy of the run status, the stage log).
- A payroll officer can cancel a deduction someone else entered on their own pay, and a cancel at the moment of calculation can leave a paid input marked cancelled.
- The calculation conventions the specification leaves open (R-175) include two that change pay in ordinary cases and are not listed: absent days are paid days,
  and the 30-day rule pays nothing to someone who joins on the 31st and 29 of 30 days to someone who joins on the 2nd of a 31-day month. They need your
  ruling (and the payroll practitioner's) in plain words before go-live; they do not block this merge if recorded.

The seven low points can wait or be ruled on.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: per-day, per-hour and attendance-linked components (and formulas over day counts) are pro-rated a second time (Build item 5, FR-HRM-PAY-03, -05)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Application/Payroll/PayEngine.cs` lines 470 to 484 (`per_day` = rate × `$paid_days`, `per_hour` = rate × `$worked_hours`,
`attendance_linked` = rate × `$worked_days` / `$working_days`) and lines 498 to 547 (`ProRata`). The three methods already turn the employee's days into the
amount: `$paid_days` is the employed days less unpaid leave (lines 133 to 137), `$worked_days` and `$worked_hours` come from the attendance results. The value
is then stored as the segment's "full-period value" and every rule other than `none` multiplies it again by the share of the period employed and not on unpaid
leave. `ComponentRules.CheckProRata` (S1.34b) accepts any rule for any method, so the configuration is valid and nothing warns. The same applies to a
`formula` that already uses `$paid_days`, `$worked_days`, `$employed_days`, `$unpaid_leave_days` or `$absent_days` (for example `BASIC / $period_days * $paid_days`).
With `none`, the value is the last segment's (line 502), so after a mid-month change of the day rate every paid day of the month is paid at the new rate.

Failure scenario (reproduced, R1): a daily-wage worker on 300 a day joins on 16 January. The administrator gives the component the pro-rata rule
"calendar days", as for the other components of the structure. The engine pays 300 × 16 × 16 / 31 = 2,477.42 instead of 4,800. Every joiner, leaver and day of
unpaid leave of that component is underpaid the same way (an unpaid day is deducted twice). Second scenario: the day rate rises from 300 to 350 on 16 January
with the rule `none`: all 31 paid days are paid at 350 (10,850) instead of 15 × 300 + 16 × 350 (10,100).

Suggested fix: for the methods whose value is already a quantity of days or hours (`per_day`, `per_hour`, `attendance_linked`), do not apply the pro-rata rule:
either refuse every rule but `none` for them (in `ComponentRules.CheckProRata`, called by S1.34b's version service, and in the engine as a problem
`errors.hrm.pay.calc.pro_rata_not_applicable`, so an existing version cannot slip through) or ignore the rule and say so in the trace. For a rate change inside the
period, weigh `per_day` by each segment's own paid days (the employed days of the segment less its unpaid leave; the engine has both by date) instead of taking
the last segment's rate; `per_hour` and `attendance_linked` need dated hours and days from the attendance provider to do the same, or a recorded convention
(R-175) that the rate in force at the period end applies. For a formula, at least warn at definition time (`IFormulaValidator`) when it uses a day-count input and
its version has a pro-rata rule other than `none`. Add unit tests: a joiner and a leaver on a `per_day` component, a rate change mid-month, and the refusal.

### 2. MEDIUM: a payroll calculation locks attendance before the period's cut-off date (Build item 2, FR-XM-26)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Payroll/Engine/PayRunService.cs` lines 61 to 145 (`CreateAsync` only refuses a closed period) and 147 to 179
(`CalculateAsync` calls `IAttendancePeriodLock.LockAsync` with any date). `AttendancePeriodLocks.LockOnAsync` (S1.31) has no date check of its own; the HR lock
does (`AttendanceLockService` line 136: refused while the company's date is on or before `cut_off_date`). With `acceptOpenItems` the unprocessed future days
are simply counted and accepted.

Failure scenario: on 5 November a payroll officer creates and calculates the November run (perhaps to preview it), accepting the open items. November's
attendance is now locked: the database refuses day results, penalties, overtime and leave usage for 6 to 30 November (S1.31 rules), so the attendance engine
cannot process the rest of the month and approved leave cannot be counted. Undoing it needs the run cancelled and a reopen approved by a second person
(CR-095), and meanwhile everyone's November attendance is stuck.

Suggested fix: refuse `POST /api/hrm/pay-runs/{id}/calculate` (key e.g. `errors.hrm.pay.run.before_cut_off`, with `cutOffDate`) while the company's date
(`EmploymentApplier.TodayAsync`) is on or before the pay period's `cut_off_date`, the same rule as the HR lock. If customers must preview a run before the
cut-off, that is a calculation without the lock, which FR-XM-26 does not allow; leave it to S1.38 and its ruling. Add an integration test.

### 3. MEDIUM: after a mid-period move to another pay group, worked days, hours, overtime, lateness and leave paid out count in both runs (Build item 1, FR-HRM-PAY-07)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Payroll/Engine/PayInputProviders.cs` lines 35 to 45 (attendance days of the whole period), 54 to 58 (encashments),
87 to 91 (overtime), 112 to 116 (lateness): every provider reads by the period's dates only and ignores `PayInputRequest.PayGroupId`. The engine limits unpaid
leave to the days employed in the run's pay group (`PayEngine.cs` line 127) but adds every other named quantity as it comes (lines 124 to 126). The snapshot
builder (`PaySnapshotBuilder.Employed`) and the exclusion constraint of `hrm.pay_run_employees` are built for exactly this case (each run pays only its own days).

Failure scenario: an employee is in pay group A from 1 to 14 November and in group B from 15 November (an assignment change; both groups monthly). Run A pays
1 to 14 November and run B 15 to 30 November, correctly for the fixed components. But both snapshots hold all November's attendance: each run sees the month's
20 worked days and 160 hours, all 12 approved overtime hours and all lateness penalties, so overtime formulas pay 12 hours twice, an attendance-linked
allowance is paid in full twice (before its own pro-rata), and lateness is deducted twice.

Suggested fix: in each provider (or once in the engine, which has the employed days), keep only the items dated on a day employed in the run's pay group:
give `PayInputItem` the date for attendance quantities (one item per day, or per day type and day), and filter dated items with `employed.Contains(date)` as
for unpaid leave. Record it in the provider contract (contracts register): a dated quantity counts only on the run's days. Add a unit test with two employed
ranges and dated overtime on both sides of the move.

### 4. MEDIUM: three gaps in the database guard of locked runs (Build item 7, FR-HRM-PAY-33, FR-XM-05; Acceptance "refused by the database for every login")

`tools/Erp.CodeGen/rules.yaml` lines 46 to 184 (S1.35a entries; migration `V202611150233__hrm__rules_s1_35a.sql`).
- (a) **TRUNCATE.** Row triggers do not fire on `TRUNCATE`. Append-only tables get a statement trigger `tg_<table>_no_truncate` from the generator
  (`SqlEmitter.cs` line 599, for example `hrm.pay_result_reversals`), but the five tables guarded here are not append-only in the design, so none was added.
  `truncate hrm.pay_result_lines` (or `truncate hrm.pay_results cascade`) by the owner login removes every locked result. Since a run's tables only grow or are
  rewritten row by row by the engine, a truncate guard on all five costs nothing.
- (b) **`run_status` is free after the lock.** `hrm.tg_pay_run_results_lock` lets `run_status` of a locked run's employees take any value (line 136,
  `v_free`). The exclusion constraint `ex_pay_run_employees_employment_id_period` ignores rows with `run_status = 'cancelled'`, so
  `update hrm.pay_run_employees set run_status = 'cancelled' where pay_run_id = <locked run>` is accepted and frees the employees' days: a regular run of
  another pay group (after a backdated move) or a final settlement run could then pay the same days again. The copy should only follow the run: require `new.run_status = v_status` (the run's status,
  already read `for share`) when the run is final.
- (c) **The stage log can be rewritten after the lock.** `hrm.tg_pay_runs_lock` frees `stage_log` (line 62), so a locked run's history of who calculated,
  approved and locked it (FR-HRM-PAY-10) can be replaced. Require that the new log starts with the old one (for example
  `jsonb_array_length(new.stage_log) >= jsonb_array_length(old.stage_log)` and the first elements equal, compared with a lateral `jsonb_array_elements ... with ordinality`).

Failure scenario: (b) as above: an operator "tidies" a payroll by SQL or a later service sets the copy wrongly; the guard that should make a paid month
untouchable lets the same days be paid by another regular run, and nothing in the locked run's own rows shows it.

Suggested fix: as in each point; a new rules.yaml entry (the merged files stay frozen only after merge, so this migration may still be regenerated). Extend
`FR_HRM_PAY_33_FR_XM_05_the_database_refuses_changes_…` with `truncate` of each table, `run_status = 'cancelled'` and a rewritten stage log.

### 5. MEDIUM: cancelling a one-time input has no own-pay check, and a cancel can pass a calculation that uses the input (Build item 1, FR-XM-32, FR-HRM-PAY-07)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Payroll/Engine/PayInputService.cs` lines 233 to 258 (`CancelAsync`) and `PayInputProviders.cs` lines 184 to 200
(`PayInputRowsProvider.ConsumedAsync`).
- `AddAsync` refuses an input to one's own pay (line 105), but `CancelAsync` does not: a payroll officer can cancel a deduction (a fine, an advance recovery
  entered as a one-time deduction) that a colleague entered on the officer's own pay. That is acting on one's own pay, which the service's own rule (and
  FR-XM-32's intent) forbids.
- `CancelAsync` reads and saves without a lock; `ConsumedAsync` updates only rows still `pending` and does not check that it found them all. If the cancel
  commits after the chunk read the input (`ForAsync`) and before `ConsumedAsync`, the result includes the amount while the row stays `cancelled`; the bonus is
  paid, the input list says it was cancelled, and the same bonus can be entered again for a later period and paid twice.

Failure scenario: HR enters a 20,000 bonus for an employee in the November run; the payroll manager starts the calculation; at that moment another officer
cancels the bonus (mistakenly thinking it a duplicate). The employee is paid the 20,000; the input shows "cancelled"; HR enters it again in December.

Suggested fix: in `CancelAsync` refuse the employee's own employment (`employment.UserRef == user`, key `errors.hrm.pay.input.own_pay`). In `ConsumedAsync`
select the rows `for update` and throw (rolling back the chunk; the employee is calculated again on the next call) when the number found differs from the
number used; in `CancelAsync` take the row `for update` too (or rely on `row_version` as a concurrency token and check it is configured so). Tests for both.

### 6. MEDIUM: the open calculation conventions (CR-093 detail f to h, R-175) include two with ordinary money effects that are not listed (FR-HRM-PAY-05, -07)

`PayEngine.cs` lines 133 to 137 (`$paid_days` = employed days less unpaid leave) and 526 to 537 (the pro-rata formula); `PayInputProviders.cs` lines 41 to 43
and 65 to 71 (absent days are a named quantity only).
- **Absent days are paid days.** A day the attendance engine marks `absent` (no punch, no leave) does not reduce `$paid_days` or any pro-rata: a monthly salary
  is paid in full for 10 days absent without leave, and a `per_day` worker is paid for them, unless the customer writes a deduction formula over `$absent_days`.
  The specification says leave without pay flows to payroll (FR-HRM-LVE-07) and is silent on unauthorised absence; this is a convention with a large default
  effect, and R-175 does not mention it.
- **The deduction form of the 30-day and custom divisors.** The formula full × (counted days × divisor − missing days × counted days) / (counted days ×
  divisor) is the "deduct missing days at monthly / 30" form. Reproduced (R2): a joiner on 31 January is paid nothing for the day worked; a joiner on 2 January
  (30 days employed) is paid 29,000 of 30,000; in February a joiner employed 1 day is paid 3 days. Many Pakistani payrolls use the "pay employed days at
  monthly / 30, capped at 30" form instead, which gives 1,000, 30,000 and 1,000. R-175 names "the deduction form" but not these outcomes.

Judgement asked for (do f to h invent a business rule the specification forbids inventing?): the specification names the methods and rules (FR-HRM-PAY-03, -05)
but not their arithmetic, so some convention had to be chosen; CR-083 lets a recommendation recorded in a change request stand as the ruling, and CR-093 did so.
Most choices in f to h are the natural reading of the names (fixed, percent of, per day, per hour, the calendar and working day rules; a whole month exact).
Four are genuine inventions with money effect: the deduction form above, `none` taking the later amount after a mid-month change, the table lookup on the
full-period earnings before it, and absent days as paid. CLAUDE.md asks to stop when the specification is silent; recording them openly, keeping them out of
statutory values and requiring a practitioner review before go-live (R-175) is an acceptable way through for an engine that is not yet live, provided R-175 lists
every one in plain words with a worked example, so the product owner and the practitioner can rule on numbers rather than on formulas.

Suggested fix: extend R-175 (and the handoff) with a short plain-language table: each convention, one worked example, the alternative. Ask the product owner
now whether the 30-day rule should be the deduction or the payment form and whether absent days should be unpaid by default (it may become a flag of the
`attendance` policy, like `CountsAsPresent`); the answer is a new engine version, not an edit (as R-175 says). No change of code is needed for this merge if
the ruling waits, but finding 1 must be fixed whatever the ruling.

### 7. LOW: a formula or percentage over a component that is not in the employee's structure, or not yet worked out, gives 0 silently (Build item 5)

`PayEngine.cs` line 402 (`percent_of` bases) and line 430 (formula component values): a base or code that has no value in the segment counts as 0. The
structure planner checks the order of evaluation when the structure is saved, but a later component version (a changed formula or base) is not re-checked
against the structures that hold it, and a component missing from one employee's structure is simply 0. The slice's principle is "never silently zero".
Suggested fix: make a reference to a component that is in the structure but has no value yet a problem (`errors.hrm.pay.calc.order`), and at least trace a
reference to a component outside the structure; re-check structures when a version changes its formula or bases (S1.34b's planner).

### 8. LOW: a one-time input added for an employee already calculated in an open run is accepted and never paid by that run (Build item 1, R-176)

`PayInputService.cs` lines 123 to 127 refuse only a locked run; `PayRunService.cs` lines 183 to 184 skip calculated employees, and the input's
`pay_period_id` ties it to this period. The input waits `pending` for S1.35b's recalculation (R-176), but the API gives no sign. If S1.35b or S1.38 do not pick
it up, the period is locked with the input pending and it is never paid. Suggested fix: answer such an input with a warning (or refuse it until recalculation
exists), and make "pending inputs of a calculated employee" a validation flag S1.38 must clear before the lock.

### 9. LOW: the run's finish never compares with the employees it should pay (Build items 5, 6)

`PayRunService.cs` lines 355 to 364: the run becomes `calculated` when no employee row is `error`. An employee in `error` who later stops being eligible (a
backdated assignment to another group, a cancelled employment) keeps the run from ever finishing; a `calculated` employee who stops being eligible keeps a
result in the run. Suggested fix: in `FinishAsync`, compare the `calculated` employees with `EligibleAsync` and list the differences (S1.38 can then exclude or
recalculate); a stage-log entry "calculation_incomplete" with `incomplete = 0` after a parallel call already finished the run is also misleading (skip it when
the run is no longer calculable).

### 10. LOW: write actions check the data scope of "read" (Build items 1, 2)

`PayRunService.cs` lines 87 and 156 and `PayInputService.cs` lines 99 and 241 ask `IDataScopeFilter.AllowsAsync(…, "read", …)` before creating a run,
calculating it, adding an input and cancelling it. A role whose read scope is wider than its write scope (read all companies, write one) could act outside its
write scope. The baseline roles hold `all` for both, so nothing is open today. Suggested fix: ask `"create"` (run, input) and `"update"` (calculate, cancel).

### 11. LOW: the rounding mode and the currency's decimals are read at every calculation call, not frozen for the run (Build item 5, ruling Q3)

`PaySnapshotBuilder.cs` lines 33 to 35: each call to `calculate` reads `hrm.pay.rounding_mode` and `ref.currencies.decimals` again, and an unknown currency falls
back to 2 places. If the setting changes between two calls of a run that stopped part-way (an incomplete employee), employees of one run are rounded by
different modes. Ruling Q3 says "mode in the pay group setting"; CR-093 detail i records the company layer instead. Suggested fix: store the mode and decimals
on the run (or in its stage log) at the first calculation and reuse them; refuse a currency without a `ref.currencies` row instead of assuming 2.

### 12. LOW: an arithmetic overflow outside a formula fails the whole batch (Build item 5)

`PayEngine.cs` lines 405, 467, 471 to 482 and 533: only `FormulaLanguage.Evaluate` turns `OverflowException` into a problem. An absurd amount (a typing error of
many digits in a compensation line or an input) makes the percent, table, per-day or pro-rata arithmetic throw, the request answers 500 and every employee of
the batch stays uncalculated. Suggested fix: catch `OverflowException` in `Calculate` and return the employee as incomplete with
`errors.hrm.pay.formula_overflow` (or a calc key).

  generated writes. But pay results themselves now have only a raw-encryption check (`GHR_06_…`); the acceptance names audit and export. Add to that test a

- Deferring the import-centre template (R-174) is acceptable for part a: the validated, all-or-none bulk endpoint exists, the import centre's transaction is

## Checked and found right

- **Decimal only.** No `double` or `float` in the engine, the parser, the snapshot or the services; literals are parsed with `decimal.TryParse` (invariant);
  every binary operation rounds half away from zero to four places (`PayRounding.Internal`), `round()` and the lines use the run's mode; a whole month gives the
  full amount at every rule (unit test, and by the formula: one segment, no missing days, numerator = denominator). Segments: a change of compensation,
  structure row or version inside the employed days starts a segment; end dates are exclusive (`CompensationPlanner` line 183 sets the old row's end to the new
  start) and the engine treats them so; joiners and leavers are the first and last segment.
- **Determinism (PAY-31).** `PayEngine.Calculate` reads only the snapshot and the calculators (no clock, no database); the calculator chosen does not depend on
  registration order (exactly one must claim a component); dictionaries are filled in a fixed order (codes, then provider and source id). The canonical JSON
  sorts every object's keys recursively (statutory frozen data and cost allocations too), the builder sorts every list (inputs by provider, source, id, name,
  date, component; lines by component; assignments by date and id), numbers keep their text, and the stored form is what is calculated (`PayRunService` lines
  236 to 238). The integration test recalculates the stored snapshot to the stored result and checks the hash.
- **Lock first (FR-XM-26) and transactions.** The lock is taken in its own committed transaction before any snapshot; a refused lock writes no employee
  (tested). Each batch runs in one transaction holding the run row `for update`, re-reads the batch's employee rows and skips calculated ones, so two
  calculations at once never write one employee twice; the guard trigger reads the run `for share`, so a write and the run's lock never pass each other; a
  failed batch rolls back whole and the next call goes on; the run becomes `calculated` and the event is written in one transaction with the totals.
- **Statutory plug-in point (Q2, AS-4).** A statutory component is never worked out by the engine: none, more than one calculator, a problem, no amount or a
  negative amount make the employee incomplete, with no result row (tested across two calls); inputs on statutory components and formulas or percentages over
  them are refused. No statutory rate, slab or threshold in the code or the migration.
- **Guard triggers** (apart from finding 4): a locked run's results, lines, employees and traces are refused for insert, update and delete; a result cannot be
  moved out of a locked run nor into one; a locked run is never deleted, never moves back (tested `locked` → `calculated` and `paid` → `locked`) and keeps every
  column but its status and log; a correction is a new result in a later run.
- **GHR-06.** `amount` and `rate` of lines, the six result amounts and the snapshot are salary-class; `/results` shows them per row through the field plan; the
  generated lists hide them (tested); the event and the stage log carry ids and counts only; run totals stay unclassified by CR-018 ruling B (R-176).
- **Permissions, scope, isolation.** Each of the seven endpoints checks its permission first (`hrm.pay.run`, `hrm.pay_runs.read`, `hrm.pay.results.read`,
  `hrm.pay_inputs.create`, `.cancel`, `hrm.pay_components.read`) and the company scope; another company's officer gets 404 or 403 (tested); the generated writes of the
  six tables are closed (tested); the seven routes are in `HrmProbe`.
- **One-time inputs.** All or none, at most 1,000; employment in scope, not one's own; period of the employee's pay group, not closed, run not locked; an active,
  non-statutory, one-time or manual component with a version; an amount needs edit access to the salary class; the pay group's currency; consumed by the run
  in the batch's transaction.
- **Tests.** Every new test names its FR or ruling; expected values in the integration and method tests are worked out in the test from the inputs; the
  S1.34b tests changed only the input names of their formulas (now from the catalogue) with the same assertions; `GeneratorTests` counts the five implemented
  placeholders and names them; R-319's fix (company date in the exception queue) is small and right.

## Response
