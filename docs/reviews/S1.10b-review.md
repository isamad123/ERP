## Summary for the product owner

Overall the second part of the approval engine is solid:
- Nobody can approve their own request. I checked every way in: the inbox, a role queue, a delegation, a reassignment,
  an escalation and an administrator's reassignment. Each one refuses the requester and the people the request is about.
- A late request moves up a level and is never approved by itself. Two escalation runs at the same moment cannot move the
  same task twice, and an escalation and an approval of the same request run one after the other.
- Delegations are not passed on, and a delegate decides with the rights of the person they stand in for, not their own.
- Time limits count working days correctly, including your own example (arrives Thursday, weekend and a Monday holiday,
  late from Wednesday).
- Every customer's data stays in its own database, and every SQL statement uses parameters.

I found **no serious (high) defect**. I found **three medium defects**. I proved each one with a test on the real
database:

1. **A disabled person's approval rights keep working through their delegate.** Example: Sara hands her approvals to
   Ali for her leave. Then Sara is dismissed and her account is disabled. Ali can still approve Sara's tasks with Sara's
   rights, until the last day of the delegation (up to 90 days).
2. **An administrator who manages delegations can give themselves anyone's approval rights.** Someone with the right to
   create delegations for others can create one "from the finance approver, to me" and then approve requests they were
   never allowed to approve. Nothing asks a second person to agree. **This needs your decision** (see finding 2).
3. **After an escalation to a role, the late approver can still decide.** If the step's escalation goes to a role that
   the late approver also holds, the late approver can pick the task up again from that role's queue and approve it.
   The slice's acceptance rule says the original approver "can no longer decide it".

There are also six low items: small gaps, one rare database lock conflict, and numbers that can disagree on screen.

I recommend fixing all three medium items before HR approvals (leave, S1.20 onwards) go live. Each needs a few lines of
code and a test. Finding 2 needs your choice first.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. MEDIUM: a delegate keeps deciding with a disabled or archived user's rights

- **Where:**
  - `WorkflowEngine.cs:712-721` (`DelegationCovers`, `DelegatedAsync`): the delegation is honoured without looking at the
    delegator's status.
  - `WorkflowEngine.cs:724-726` (`CanDecideAsync`) → `ApproverResolution.cs:124-128` (`HoldsAsync`) →
    `AccessSnapshot.LoadAsync`: the delegator's rights are read from their role assignments, which a disabled account keeps.
  - `WorkflowEngine.Reading.cs:31` (inbox): the delegator's tasks stay in the delegate's inbox.
  - `AccountServices.cs:326-334`: disabling or archiving counts the open tasks (P7) but leaves delegations in force.
- **Scenario:** Sara (approver) delegates to Ali for five days. An administrator disables Sara ("dismissed"). Ali still
  sees Sara's task as "for Sara" and approves it; the request ends approved, decided with the rights of a disabled account.
  This lasts until the delegation's last day, up to `wf.delegation.max_days` (90). Sara's own sessions were revoked, so
  her rights are used by someone else after the organisation took them away.
- **Proof:** `Proof_1_a_disabled_delegators_rights_are_still_used_by_the_delegate`: disable answers 200, the task is in Ali's
  inbox, Ali's approve answers 200, the request is `approved`.
- **Fix (recommended):** a delegation applies only while the delegator is an active user: add
  `and exists (select 1 from iam.users u where u.id = dl.from_user_id and u.status = 'active' and not u.is_deleted)` to
  `DelegationCovers` (used by both the inbox and decisions). Their open tasks then go the ADM-USR-03 way: the
  administrator reassigns them (the disable answer already reports how many), and escalation still moves them up when
  late. No data change, no change request.
- **Test to add:** the proof turned round: after disabling Sara, Ali's inbox lacks the task and approve is refused (404);
  after re-activating Sara inside the dates, it works again.

### 2. MEDIUM (needs a product-owner decision): an administrator of delegations can give themselves another person's right to decide

- **Where:** `DelegationService.cs:78-93` (`CreateCoreAsync`, administrator path): the delegate may be the administrator.
  `WorkflowContracts.cs:206`: `iam.delegations.create` is not a sensitive permission, so no second person approves.
- **Scenario:** Mallory holds a custom role with `iam.delegations.create` (data scope "all"), `wf.workflow_tasks.read` and
  `wf.workflow_tasks.update`, but not the right a payroll or "guarded" document type requires. A request waits for Sara.
  Mallory posts `POST /api/iam/delegations {fromUserId: Sara, toUserId: Mallory}` and then approves Sara's task with
  Sara's rights. This gets round S1.06's rules: "cannot grant what you do not hold" and maker-checker for sensitive access
  changes. The delegation is in the change log, so it is traceable, but nobody had to agree.
- **Proof:** `Proof_3_a_delegations_administrator_hands_themselves_another_users_decide_right`: Mallory's approve is 404
  before, the delegation is created (201), and Mallory's approve is then 200 on a document type whose decide permission
  (`org.companies.update`) Mallory does not hold.
- **The specification** (Q8, ADM-ROL-09) says "an administrator with the permission creates one for anyone". It does not
  say whether the administrator may name themselves, or whether creating one for others needs a second person.
- **Options:**
  - **(a) Recommended:** in the administrator path, refuse a delegation whose delegate is the caller
    (`errors.delegation.to_self_admin`); an administrator who needs to cover a colleague asks another administrator, or
    the colleague delegates themselves. A code rule only, no change request.
  - **(b)** As (a), and in addition creating a delegation **for someone else** goes through maker-checker (the S1.06
    mechanism, a new action type). This changes the permission catalogue and a contract, so it needs change request
    CR-060 and more work.
  - **(c)** Accept as it is, and record it as a risk (R-052).

### 3. MEDIUM: after escalation to a role, the late approver can claim and decide the escalated task

- **Where:** `WorkflowEngine.Escalation.cs:110-116` and `:147`: the late assignee is removed from the people counted,
  but the new task is a role-queue task. `WorkflowEngine.cs:701-704` (`CapacityAsync`, queue path), `:468`
  (`ClaimAsync`) and `WorkflowEngine.Reading.cs:29-30` (inbox queue rows) accept any eligible holder of the role,
  including the person whose task was just escalated.
- **Scenario:** a step is "Bob, escalation: role Finance managers". Bob holds that role too. Bob lets the task run late; it
  is escalated to the Finance managers queue. Bob sees the queue task in his inbox, claims it and approves it. The
  acceptance rule "the original assignee can no longer decide it" (slice S1.10, Acceptance, escalation) is broken, and
  the escalation achieved nothing. With a manager or a named user as escalation target this cannot happen, because the
  code already leaves Bob out (`p != assignee`).
- **Proof:** `Proof_2_the_late_assignee_claims_and_decides_the_task_escalated_to_a_role_they_hold`: after the escalation run
  the late task is `escalated`, the new queue task is in Bob's inbox, and Bob's approve answers 200 (request `approved`).
- **Fix (recommended):** a person whose task on a step was escalated may not claim or decide a role-queue task of the
  same step of the same request. One check shared by the inbox queue rows, `CapacityAsync` and `ClaimAsync`:
  `not exists (select 1 from wf.workflow_tasks e where e.instance_id = t.instance_id and e.step_key = t.step_key and
  e.status = 'escalated' and e.assignee_user_id = <user>)`. Read-time only, no schema change. An administrator can still
  reassign the task to Bob on purpose (the reassign path names a person; it is not a queue).
- **Test to add:** the proof turned round: Bob does not see the queue task, his claim and approve are refused (404),
  and Carl (another holder) claims and approves it.

### 4. LOW: escalation does not check that the next level may decide

- **Where:** `WorkflowEngine.Escalation.cs:104-122`. Reassignment checks `CanDecideAsync` for its target
  (`WorkflowEngine.cs:386`); escalation does not.
- **Effect:** a manager without `wf.workflow_tasks.update` or the document type's decide permission gets the task, cannot
  decide it, and the request waits another time limit before it moves up again. Nothing is approved wrongly; it is only slow.
- **Fix:** keep only the people found who `CanDecideAsync`; when none remain, try the step's escalation target, else
  "nobody above" (overdue for administrators).

### 5. LOW: a reassignment can give one person two open tasks of the same step

- **Where:** `WorkflowEngine.cs:363-395` (`ReassignCoreAsync`), also used by reassign-all.
- **Effect:** with several holders on one step (ruling R1), reassigning Ann's task to Ben, who already holds a task of
  that step, gives Ben two inbox rows for one request. The first decision closes the other, so the outcome is right; it
  is confusing on screen. Escalation already avoids this (`WorkflowEngine.Escalation.cs:124-131`).
- **Fix:** refuse (`errors.workflow.reassign_target_has_task`), or close the reassigned task without opening a second
  one for Ben.

### 6. LOW: withdraw or cancel of a returned request can deadlock with the module's resubmit

- **Where:** `ResubmitAsync` (`WorkflowEngine.cs:119-161`) runs inside the module's save: the module locks its document,
  then the engine locks the request (`WorkflowDb.cs:114`, `for update`). `WithdrawAsync` (`WorkflowEngine.cs:445`) and
  `CancelAsync` (`WorkflowEngine.Monitor.cs:126`, new in S1.10b) lock the request first and then call the module's
  outcome handler, which updates the document.
- **Effect:** if a requester resubmits at the same moment an administrator cancels the same returned request (or the
  requester withdraws it from a second tab), PostgreSQL ends one of the two with a deadlock error, which the caller sees
  as a server error. No data is damaged; the person tries again. Not reproduced: no 1.0 module resubmits yet.
- **Fix:** document the lock order for module authors in the `IWorkflowService` contract (lock the document, then call
  the engine); in `WithdrawAsync` and `CancelAsync`, ask the module to lock its document before the engine locks the
  request (a `LockDocumentAsync` member on the outcome handler), or retry once on deadlock (40P01). The real test comes
  with the first module that resubmits (S1.21 leave).

### 7. LOW: the "open tasks" number on disabling a user can disagree with the list

- **Where:** `AccountServices.cs:327-333` counts every pending task of the user, in every company and every document type.
  `GET /api/wf/users/{id}/open-tasks` (`WorkflowEngine.Monitor.cs:161-192`) lists only the administrator's companies and
  licensed document types.
- **Effect:** a Company Admin can be told "3 open tasks" and then shown 1. It also tells them that tasks exist in other
  companies.
- **Fix:** count with the same rules as the list, for the caller.

### 8. LOW: "my requests" shows the same question for every waiting task

- **Where:** `WorkflowEngine.Reading.cs:202-205` and `:217-220` (`LastQuestionAsync` reads the last question of the whole
  request).
- **Effect:** when two approvers of one step (ruling R1) each ask a different question, the requester sees the last
  question twice and never sees the first.
- **Fix:** read the last `info_requested` history row of each task (`task_id = $2`), falling back to the request's last
  question for a task that inherited one (ruling P6).

### 9. LOW: a test promises a check it does not make

- **Where:** `InboxAndAdministrationTests.cs:244-248`. The comment says "the requester cannot receive their own request's
  task: refused for that task, the others go on", but the test reassigns to a non-excluded successor and expects
  `done = 2`. Reassign-all to an excluded person is not tested. The code refuses it correctly (`WorkflowEngine.cs:376-379`),
  but a regression would go unnoticed.
- **Fix:** add a third request raised by the successor, expect `done = 2, refused = 1` with `errors.workflow.assignee_excluded`.

## Checked and found correct

- **Tenant isolation:** every query uses the request's tenant data source or the job's bound scope; the escalation
  runner binds each customer's own database and licence and skips customers not in full access (CR-044 (4)). The
  isolation probe covers every new endpoint (claim, release, bulk, monitor, cancel, open tasks, reassign-all, delegations);
  the suite is green.
- **SQL safety:** all values are parameters. The dynamic parts of the inbox and monitor queries append fixed clauses with
  numbered placeholders only (`Filter` helpers); `DelegationCovers` interpolates only placeholder names and column names
  chosen in code.
- **Nobody approves their own request (FR-XM-32, Q6):** the requester and subjects are excluded in the inbox
  (`Reading.cs:27`), `CapacityAsync` (`WorkflowEngine.cs:686`), `ClaimAsync` (`:468`), reassignment targets (`:376`),
  escalation targets (`Escalation.cs:108`, `:113`), administrator reassign and cancel (`WorkflowEngine.cs:597`,
  `Monitor.cs:144`). The S1.10a database trigger still guards assignees.
- **Never auto-approve:** escalation only marks tasks `escalated` and opens new ones; "nobody above" and "last level"
  leave the task pending and overdue, handled once (`escalated_at`).
- **Concurrency:** decisions, claim, release, reassign-all and escalation all lock the request, then the task, in that
  order; the second of two claims gets 409; two escalation runs cannot escalate the same task twice; delegation creation
  is serialised per delegator (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`), so overlapping delegations cannot slip in side by side.
- **Delegation (Q8, P2, P5):** not passed on (only `from = assignee` is matched); unclaimed queue tasks are not delegated;
  the delegate decides with the delegator's rights (`CanDecideAsync(actingFor)`), proven by the existing guarded-type
  test; "acting for" is on the task, the history and the outcome; whole days in the customer's time zone; at most
  `wf.delegation.max_days`; revocation acts at once; create and revoke are in the change log.
- **Administrator delegations (CR-044 (2)):** need a grant whose data scope for `iam.delegations` is "all". A company-limited
  assignment of such a grant also acts for people of other companies; this matches the access engine's rule for tables
  without a company column ("the permission decides").
- **Working calendar (P3, P4, Q16):** the P3 example is a unit test and an end-to-end test; only public and religious
  holidays count; a week of seven off days cannot hang the search; daylight-saving midnights are handled; the settings
  are validated (1 to 365 days, at most six off days).
- **Bulk approval (Q12):** each task in its own transaction, at most `wf.bulk_approval.max_items`, the type and the step
  must both allow it, one result per task.
- **Licensing (Q11):** inbox, counts, monitor, my requests and escalation skip unlicensed document types; FR-XM-29's
  query deliberately counts them all.
- **Migrations and generated code:** one new expand-only migration; frozen files untouched; generator drift 0.
- **Rulings P1 to P7 and CR-044 (1) to (7)** are implemented as recorded (P7: 200 with `openWorkflowTasks` for disabled
  and archived only).
