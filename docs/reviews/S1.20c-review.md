**Summary for the product owner.** This part is careful work. A transfer between companies ends the first employment and starts
a linked one with a new employee number, in one transaction. The three carry-over choices are recorded on both events. When
transfers need approval, the transfer waits for the approval route, and the person who asked cannot approve it. A rejection cancels
both sides. A future transfer is finished on its date by the daily job. The database rule "one current main employment per
person" holds in every path I traced: at once, on a future date, and after an approval. The generator change leaves the frozen
migrations untouched. The whole test suite passes.

One high point must be fixed before the merge:
- **a plain "data correction" can move a transfer's date in one company only.** HR can correct the start of the new employment to
  another date. This needs only the ordinary change permission, and no approval. The first employment still ends on the old date.
  I reproduced this: the person then had job details in both companies on the same days, which later payroll would pay twice.

Two medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- a future transfer copies an open probation at once and leaves the first one open, so managers are reminded twice;
- the contract and probation endpoints have almost no permission, data-scope or field-security tests, which the slice's
  acceptance list asks for.

The eight low points can wait.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: a data correction of the linked start moves the transfer in one company only, without the transfer's permission, approval or checks (FR-XM-30, FR-XM-32, FR-XM-05)
- The transfer's starting event on the linked employment is an ordinary `hire` event, with reason `transfer_in`
  (`EmploymentService.Moves.cs:286-301`). The change service refuses corrections of a `separation`
  (`EmploymentService.Changes.cs:140-143`), but it accepts a `data_correction` of this hire like any other. A correction whose
  root kind is `hire` may move the date earlier than the joining date (`:194`). `ApplyEmploymentFieldsAsync` then moves the joining
  date, and also the service start date when it equalled the joining date (`EmploymentApplier.cs:146-155`). The correction's
  `corrects_kind` is `hire`, which is never in `hrm.employment_events.approval_kinds`, so it applies at once. It needs only
  `hrm.employment_events.create` in the receiving company, not `hrm.employments.transfer`. The first employment's separation event
  cannot be corrected, so its end stays where it was. An untargeted correction can likewise set `service_start_date`
  (`EmploymentService.Changes.cs:394-414`), which silently undoes a recorded `service: restart` or `keep` choice.
- Failure scenario (reproduced, see Evidence): approval of transfers is on in both companies. An employee moves from company A to
  company B on the 1st of the month, approved by a second HR administrator. Later, an HR officer in B who does not hold the transfer
  permission records a data correction of the `transfer_in` hire dated 30 days earlier. It applies at once with nobody else
  involved. B's employment now starts a month before A's ends. Both legal entities hold job details, and later both payrolls, for
  the same days, and `hrm.employee_changed` tells later modules to recalculate B from the earlier date. A correction to a later date
  (up to today) leaves a gap instead, in which the person is employed by neither company. FR-XM-30 ("end one employment, start a
  linked one") no longer holds, and the approval the customer configured for transfers is bypassed.
- **Fix:** treat the start of a transfer like the end: refuse a `data_correction` whose target (or root, through
  `CorrectsEventId`) is a `hire` with `changes.transfer` (reason `transfer_in`), with the same `CorrectionTarget` answer. A wrong
  transfer date is put right by cancelling and re-recording the transfer (S1.25 withdrawal), or by a later "transfer date
  correction" that moves both events together under the transfer permission and approval. Also refuse `service_start_date` in an
  untargeted correction of an employment whose first event is a `transfer_in`, or hold it under the transfer kind. Add a test that
  the correction is refused.

### 2. MEDIUM: a future transfer copies an open probation at once and leaves the first one open
- `TransferApplier.ApplyAsync` runs when the transfer is recorded (or approved), not on its date. `CarryProbationAsync`
  (`TransferApplier.cs:89-111`) then adds an open probation to the linked employment, with the same dates, at once. The first
  employment's probation stays `in_progress` or `extended` (detail j: "stays as it was") and is never closed, even after the
  separation takes effect.
- Failure scenario: an employee on probation until 30 June is transferred with service kept, effective 1 June, recorded on 10 May.
  From 10 May both employments are current, each with an open probation. The daily `hrm.employment_terms_due` job reminds the
  managers and HR administrators of both companies about two probations for one person. If HR confirms the probation in company A
  before 1 June, only A's record closes, and B's copy keeps reminding and stays open. After 1 June, A's record is an open probation
  on a separated employment for ever, and any open-probation list or report counts it. With service kept, a person already confirmed
  in A arrives in B with no confirmation date and no probation, so B shows them unconfirmed. Ruling Q9 says only that the service
  start is carried.
- **Fix:** carry the probation when the separation takes effect (immediately, on approval, or in the daily job's `SeparateAsync`),
  and close A's record then, for example with `not_confirmed` and a note, or a status meaning "carried" if you rule a new status
  through a change request. With service kept, also carry `confirmation_date`, or record a ruling that it is not carried. Add a
  test for a future transfer with an open probation.

### 3. MEDIUM: the contract and probation endpoints are nearly untested for permission, data scope and field security
- The slice's Acceptance list says: "Every endpoint: … permission tests, data-scope tests (own, team, subtree, company),
  field-security tests; tests cite the FR ids." In `EmploymentTermsTests.cs`, the only permission test of the five contract and
  probation endpoints is "not confirmed" without `hrm.probation_records.update` (`:501-506`). Nothing tests a contract create or
  renewal without `hrm.contracts.create`, out of scope, or with a field hidden (`EmploymentTermsService.cs:198-211`). Nothing tests an
  extension or confirmation out of scope (`:401-417`). There is no field-security test of `extended_to`, `decision_date` or `terms`.
  For transfers, the scope test covers only "no scope in the receiving company" (`:304-310`), not a role limited in the first
  company, and not field security.
- Failure scenario: a later edit drops the `scope.AllowsAsync(contract, …, "create")` call or the field map in
  `SaveContractAsync`. Nothing fails, and a branch-limited officer can create contracts for any employee of the company.
- **Fix:** add the tests the acceptance list names, citing FR-HRM-EMP-09 and FR-HRM-EMP-10: for each terms endpoint, one without the
  permission, one out of scope, and one with a hidden field; for transfers, one with a field hidden in the receiving company.

### 4. LOW: a change waiting for approval on the first employment can be stranded by a held transfer
- `TransferOutcomeAsync` (`EmploymentEventHandler.cs:158-163`) checks again only approved or effective events dated on or after
  the transfer. While a transfer waits, the change service still accepts changes dated before it, including ones that wait for
  approval themselves.
- Failure scenario: a transfer dated today waits. HR records a promotion dated last week, which also waits. The transfer is
  approved first, and the first employment is separated. The promotion's approval then always fails with
  `errors.hrm.employment.not_current`, and its task stays in the approvers' inbox until someone rejects it.
- **Fix:** refuse the approval of a held transfer while another change of the first employment is `pending_approval` (as
  `PlanTransferAsync` does at record time, `EmploymentService.Moves.cs:179-182`), or refuse new changes needing approval while a
  transfer waits.

### 5. LOW: the transfer answer pairs the linked employment's id with the first employment's row version
- `SaveTransferAsync` returns `id = to.Id` together with `rowVersion = from.RowVersion` (`EmploymentService.Moves.cs:331`, `:347-360`).
  Every other employment answer pairs an id with that row's own version. The linked employment's version (1, or 2 after it becomes
  the main one) is not returned.
- Failure scenario: a client (S1.21) uses the answer's `id` and `rowVersion` for the next change of the new employment and gets a
  concurrency conflict, or it passes by chance when the numbers happen to match.
- **Fix:** return `rowVersion` for the linked employment and `fromRowVersion` for the first one, and update the contract row.

### 6. LOW: the fixes of S1.20b lows 7 and 11 have no test that exercises them
- The daily job's cursor (`EmploymentEventsDueJob.cs:33-56`, `:100`) reads a second page only when more than 200 approved events are
  due, and no test creates that many events or events that are not yet due in another time zone. The throw-away check above shows
  the query translates, but the paging is unproven. The `for share` lock on the manager (`EmploymentService.Changes.cs:447-450`) has
  no test either. Two opposite manager changes now end in a PostgreSQL deadlock error, which `InTransactionAsync` does not catch
  (it catches `DbUpdateException` only), so the loser gets a 500 instead of a conflict.
- **Fix:** add a job test with a company in a time zone ahead of the others and more than one page of events. Catch
  `PostgresException` with SQL state `40P01` in `InTransactionAsync` and answer `Concurrency`.

### 7. LOW: a rehire does not get the hire-only rules of corrections
- `EmploymentService.Changes.cs:194` and `:255` treat only root kind `hire` as the start of an employment. A correction of a `rehire`
  cannot move the joining date earlier (it is refused as `before_joining`). A correction can move it after today on an active
  employment, and the employment stays `active` with no job details today. A hire gets `joining_after_today` in the same case.
- **Fix:** use one helper ("starts the employment": `hire` or `rehire`) at both places and in `EmploymentEventHandler`, and check that a
  corrected rehire still joins after the former employment's separation date.

### 8. LOW: contracts are not ended when an employment ends or is cancelled
- Contracts of the first employment stay `active` after a transfer. One without an end date stays active for ever, because the job
  expires only contracts with an end date (`EmploymentTermsDueJob.cs:64`). A contract can be created on the linked employment while
  a transfer waits for approval (`CurrentEmploymentAsync` accepts `pre_joining`), and it stays active after a rejection cancels that
  employment. Contracts may also start or end after a recorded future separation.
- **Fix:** refuse a contract on a waiting transfer's linked employment and beyond a recorded end. On separation (and on cancellation
  of the linked employment), mark open contracts `terminated`, or record that S1.25 does it and add it to R-227.

### 9. LOW: a held transfer gives the approval route no unit and no receiving company
- `SaveTransferAsync` passes company, kind `transfer`, date and employee number (`EmploymentService.Moves.cs:316-326`), but not
  `unit`, which the document type declares and the change service passes (`EmploymentService.Changes.cs:497-500`). Nothing tells a
  route an inter-company transfer apart from a move within the company.
- Failure scenario: a customer's route sends transfers into unit X to that unit's head. Inter-company transfers never match it, and
  they take the default step.
- **Fix:** pass `unit` (the receiving unit) and add a `to_company` field to the document type, so routes can tell the two apart
  (this also helps R-226).

### 10. LOW: the reminder job does everything in one transaction
- `EmploymentTermsDueJob.RunAsync` (`:37-99`) reads every open probation and every active contract ending within 366 days, reads
  the settings for each row, and expires and reminds in one chunk. With tens of thousands of employees that is a long transaction
  inside the 900-second timeout, and one bad row rolls back every reminder of the day.
- **Fix:** page by id as `EmploymentEventsDueJob` does, and read the settings once per company.

### 11. LOW: the generator key `fks_replaced_in` has no unit test
- `DesignLoader.cs:1216-1236` and `SqlEmitter.cs:312-320` are covered only by the drift check of the one table that uses the key. The
  two refusals ("not the company-scoped pair", "column without a foreign key") are never run, and no test asserts that the frozen
  file still emits the pair form.
- **Fix:** add a `GeneratorTests` case with a small design in memory: the frozen file has the pair and its index, the slice file
  drops and adds the plain key with its own index, and both refusals fire.

## Checked and found sound
- **FR-XM-30, one transaction.** At once: number taken first (gap-allowed, R-224). Then, under the first employment's lock,
  re-planned and checked for the row version, the separation event, the linked employment, its `hire`/`transfer_in` event, the rows
  of both employments, the probation and the outbox events are saved, and the transaction commits only on success
  (`InTransactionAsync` rolls back everything else). A held transfer saves the same rows and starts the workflow on the same
  transaction. The carry-over choices are validated, defaulted from the receiving company's settings, and written to `carry_over`
  of both events. `changes.transfer` links both ways.
- **One current main employment per person.** At once: the linked employment is saved with `is_primary = false`. It becomes active,
  the first one is saved as `separated`, and only then does the linked one become the main employment (`EmploymentApplier.SeparateAsync`).
  On a future date the daily job does the same when the separation takes effect, whatever order the two events are processed in,
  and in either time-zone order (R-229). A rejection cancels the linked employment. A second transfer is refused while a separation
  is approved or effective. A hire, a rehire or a merge of a person with a current main employment is refused as before.
- **FR-XM-32.** The transfer is held when either company lists `transfer`. The route refuses the requester (tested), and the
  employee's user is passed as a subject. The decision runs on the deciding transaction with both employments locked, and checks
  again the state, later changes, the end (BR-02) and the open items with the current report. A problem rolls the decision back
  (`WorkflowException`). The confirmation endpoint goes through the change service, so the approval configured for confirmations
  applies. `separation`, `rehire` and `hire` cannot be recorded through `/events`, and the hire service refuses the `transfer_in` reason.
- **FR-XM-05/06.** A separation cannot be corrected, and no change may be dated on or after a recorded or waiting end. Cancelled
  events and employments keep their rows, and the linked employment's number stays used after a rejection. A renewal is a new linked
  contract; the old one only changes status. Contract numbers come from `hrm.contract_no`, taken before the transaction.
- **Data scope and field security on writes.** A transfer checks `transfer` scope on the first employment and in the receiving
  company, read scope on the first job details there, field editability of the employment fields in the receiving company and of
  `status`/`separation_date` on the first employment, and the read scope of the new employment after saving. Contracts check the
  employment's read scope, `create` scope and field map. Probation checks read and `update` scope and the field plan on the stored
  record. Rehire goes through the hire service.
- **Row versions.** Transfer requires and checks the first employment's version, both outside and under the lock. Renewal checks the
  old contract's version under the lock, and probation decisions check the record's version under the lock. Every stored event,
  employment, contract and probation row that changes is bumped (`Bump`).
- **Generator and migration.** `fks_replaced_in` makes the first migration emit the company-scoped pair as before (`AsFirstCreated`;
  no drift, frozen files unchanged). `V202611152317__hrm__design_s1_20c.sql` adds the plain key's index, drops and re-adds
  `fk_employments_previous_employment_id` as the plain key, keeps the old pair index, and adds the partial unique index "one open
  probation per employment". The catalogue test (`expected-catalog.json`) matches the migrated database.
- **Daily jobs.** `EmploymentEventsDueJob`: the cursor `(effective_date, id)` is captured before each chunk, so a retry reads the same
  page; it stops when a page is short; each event is re-read under the employment lock and moved only if it is still approved
  (S1.20b lows 6 and 11). `EmploymentTermsDueJob`: expires contracts past their end in the company's time zone, reminds once per end
  date (outbox look-up, R-225 accepted), skips non-current employments, and finds the line manager's user on the day. Its payloads
  carry ids, codes and dates only.
- **S1.20b lows carried here.** Low 9: the approval handler repeats the manager check and the backdated change check, and keeps the
  new report on the still-pending event. Low 7: the manager's employment is held `for share` (see finding 6 for the deadlock answer).
  Low 8 stays recorded as R-220.
- **Counters.** The meter and the seat count count distinct persons, so a transfer takes no second seat; a hire still takes its seat
  through `ILimitGuard` on its transaction.
- **Tenant isolation.** All eight new routes carry `HrmEndpointMetadata`, and `HrmProbe` has a target for each, with real material (a
  second company, a former employment, a contract, a probation). The suite passes. No `tenant_id`; the business date comes from the
  injected clock and `org.time_zone`; SQL is parameterized.
- **Tests.** Every new test names its FR ids (FR-XM-30, FR-XM-28, FR-XM-32, BR-01, BR-02, FR-HRM-EMP-04, -09, -10). The acceptance
  example (transfer from 1 March, ends 28 February, new number, service kept, choice recorded, each company sees only its own
  employment) and the rehire acceptance (linked, new number, the former number refused when typed again) are proven through the real
  API and database. The changed generator test keeps its intent: the contract number now comes from the service, not from a request.
- **Scope discipline.** Nothing beyond Build items 11 to 13 and the three carried lows. Separations other than transfers, contract
  termination and screens are left to S1.25 and S1.21.
