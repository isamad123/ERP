## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: a returned overtime claim or request is a dead end (Build item 9, FR-HRM-ATT-12)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/AttendanceConsequenceHandlers.cs` lines 41 to 44 and 84 to 94. The kernel treats "return" as
*not* the end of a request: `WorkflowEngine.ReturnAsync` leaves the instance open with status `returned` (`CompleteAsync` sets no `completed_at`), and
the requester then either resubmits it (`ResubmitAsync`) or withdraws it (`WithdrawAsync`, which calls the handler again with outcome `cancelled`).
`WorkflowDb.OpenInstanceAsync` counts `returned` as open, so `StartAsync` refuses a new request for the same document (`errors.workflow.request_open`).

The overtime handler instead treats `Returned` as final: a claim goes back to `calculated` with `workflow_instance_id` cleared, a pre-approval becomes
`rejected`. After that, every later outcome on the still-open instance throws `NotPending` at line 43 (the row is no longer `submitted`):
- claiming again: the service starts a new instance for the same entry id → 409 `errors.workflow.request_open`;
- withdrawing: the handler throws → refused;
- resubmitting: the task opens again, but approving and rejecting both throw → it stays in the approver's inbox for good;
- and because `workflow_instance_id` is now empty, the nightly engine may even delete the `calculated` entry (`AttendanceConsequences.cs` line 101, the
  trigger allows it), after which every outcome throws `errors.hrm.attendance.request.not_found`.

What goes wrong: overtime the approver returns for a detail can never be paid or converted (worked overtime lost; R-314 relies on claims being made),
and undecidable tasks pile up in the approvals inbox. A returned pre-approval leaves a stuck instance in the same way (a new request for the day is
possible, since the old row is `rejected`). The waiver handler (lines 142 to 145) has the softer form of the same mistake: after a return the
penalty has no instance, so a new waiver answers `request_open` instead of `waiver_pending`; withdrawing first does work. No test returns or
withdraws a claim, request or waiver.

How checked: reproduced (RA).

Suggested fix (the S1.32b pattern, review S1.32b finding 3): on `Returned` keep the row as it is (`submitted`, instance kept); accept `Approved`,
`Rejected` and `Cancelled` while the row is `submitted`; `Cancelled` (withdrawn) then sends a claim back to `calculated` and ends a pre-approval as
`rejected`; let the claim endpoint resubmit a returned instance of the same entry (`ResubmitAsync`) instead of starting a new one, or tell the caller
to withdraw it first. Do the same for the waiver (keep `workflow_instance_id` while returned). Add tests: return, then resubmit and approve; return,
then withdraw and claim again; the same for a pre-approval and a waiver.

### 2. HIGH: comp-off below one unit of the leave type is converted, then neither earned nor paid (Build item 10, FR-HRM-LVE-08, ATT-12)

`src/Modules/Hrm/Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/AttendanceConsequences.cs` lines 133 to 159 (and `AttendanceConsequenceHandlers.cs` lines 78 to 82,
`AttendanceConsequences.cs` line 123): approved overtime whose rule says `comp_off` becomes `converted_to_comp_off` and writes the event with
`days = minutes / compOffDayMinutes` to four places. The S1.32b consumer (`Leave/LeaveFeeds.cs` lines 114 to 123) rounds that down to the leave type's
unit (`day`: whole days; `half_day`: halves; only `hour` keeps the minutes) and posts nothing when the result is zero. The entry is frozen
(`converted_to_comp_off` never changes, trigger `hrm.tg_overtime_entries_rules`) and `IOvertimeInputs` leaves it out.

What goes wrong: on a day-unit comp-off type every conversion of less than `compOffDayMinutes` disappears (five hours on a Sunday with 480 minutes to a
day: 0.625 → 0), and above it the fraction is lost (1.75 → 1); on a half-day type everything below a half day is lost each time. Weekly-off and holiday
work of a few hours is common, so employees would systematically lose both the pay and the leave, with nothing on the balance and no message. The
policy check does not relate `compOffDayMinutes` to the leave type's unit, and no test uses a remainder (the test converts exactly 420 of 420).

How checked: reproduced (RB).

Suggested fix (needs a ruling on the business rule; recommendation): convert only what the comp-off leave type can earn. Work out the quantity in
the type's unit before converting (the same rounding as the consumer, in one shared rule); when it is zero, do not convert: keep the entry
`approved` so it is paid at its multiplier, and say so in the explanation; when there is a remainder, either pay the remainder minutes or (simpler)
carry it: record the minutes not yet earned on the entry and add them to the next conversion of the same employment and type in the leave year. Add a
policy check warning when `compOffDayMinutes` is not a multiple of the type's unit, and tests with 300 of 480 minutes on day and half-day types.

### 3. MEDIUM: overtime approved before the day but not worked uses up the monthly cap (Build item 9, FR-HRM-ATT-12)

`AttendanceConsequenceHandlers.cs` lines 63 to 68: the minutes "already approved in the month" are the sum of `approved_minutes` of approved, paid and
converted entries. For a pre-approval the payable minutes are the smaller of approved and worked (`OvertimeInputReader`, line 169; data design note),
so an approval of 120 minutes for a day on which no overtime was worked still counts 120 against the cap for the rest of the month.

What goes wrong: a manager pre-approves generously ("up to two hours on release nights"), the release finishes on time, and later overtime that was
really worked is refused `cap_reached` although nothing was paid. Over a month this can block most legitimate overtime of a team.

How checked: reproduced (RC).

Suggested fix: count, for each other entry, the minutes it actually uses: the payable minutes (`min(minutes, approved_minutes)`) once its day has been
processed (`attendance_day_id` set), and `approved_minutes` while its day is still ahead. Record this as a ruling under CR-089 h (it says only that
approved entries count). Add a test like RC that expects the second approval to pass.

### 4. LOW: a penalty without its leave is cancelled and applied again every time its month is processed (Build items 7 and 8)

`AttendanceConsequences.cs` lines 239 to 258: `SameEffectAsync` answers false whenever an applied leave penalty has no `leave_entry_id` (no leave type
with the code, or a closed leave year, ruling d). So each later run that writes any day of that month cancels the penalty and applies a new one that
again posts nothing. The table fills with cancelled copies, and a waiver waiting on the penalty is silently voided: the old row is cancelled, the
approval then does nothing (`PenaltyWaiverHandler` line 120), and the new row is applied without a waiver. The same churn follows retiring a leave
type (line 262 reads active types only): the next run gives back every deduction of that month.

Suggested fix: when the plan's leave type still resolves to the penalty's `leave_type_id` (or both are missing) and the units are the same, follow
the penalty instead of replacing it; retry posting the leave only when the type now exists. Keep retired types matching by id.

### 5. LOW: overtime on a rest day is accepted in a policy that ignores work on that day (Build item 9, CR-089 a)

`Erp.Hrm.Application/Attendance/AttendancePolicy.cs` (check of `overtime`): `overtime.weeklyOff` (or `holiday`) is accepted with
`weeklyOffWork: ignore` (or `holidayWork: ignore`). The day rules then record no worked minutes (`DayRules.cs` lines 347 to 357) and
`LatenessAndOvertime.OvertimeOf` gives none, so HR's overtime rule is silently never used. Ruling a says the check refuses values used only with
another choice. Suggested fix: refuse the rest-day rule with an error such as `errors.hrm.policy.attendance.overtime_rest_work_ignored` (both texts).

### 6. LOW: the two database rules allow transitions the application never makes (Build items 7 and 9)

`tools/Erp.CodeGen/rules.yaml`, function `hrm.tg_overtime_entries_rules`: from `approved` any status but the frozen ones may follow, so a direct
update can send an approved entry back to `submitted` and approve it again with other minutes, or back to `calculated`, clear its instance and delete
it. Function `hrm.tg_attendance_penalties_rules`: a penalty that deducted leave may end (`cancelled` or `waived`) without `leave_reversal_id`. The
application is correct today (and generated writes are closed); this is defence in depth. Suggested fix: allow only the transitions the services
make (`calculated → submitted → approved | rejected | calculated`, `submitted(pre) → rejected`, `approved → paid | converted_to_comp_off`), and require
`leave_reversal_id` when a penalty with `leave_entry_id` is waived (a cancellation in a closed year keeps it applied, R-313, so it can be required
there too).

### 7. LOW: races between the engine and a decision end in errors, and one can skip a conversion

- `LeavePenaltyUsage.ReverseAsync` (lines 65 to 72) checks "already reversed" before taking the employment-and-type lock. A waiver approved while the
  engine cancels the same penalty both pass the check; the second insert fails on `uq_leave_ledger_reverses_entry_id`, so the decision (or the job
  run) fails and must be repeated. Data stays right. Take the lock first, then check.
- `AttendanceConsequenceService.StartAsync` (lines 315 to 319) catches only `DbUpdateException` with a `PostgresException` inside; a claim that meets
  the engine changing the same row gets `DbUpdateConcurrencyException` (row version) and answers 500. Answer 409 (try again) instead.
- An approval of a pre-approval converting to comp-off (handler lines 78 to 82) and the engine processing its day at the same moment can each see
  the other's old state: the handler sees no processed day, the engine sees `submitted`. Nobody converts; the entry stays `approved` with worked
  minutes and is paid in cash, and the next run only converts it if the day's result changes again. Take the same per-employment lock
  (`hrm.overtime:<employment>`) in the engine's overtime step, or have the engine convert any approved comp-off entry whose day shows minutes.

### 8. LOW: the reason of a rejected waiver stays on the penalty as if it were current

`AttendanceConsequenceService.cs` lines 209 to 211 write `waiver_reason` when the waiver is asked; a rejected or withdrawn waiver leaves it on the
applied penalty, and a second waiver overwrites it. A list showing "reason" next to an applied penalty is misleading, and the first reason is then
only in the workflow history. Suggested fix: clear it when the waiver ends without approval (the delete rule already keys on `workflow_instance_id`
being set once; keep a separate "was asked" flag if that matters), or document that the field means "the last waiver asked".

## Checked and found right

  `EarlyMinutes` is zero within grace); counting in date order per calendar month; each day keeps the rule of its own policy version; a waived
  penalty still counts while its day is late. Overtime on a working day is the time after the shift's end (before its start only when the rule says),
  never more than worked, present or late days only; rest days take all recorded work; the minimum is applied; caps daily then monthly.
- Engine reconciliation: a cancelled penalty is saved before its replacement is inserted (partial unique index); locked days are never changed or
  newly penalised (FR-XM-26); a closed leave year leaves the deduction applied and the waiver is refused (R-313); penalties and entries are written in
  the processor's transaction, so a failed run leaves nothing half done; repeat runs write nothing when results are unchanged (tested).

## Response

(standing ruling CR-083). Tests named `review_n` cite the finding.

1. **Fixed (high).** `OvertimeHandler` and `PenaltyWaiverHandler` treat `returned` as not the end: nothing changes and the request stays open.
   Withdrawn or cancelled: a claim goes back to `calculated` (it can be claimed anew), a pre-approval ends `rejected`, a waiver ends with the penalty
   applied. The claim and waiver endpoints send a returned request again (`IWorkflowService.ResubmitAsync`, the S1.32b pattern); a request still
   waiting answers 409 `not_claimable` / `waiver_pending`. A returned pre-approval is withdrawn and asked again (the day's entry stays `submitted` until
   then). Tests `review_1_..._a_returned_claim_or_waiver_is_sent_again_or_withdrawn_and_claimed_anew` and `review_1_..._a_returned_waiver_is_sent_again_and_approved`.
2. **Fixed (high), ruling l.** Conversion earns only what the comp-off leave type can earn in its unit (pure rule `LatenessAndOvertime.CompOffEarnable`,
   the same rounding as the leave consumer): hours keep every minute, half days and days only whole halves or days of `compOffDayMinutes`. The earned
   minutes are recorded in the new column `hrm.overtime_entries.comp_off_minutes` (expand-only, same unmerged S1.29b design file) and sent exactly; the
   rest of the approved minutes stays payable through `IOvertimeInputs` (status `converted_to_comp_off`, payable = approved and worked minus converted).
   Less than one unit converts nothing: the entry stays `approved` and is paid. Without a single comp-off type every minute is converted and the leave
   engine waits for HR to create one, as S1.32b defines. Tests `review_2_...` (integration: day, half-day and hour types with 300 of 480 minutes; unit:
   five cases).
3. **Fixed (medium), ruling m.** The monthly cap counts, for each other entry, the payable minutes (the smaller of worked and approved) once its day is
   processed, and the approved minutes while its day is ahead. Test `review_3_...`.
4. **Fixed (low).** A leave penalty is followed (not replaced) when the plan's leave type code still names the penalty's type, retired or not, or when
   both have none; it is replaced only when the type it lacked now exists. A waiting waiver is no longer voided by the churn.
5. **Fixed (low), ruling n.** The policy check refuses a weekly-off or holiday overtime rule while `weeklyOffWork` or `holidayWork` is `ignore`
   (`errors.hrm.policy.attendance.overtime_rest_work_ignored`, both languages); the unit table case 11 now reads "no holiday rule".
6. **Fixed (low).** `tg_overtime_entries_rules` allows only calculated → submitted, submitted → approved | rejected | calculated (a claim only),
   approved → paid | converted_to_comp_off, and `comp_off_minutes` only on a converted entry within its approved minutes; `tg_attendance_penalties_rules`
   requires `leave_reversal_id` when a penalty that deducted leave ends. Test `review_6_...`.
7. **Fixed (low).** `LeavePenaltyUsage.ReverseAsync` takes the employment-and-type lock before checking; the request endpoints answer 409
   `errors.hrm.attendance.overtime.changed` on a row-version conflict; the engine's overtime step takes the same per-employment lock as an overtime

8. **Ruled (low), ruling o.** `waiver_reason` is the reason of the last waiver asked (data design column note changed); the workflow history keeps every
