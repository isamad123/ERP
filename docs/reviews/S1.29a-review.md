**Summary for the product owner.** The core of the engine is sound. A day's result is worked out by pure, well-tested rules from the published
roster, the customer's attendance policy, approved leave and duty and the counted punches, and each result carries an explanation stored as
message keys and values (English and Urdu texts exist for every key). No number is built in: a company without an attendance policy gets "no
attendance policy applies; ask HR" and nothing is stored. Results are true row versions: the database refuses any change to a result except
marking it replaced once, and refuses deletion; running the same day again with the same inputs adds no row (I checked this with the tests and
by reading the comparison). A day in a locked period is never worked out again. The four new endpoints check their permission and the data
scope, and are in the tenant-isolation suite. Only the injected clock is used. CI is green and every test passes here too.

One high point should be fixed before the merge:
- **Every employee's normal morning punch makes the engine work out the unfinished day at once and raise a "missing out punch" exception.**
  Because yesterday already has a result after the nightly run, the first punch of today is treated as a "late punch" and re-queues yesterday
  *and today*. Today is then stored as "incomplete" with an open exception and an "exception raised" event, which only closes when the person
  punches out. With 500 employees the HR exception queue would hold about 500 false exceptions every working day, and once S1.30 sends
  notifications on that event, every employee would be told each morning that their out punch is missing. I reproduced it.

Four medium points need a fix or your ruling:
- the nightly run at 02:00 works out "yesterday" while yesterday's night shift (22:00 to 06:00) is still running, so every night-shift
  worker gets the same false exception every night (reproduced in part);
- the policy's "shift window" is cut at midnight for a shift that does not cross midnight: on a 15:00 to 23:00 shift, an out punch at 00:10
  inside a 2-hour window is lost, the day shows "incomplete" and the next day gets an "outside tolerance" exception (reproduced);
- hourly leave (a 1.0 requirement, FR-HRM-LVE-04) would turn the whole day into "on leave": the reader treats the leave quantity as a part of a
  day, while the data design stores hourly leave in hours (read; S1.32b writes requests, so not yet visible);
- any approved duty request, even a two-hour official visit, makes the whole day "on duty" and ignores lateness, absence and punches; the
  specification does not say this (FR-HRM-ATT-14 says duty counts as present "per policy"), so it is a rule the slice invented and needs your
  ruling (read).

The four low findings (6 to 9) can wait or be ruled on.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. HIGH: an ordinary first punch of the day re-queues its own unfinished day and raises a false "missing out punch" (Build items 4 and 6)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/AttendanceEventStore.cs` lines 159 to 174 (`RequeueLateAsync`: when the day before *or* the day itself has a
current result, both days are re-queued) with `AttendanceProcessJob.cs` lines 224 to 227 (`AttendanceReprocessConsumer` processes up to and
including the company's today) and `AttendanceDayProcessor.cs` lines 216 to 254 (a new kind raises an exception and the event).

What goes wrong: Build item 6 says "a *late* punch ... re-queues that day only", and the method's own comment says "Days not processed yet need
nothing". But the test "does the day before have a result" is true for every employee every morning after the nightly run (absent, weekly-off
and holiday days are stored too). So the first punch of today re-queues yesterday and today; today is processed with one punch, stored as
`incomplete` with an open `missing_out` exception, and `hrm.attendance.exception_raised` is written. Every later punch of the day re-queues
again (today now has a result), adding row versions, until the out punch closes the exception. Consequences: the ATT-11 queue is full of false
exceptions during every working day; S1.30's notifications on `exception_raised` would reach every employee daily; today's half-finished results
are visible to readers and to S1.30's dashboard as if final; and each punch costs a two-day reprocess (R-160 speed).

How checked: reproduced (R1).

Suggested fix: re-queue a date only when *that date* already has a current result (the day before only when the day before has one), so an
unprocessed day waits for the nightly run; and never store a result (nor raise exceptions) for a day whose owned range has not ended yet
(`now` before `OwnedRange(...).To` in the company's zone): return `skipped` with a new reason such as `hrm.att.skip.not_finished`, also for the
endpoint and the consumer. Add a test that a normal in punch after a processed day writes no `days_requeued` event and no exception. (If you
want punches on days the nightly run skipped, for example an unplanned day with no punch at that time, to be picked up later, re-queue any date
on or before the last date the nightly run covered, rather than "a result exists".)

### 2. MEDIUM: the nightly run works out a night shift before it ends (ruling Q3, Q5)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/AttendanceProcessJob.cs` lines 160 to 162 (the nightly run covers `today - 1` only) with the schedule
`daily at 02:00` in `Access/jobs.json` (`hrm.attendance_process`) and `DayRules.OwnedRange` (a night shift owns the next day's punches until
its end plus the window).

What goes wrong: at 02:00 a 22:00 to 06:00 shift that started yesterday is still running and owns punches until 06:00 plus the window (08:00
with a 120-minute window). The run stores yesterday as `incomplete` with `missing_out` and raises the event for every night-shift worker every
night; the morning out punch then re-queues and closes it (after finding 1's fix the morning punch still re-queues yesterday, because yesterday
has a result). The same applies to any shift ending after 02:00 minus the window. Separately, a night the worker host is down is never caught up:
the next run covers only its own yesterday, and a skipped day has no result, so no late punch re-queues it.

How checked: reproduced in part (R2: the day processed with only its in punch); the 02:00 timing read from the manifest and the code.

Suggested fix: with finding 1's "not finished" rule, have the nightly run cover the last two (or a few) days, so a day is stored the first night
after its owned range has ended; unchanged days cost one comparison and write nothing. Mention the window in the job description.

### 3. MEDIUM: the shift window is cut at midnight for a shift that does not cross midnight (CR-089 S1.29a detail a, ruling Q3)

`Erp.Hrm.Application/Attendance/DayRules.cs` lines 206 to 231 (`OwnedRange` extends past midnight only when `CrossesMidnight` is true) against
lines 331 to 334 (the window `shiftEnd + shiftWindowMinutes` used to count punches) and `AttendanceDayProcessor.cs` line 116.

What goes wrong: CR-089 a defines `shiftWindowMinutes` as "how far before a shift's start and after its end a punch belongs to it". For a shift
ending at 23:00 (a common second shift of a three-shift factory: 07 to 15, 15 to 23, 23 to 07) the window reaches 01:00, but the day owns punches
only to 24:00. A worker who stays past midnight loses the out punch: the day is `incomplete` (`missing_out`), worked minutes 0, and the next
day gets an `outside_tolerance` exception. S1.29b's overtime will start from these worked minutes. The mirror case (a shift starting shortly
after midnight with punches before midnight) is the same.

How checked: reproduced (R3).

Suggested fix: in `OwnedRange`, end the day at `max(midnight, shiftEnd + window)` for every shift with a shift (not only crossing ones), and start
the next day at `max(midnight, previous shiftEnd + previous window)` likewise; keep "consecutive days never overlap". Add the 15:00 to 23:00 case
with an out punch at 00:10 to the scenario table.

### 4. MEDIUM: hourly leave would make the whole day "on leave" (contract `IApprovedLeaveDays`, CR-089 S1.29a detail d and j)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/AttendanceEngineReaders.cs` line 26 (`Math.Min(r.Quantity, 1m)`; the part comes only from the request's start
and end part) with `DayRules.cs` lines 297 to 299 (`quantity >= 1` is a whole day).

What goes wrong: leave types count `calendar_days`, `working_days` or `hours` (unit `day`, `half_day` or `hour`, S1.32a), FR-HRM-LVE-04 (1.0)
requires hourly leave, and the data design's own check on `hrm.leave_request_days` says the daily total is "≤ 1 day (or shift hours for hourly
leave)", i.e. an hourly request's `quantity` is in hours. The reader caps 2 hours at 1 and returns part `full`, so a two-hour leave makes the day
`on_leave`; 0.5 hour (quantity 0.5, part `full`) halves the full-day minutes. The contract row and the handoff to S1.32b describe the quantity as
"≤ 1" without saying how hours are converted, so S1.32b has nothing to keep true.

How checked: read (no leave request can be approved before S1.32b).

Suggested fix: decide the contract now: either the reader joins the request's leave type and converts hours to a part of the day (hours × 60 ÷
the day's full-day minutes, never above 1) with part `full`, or the contract carries `minutes` for hourly leave and `DayRules` shrinks the
full-day and absence minutes by them. Add a unit scenario for a two-hour leave; update the contracts row and the handoff note to S1.32b.

### 5. MEDIUM: any approved duty request makes the whole day "on duty" (spec silent; needs a ruling)

`Erp.Hrm.Infrastructure/Attendance/AttendanceEngineReaders.cs` lines 53 to 63 (a request overlapping any minute of a local day covers that day)
with `DayRules.cs` lines 309 to 318 (an approved duty decides the day and its punches are not counted).

What goes wrong: `hrm.duty_requests` holds a time span (`start_at`, `end_at`), and FR-HRM-ATT-14 says on-duty, official visit and work-from-home
requests "count as present per policy". Neither the specification, the slice nor CR-089 (detail d only gives the order "approved duty, then
punches") says that a part-day request covers the whole day. As built, a two-hour official visit at 15:00 makes a day `on_duty` even if the
employee came at 11:00 or not at all, which removes lateness and absence that S1.29b and S1.35 turn into deductions. A request spanning midnight
also marks both dates. CLAUDE.md asks to stop when the specification is silent.

How checked: read.

Suggested fix (needs your ruling, recorded in CR-089): either a duty decides the day only when it covers the shift (or the policy says so per kind,
which S1.30's `counts_as_present` field could carry), and otherwise its minutes count as worked time inside the shift (lateness measured after
the duty if it starts the day); or keep "any duty covers the day" as an explicit ruling.

### 6. LOW: codes appear untranslated inside the Urdu (and English) explanation

"…اس دن کو weekly_off بناتا ہے" (`attendance.ur.json`). Ruling Q7 wants both languages to render.

How checked: read (`DayRules.Render` and both text files).

Suggested fix: store a key for each such value (for example `hrm.att.value.weekly_off`) and translate it at display, or add ICU `select` forms
to the texts; extend the unit test that renders both languages to fail on a raw code.

### 7. LOW: possible deadlock between a job chunk and the re-queue consumer or the endpoint

`AttendanceEngineReaders.cs` line 181 (`AttendancePeriodLocator` takes the pay period's advisory lock every time a new processor first meets the
period, even when the attendance period already exists) with `AttendanceDayProcessor.cs` line 56 (the employment lock) and
`AttendanceProcessJob.cs` lines 116 to 176 (one transaction per chunk of 50 employments).

What goes wrong: a chunk holds the lock of employment 1 and of the period, then waits for employment 5's lock; a consumer that already holds
employment 5's lock (a late punch) then waits for the period lock. PostgreSQL ends one transaction with a deadlock error; the consumer retries,
the job attempt fails and retries from its checkpoint (three attempts). Not data loss, but failed nightly attempts are likely once device punches
arrive while the run is going.

How checked: read, not reproduced.

Suggested fix: read the attendance period first and lock only to create it, or create it with `insert … on conflict (pay_period_id) do nothing`
(the key is unique) and read it back, with no advisory lock.

### 8. LOW: a day that becomes "not processed" keeps its old result and open exceptions

`AttendanceDayProcessor.cs` lines 83 to 122: when a day is now skipped (the policy assignment removed, a backdated separation, a roster line
removed and no punch), the current row stays and its open exceptions stay open in the queue, while the explain endpoint's preview says the day
is not processed.

How checked: read.

Suggested fix: when a day with a current result is now skipped for a reason other than `locked`, close its open engine exceptions (status
`closed`) and say so in the answer; whether the stale result should be superseded is S1.31's question (record it in R-159).

### 9. LOW: with half-day leave the other half's lateness is not checked, and the job ignores the requester's data scope

- `DayRules.cs` lines 377 to 378: with `first_half` leave the lateness is 0 whatever the arrival time (the second half's start is never
  measured); with `second_half` leave early leaving is 0 likewise. Ruling d says only "its half is not checked". The midpoint of a shift is not
  defined anywhere, so this needs a value from the shift or the policy, not a guess: ask, or record the current behaviour as the ruling.
- `AttendanceProcessJob.cs` lines 97 to 148: a user holding `hrm.attendance_days.process` with a unit-limited scope can queue the job for the
  whole company or any `employment_id`; the endpoint checks the scope, the job does not. The effect is small (the same results the nightly run
  would write), but the job log reports counts outside the user's scope. Check the unit and employment against the requester's scope when the
  run is queued, or record that the permission is company-wide.

How checked: read.

## What I checked and found correct

- No default numbers or statutory values: the `attendance` schema requires every rule value and fills none; schema maximums (240, 720, 1440)
  are bounds, not values; `MostDays` 62, chunks of 50 and lists of 500 are technical limits (AS-4, R-002).
- Repeatability: `Same` compares every stored field, the policy version, the period and the explanation JSON (order of keys does not matter);
  the second job run and the resumed run write nothing (integration tests, and R1's first step).
- Row versions: the trigger refuses delete, any change of a replaced row, and any change other than `superseded_at` (plus audit fields and
  `row_version`); the partial unique key keeps one current row; the generator change for `uniques_replaced_in` emits `drop constraint` for a plain
  key and the base migration still creates it as before (no drift).
- FR-XM-26: the lock is asked for each date before any read or write, in the job, the endpoint and the consumer; a locked day is reported as
  skipped and not touched.
- Exceptions: a kind still raised moves to the new row; an open one no longer raised becomes `regularized` or `closed`; one event per new row.
- Night shifts: punches of the next morning belong to the night shift up to its end plus its own policy's window; consecutive days do not
  overlap; time zone from `org.time_zone`, the same as the event store's dates.
- Grace edges and rounding: `late > grace` (equal is within grace), whole minutes cut towards zero, rounding of worked minutes only, nearest
  rounds a half step up; covered by the unit table.
- Permissions and scope: `hrm.attendance_days.process` (HR admin baseline), reads under the generated read permissions with the data scope of
  the action; unmapped punches only with their own read permission; all four routes are in `HrmProbe`.
- Injected clock only; no hard-coded user-facing text except finding 6.
