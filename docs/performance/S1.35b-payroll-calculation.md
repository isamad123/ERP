# S1.35b payroll calculation: measured run of 10,000 employees (GHR-01, FR-HRM-PAY-34)

Target (GHR-01, architecture reference section 12): payroll for 10,000 employees in under 15 minutes; the load test at 50,000 is slice S1.52 (R-173).

## How it was measured
- Test: `tests/Erp.IntegrationTests/PayRuns/PayRunPerformanceTests.cs` run once with `ERP_PAY_PERF_EMPLOYEES=10000` (and `ERP_PAY_PERF_LANES` 1 and 4 for the
  comparison). The normal suite runs the same test at 1,000 employees (`[Trait("Suite", "Performance")]`).
- Data: one employee hired and paid through the real API on a structure of ten components (fixed basic, four percentages of the basic, two formulas, two
  fixed deductions, one one-time component), copied by SQL to 10,000 employees of one pay group. No attendance is processed (the lock accepts the open items),
  no statutory calculator is registered (none exists before S1.36 and S1.37).
- Path: the real request (`POST /api/hrm/pay-runs/{id}/calculate`: lock first, lanes queued), the real job `hrm.payroll_calculate` on the real worker
  (two slots per customer, the test rig's default), chunks of 200 (default), every result, line, employee row and trace saved through the audited EF save,
  the trace written per result. Time is from the request to the end of the last lane.
- Machine: the build session's container, 4 logical processors (Intel Xeon @ 2.10GHz), 15 GiB memory, PostgreSQL 16.15 on the same machine, .NET 10.0.15,
  Debug build. A production server with the database on its own machine should be faster, not slower.

## Results (2026-11-26)

| Lanes (`hrm.pay.max_parallel_chunks`) | Calculation of 10,000 | Per employee | Recalculation of 1,000 (fresh snapshots) | Peak working set of the test process |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 55.1 s | 5.5 ms | 14.4 s | 699 MiB |
| 2 (default) | 39.3 s | 3.9 ms | 14.2 s | 807 MiB |
| 4 (the worker runs two at a time) | 41.5 s | 4.2 ms | 14.6 s | 757 MiB |

- 10,000 employees take under one minute, about 1/20 of the 15-minute target. Two lanes are about 30 percent faster than one; four lanes on a worker with two
  slots per customer gain nothing more (the extra lanes wait for a slot).
- Stored per employee: snapshot 2.3 KiB (compressed), trace 3.8 KiB.
- Memory: the managed heap of the test process grew by 55 to 67 MiB over the run (it holds the API host, the worker and the test); the calculation keeps one
  chunk at a time, so it does not grow with the number of employees.
- The 1,000-employee test in the normal suite: 6.5 s (6.5 ms per employee) on the same machine; its budget is the linear share of the target (projected
  10,000 under 15 minutes) and at most twice the per-employee cost of a baseline in the same run, so a calculation that grows faster than the number of
  employees fails whatever the machine's speed.

## Not measured here
Real structures with the statutory calculators of S1.36 and S1.37, attendance and leave inputs for every employee, and 50,000 employees (S1.52, R-173).
