## Goal
Once payroll has calculated a period, the attendance and leave of that period cannot change underneath it. HR may also lock a period
at its cut-off date. A locked day is never rewritten: if a missing punch is regularized, leave is cancelled or overtime approved for a
locked day, the change still goes through approval (with HR in the route), is recorded as a correction of that day, and payroll is told
to carry the difference into the next open payroll. If payroll cancels the run that caused the lock before approving it, the period
opens again and everyone affected is told. Locking with unresolved exceptions needs a named person's override, which shows on the
override report.

## What exists already (and stays)
- `hrm.attendance_periods` (open or locked, pay group, pay period, `locked_by_run_id`; exclusion on pay group and dates), created by
  S1.29a, whose read side of `IAttendancePeriodLock.IsLockedAsync` already answers from the status.
- Placeholder functions `hrm.todo_attendance_days_lock()` and `hrm.todo_attendance_periods_lock()` (todo-stubs, owner S1.31).
- `hrm.retro_adjustments` (pending, applied, cancelled; amount required), owned by S1.39.
- Proposed late and early actions `hrm.attendance_rule_actions` (S1.29b), overtime entries, regularization and duty requests, leave days.

## Found while preparing (needs a ruling)
1. **Reopening.** ATT-21 speaks of reopening; the data design says a locked period never reopens except when the locking run is cancelled
   before it is locked itself (Q2).
2. **Cut-off lock versus calculation lock.** ATT-17 locks by cut-off; FR-XM-26 locks at calculation (Q1).
3. **Corrections have nowhere to live.** `attendance_days` is unique per employee and date and frozen; `retro_adjustments` needs an amount
   and a component, which only payroll can compute (Q5).
4. **BR-09's override** would let payroll run on unlocked attendance, which FR-XM-26 forbids (Q4).
5. **S1.20 Q6** says the lock "refuses changes inside a locked period"; FR-XM-26 and PAY-06 say a later change is accepted as a
   retroactive adjustment. The higher rule wins (Q7); S1.20's contract method is answered accordingly (lane B is told in the handoff).
6. **No override report exists** for FR-XM-32 (S1.10 noted none in 1.0 yet) (Q4).

## Build
### Part S1.31a: lock, reopening, guards (Opus, fresh review)
1. **Lock (ATT-17, FR-XM-26, Q1).** `IAttendancePeriodLock.LockAsync(periodId, reason)` for HR with `hrm.attendance.lock` after the pay
   period's cut-off date, and `LockForRunAsync(payGroupId, payPeriodId, runId)` called by the payroll engine (S1.35b) in the calculation
   transaction: open becomes locked with `locked_at` and (for the run) `locked_by_run_id`; already locked stays locked. Before locking,
   every day of the period for every employee of the pay group is processed (S1.29 job, synchronous for the period) and the proposed late
   and early actions are applied (S1.29 Q9: leave usage through the S1.32 service, pay inputs, warnings) in the same transaction.
   Setting `hrm.att.auto_lock_at_cutoff` (default off) lets the daily job lock at the end of the cut-off date.
2. **Open exceptions at lock (BR-09, Q4).** Setting `hrm.att.lock_open_exceptions` (`block` default, or `warn`); with `block`, locking with
   open exceptions needs permission `hrm.attendance.lock.override` and a reason; each use is a security event of kind `override`
   listed by `GET /api/aud/overrides?from=&to=` (the FR-XM-32 exception report; screen with S1.51) and the lock record keeps the counts.
3. **Database guards (FR-XM-26, FR-XM-05).** Implement the two todo stubs as rules.yaml `functions` (new migration file, never an edit of
   a merged one): when a period is locked, refuse insert, update and delete on `attendance_days`, `leave_request_days`, `overtime_entries`,
   `attendance_rule_actions` and the decision of `regularization_requests` and `duty_requests` dated in it; refuse locked to open except
   through item 4. The application path refuses first with a clear message; the trigger stops every other path.
4. **Reopening on run cancellation (ATT-21, Q2).** Only `ReopenForCancelledRunAsync(runId)`: allowed when `locked_by_run_id` is that run
   and the run was cancelled before reaching `locked` (S1.35 cancel, S1.38 later); applied actions of item 1 are reversed by reversal
   entries (ledger) and cancelled pay inputs; no other reopening exists. HR locks without a run never reopen.
5. **Events (ATT-21).** `hrm.attendance.locked` and `hrm.attendance.reopened` {company, pay group, pay period, run, employment ids in pages
   of 1,000}; catalogue entries for payroll officers; the payroll engine (internal) and notifications consume them.
6. **Readiness and screen (PAY-38).** Register the `attendance_locked` check in `pay-readiness.json` (S1.34): not locked, locked by HR,
   locked by run; HR screen "Attendance periods" per pay group: status, cut-off, open exceptions, Lock button with override dialog.
### Part S1.31b: changes after lock (Opus, fresh review)
7. **Late-change path (FR-XM-26, ATT-17, Q3).** A regularization, duty request, overtime approval or leave request, cancellation or
   modification dated in a locked period is accepted as a **late change**: its workflow context carries `in_locked_period = true`, and the
   seeded default routes add an HR step on that condition (conditional routes, S1.10; the customer may change the route but not remove
   approval: a route with no step is refused by the engine); nobody approves their own (FR-XM-32).
8. **Corrections record (Q5).** On approval the S1.29 processor computes the day again in "what-if" mode (nothing written to
   `attendance_days`) and writes an append-only row in new table `hrm.attendance_day_corrections`: employment, work date, original
   attendance day, attendance period, cause (type and id), original and corrected result, worked, late, early and overtime minutes, paid
   and unpaid leave quantities, approved overtime minutes by day type, `explanation`, status `pending` → `consumed` (one way, by S1.39) or
   `superseded` (by a later correction of the same day). Leave balances move at once through the S1.32 ledger (entries dated today,
   referencing the locked date); the locked `leave_request_days` rows are not touched.
9. **Recalculation flag (ATT-17).** Event `hrm.attendance.changed_after_lock` {employment, work date, period, correction id}; HR contract
   `IAttendanceCorrections.PendingAsync(employmentId)` and `MarkConsumedAsync(correctionIds, runId)` for S1.39; payroll officers see the
   pending count per pay group (readiness item `late_attendance_changes`).
10. **Change impact (GHR-04, Q7).** The `IEmploymentChangeImpact` of S1.29 adds "K days are in locked periods; their attendance will not be
    rewritten; a correction will be recorded if their result changes"; a backdated employment change is never refused for this reason.
11. **Reads.** `GET /api/hrm/attendance/days/{employment}/{date}` (S1.29) shows the locked result and the corrections below it;
    `GET /api/hrm/attendance/corrections?payGroup=&status=` for payroll officers (scoped).

## Acceptance
- FR-XM-26: calculation (test caller of `LockForRunAsync`) locks the period; afterwards every write path (API, processor, a direct SQL
  update as the application login) to the guarded tables for a locked date is refused; a direct update of `status` back to open is refused.
- ATT-17: HR cannot lock before the cut-off date; auto lock at cut-off works with the setting on; open exceptions block without the override
  permission; with it, the override and its reason appear in `GET /api/aud/overrides`.
- ATT-21: cancelling the locking run before approval reopens the period, reverses the applied late action and publishes
  `hrm.attendance.reopened` with the employment ids; an HR lock never reopens; a run that reached `locked` cannot reopen the period.
- Late change: a regularization for a locked date routes through HR, on approval writes one correction (present instead of absent), moves
  no locked row, raises `hrm.attendance.changed_after_lock`, and appears in `IAttendanceCorrections.PendingAsync`; cancelling approved
  leave on a locked date restores the balance by a reversal entry dated today and records an unpaid-to-paid correction.
- Rule actions proposed by S1.29 are applied exactly once at lock (lock twice: same entries).
- Tenant-isolation test for every endpoint; permission tests; FR ids in test names; fresh Opus review per part.

## Questions (each recommendation is the ruling, CR-083)
- **Q0 Parts.** *Recommendation:* two parts, Opus with a fresh review each. Alternative: one part (over the CR-047 size).
- **Q1 Cut-off and calculation.** *Recommendation:* both: HR may lock after the cut-off date (or the daily job when the setting is on), and
  calculation always locks (FR-XM-26). Alternative: calculation only (ATT-17's cut-off lock unmet).
- **Q2 Reopening.** *Recommendation:* only when the locking run is cancelled before it is locked itself, as the approved data design says;
  ATT-21's reopened event is raised in that case. Alternative: HR reopening with elevated permission (would contradict FR-XM-26 and the
  data design; would need a change to an FR-XM rule, not taken).
- **Q3 Approval of late changes.** *Recommendation:* the normal document workflow plus a conditional HR step on `in_locked_period`.
  Alternative: a separate "late change" document type (one more approval for the employee).
- **Q4 BR-09 override.** *Recommendation:* read as "lock with unresolved exceptions"; calculation never runs on unlocked attendance
  (FR-XM-26 is higher); the override is a permission plus reason, recorded on the new override report. Alternative: let payroll calculate
  on unlocked attendance (breaks FR-XM-26; not possible without changing the rule).
- **Q5 Corrections record.** *Recommendation:* new table `hrm.attendance_day_corrections` as item 8; S1.39 turns pending corrections into
  `hrm.retro_adjustments` with amounts by recalculating the original period. Alternative: write `retro_adjustments` here with amount zero
  (a placeholder amount would be stub data that pretends to work).
- **Q6 Leave after lock.** *Recommendation:* the ledger moves at once (entries dated today, `source` the late change); the locked day list
  stays; payroll reads the correction for the money. Alternative: refuse leave changes after lock (contradicts "accepted as retroactive
  adjustment").
- **Q7 Backdated employment changes.** *Recommendation:* never refused for a locked attendance period; the impact view tells the user and
  corrections are recorded; S1.20's wording "refuses changes inside a locked period" is read as "never rewrites the locked period" (lane B
  is told). Alternative: refuse (would block backdated revisions, PAY-06).
- **Q8 Lock scope.** *Recommendation:* per pay group and pay period (data design), all its employees together; an employee moved between
  groups mid-period is judged by the group valid on each date. Alternative: per employee locks (not in the design).
- **Q9 Notifications.** *Recommendation:* catalogue entries for locked, reopened and changed-after-lock to payroll officers; employees are
  told only through their request's own decision notification. Alternative: tell every employee on lock (noise).
- **Q10 Change request CR-091.** *Recommendation:* approve: table `hrm.attendance_day_corrections` (D-35, new migration file); the two lock
  functions in rules.yaml (implemented entries replacing the todo stubs, new migration file); permissions `hrm.attendance.lock`,
  `hrm.attendance.lock.override`; settings `hrm.att.auto_lock_at_cutoff`, `hrm.att.lock_open_exceptions`; events `hrm.attendance.locked`,
  `hrm.attendance.reopened`, `hrm.attendance.changed_after_lock`; contract methods `LockAsync`, `LockForRunAsync`,
  `ReopenForCancelledRunAsync` and `IAttendanceCorrections`; workflow context field `in_locked_period` and seeded conditional HR steps;
  readiness checks `attendance_locked`, `late_attendance_changes`; `GET /api/aud/overrides`. Expand-only.
